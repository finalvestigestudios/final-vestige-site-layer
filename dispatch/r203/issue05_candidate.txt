Subject: An 1843 book and a modern focus recording
Preheader: What James Braid could document, and what a seller still needs to prove.

In 1843, James Braid published Neurypnology, a book that challenged explanations associated with animal magnetism. Its nineteenth-century case reports are historical records—not modern clinical proof.

A recording can exist. Someone can find it pleasant. Its seller can promise better concentration. One claim does not prove the next.

Hypnosis Live lists Total Focus as a 33-minute, 47-MB downloadable recording attributed to J.A. Amos. We have not tested it, and broader research on hypnosis does not establish the item's advertising claims.

The product page promises a 14-day refund window. The merchant's FAQ adds a limit of five refunded sessions per year. Exact checkout and refund performance remain unchecked.

Before buying any digital audio, write down its format and length, the precise advertised outcome, what has been independently tested about this specific recording, and the complete refund terms. If you can't answer, put 'unknown' on the page.
