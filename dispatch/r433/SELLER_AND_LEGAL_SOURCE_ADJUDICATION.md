## Commercial email legal scope
The [FTC CAN-SPAM business guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) emphasizes the **primary purpose** of mixed messages. Calling an email "editorial" or putting a free Fieldbook at the beginning does NOT automatically exempt a message promoting a merchant product; treat #03/#05 as potentially commercial and apply the full sender, postal and opt-out gates. The [FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) supports prominent, understandable material-connection disclosure **beside the qualifying recommendation**. For a guide linking to a merchant: "If you buy through a qualifying link in this guide, Final Vestige may earn a commission." Use this only when a real earning relationship exists or clearly label it *prospective* during internal review; it never replaces near-link disclosure on the guide itself.

- WordPress [email appearance](https://wordpress.com/support/newsletter/email-appearance/) describes a **native non-sending Preview email** distinct from *Send test email*. Neither was used; HTML/static browser reviews are not equivalent.
- WordPress [subscribers](https://wordpress.com/support/subscribers/) explicitly excludes Not confirmed from headline subscriber totals and gives the **Email subscription=Subscribed** diagnostic filter. Distinguish recipient eligibility from confirmation-mail receipt and welcome-mail receipt.
