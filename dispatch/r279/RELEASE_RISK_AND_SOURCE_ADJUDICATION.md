## 1. Material editorial finding: the Issue 04 number is unexplained
The original subject is **“Fourteen seconds. A much longer explanation.”** The body explains the Apollo 1 investigation but **does not explain what the fourteen seconds measured**, or give a contemporaneous NASA reference for the figure. A curious reader could reasonably interpret it as the duration of the fire or time to death; that is **not** what the official preliminary timeline establishes.

A February 3, 1967 NASA statement says that at 6:31:17, **14 seconds after the first indication of the fire, cabin pressure was roughly 29 psi and the cabin ruptured**. It expressly warns that its preliminary, differently sourced timing data may have **1–2 seconds synchronization error**. A NASA NSSDC retrospective says last crew communication ended 17 seconds after first indication; another NASA retrospective reports radio transmissions ending 18 seconds after the first fire report. Do not collapse these distinct endpoints into “fire lasted 14 seconds,” “they died in 14 seconds,” or “NASA fixed the source of the spark.” The original cause was never conclusively pinpointed.

**Recommended founder review of Issue 04, NOT an adopted edit:**
- Safer subject candidate: **“Apollo 1: the cause the investigators couldn't prove”**.
- Preheader candidate: **“The hatch, the oxygen-rich cabin and the evidence that survived the fire.”**
- If keeping the 14-second subject, add a visible body sentence explicitly attributing it to *cabin pressure rupture after first indication*, explain clock uncertainty, and cite NASA directly. This is source accuracy, not optimized clickbait.
