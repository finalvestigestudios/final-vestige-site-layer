Seven days, one honest record

You already have a Fieldbook. Here is a better way to use it.

Seven days is enough time to collect observations. It is not enough time to prove that a particular technique has transformed you.

When people describe a new routine, the strongest-sounding sentence is often the least useful: “It worked.” Worked at what? Starting on time? Staying with a task? Feeling calmer afterward? Finishing something that had been sitting unfinished?

At Final Vestige, an account becomes more valuable when we can separate the event from its interpretation. A witness says a light moved. A record shows when and where. The explanation comes later. Your own attention deserves the same courtesy.

Try this before choosing another tool. Pick one ordinary task you would like to approach more deliberately. For the next three sessions, write down four things:

  1. The plan: What did you intend to work on, and for how many minutes?
  2. The conditions: What interruptions, noise or competing demands were present?
  3. The observation: Did you start? How long did you actually continue? What happened?
  4. The uncertainty: What else might explain a better—or worse—session?

Do not force an improvement. A session you abandoned is still information, especially if the obstacle was outside your control. The point is to make a useful next decision, not to manufacture a success story.
