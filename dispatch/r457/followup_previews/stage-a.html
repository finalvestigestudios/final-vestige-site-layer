The notebook is more useful than the promise

A five-minute way to separate what happened from what you expected.

Someone places a diary next to an old photograph. The photograph shows a moment. The diary tells us what its owner believed it meant. An investigator needs both, but shouldn't mistake one for the other.

Try that distinction on a routine you want to build. At the end of a day, write three lines: I intended to..., I actually did..., What interrupted me.... Do this even on days when the experiment fails. A record of missed days tells you about feasibility; it doesn't prove any method succeeded or failed in a clinical sense.

This is the principle behind the Seven Day Focus Fieldbook. It asks for observations before conclusions, makes space for a modest starting routine and keeps a thirty-day log if you want to continue. The 52-page PDF is free. You never need a paid audio program or to subscribe again to use it.
