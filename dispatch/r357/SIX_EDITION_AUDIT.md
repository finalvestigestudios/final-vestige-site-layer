## Discovered historical precision hazard, Issue 04
The original subject "Fourteen seconds. A much longer explanation." can be misunderstood as an exact ignition-to-death interval. NASA's February 3, 1967 preliminary statement identifies about **14 seconds from first indication of the fire to cabin rupture**, with possible 1–2-second clock differences (https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/Apollo204/seamans.html). The later Apollo 204 board's estimate is **about 15 seconds after the first verbal report to rupture** (https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/Apollo204/invest.html). NASA NSSDC gives a separate 17-second interval to last crew communication (https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/apollo1info.html). Different definitions must never be collapsed. The candidate subject omits the ambiguous precision; body explains the difference and notes no verified initial spark.
