## What "fourteen seconds" fails to define
Canonical #175 original subject: **"Fourteen seconds. A much longer explanation."** The body describes the Apollo 1 fire, unknown initiating source, oxygen-rich cabin and emergency-exit constraints but never states the starting/ending events whose interval is fourteen seconds. An implied stopwatch is not reliable editorial evidence.

NASA's Apollo 204 inquiry and chronology locate the first **crew report** of fire at about 6:31:04 EST, describe flames spreading intensely by about 6:31:12, and estimate cabin pressure-vessel rupture at about 6:31:19. The latter interval is roughly 15 seconds from the reported fire, depending on which records/timestamps are being compared; it is **not a measured time from the first ignition**, which was not conclusively identified. Even if a separate record gives a 14-second interval, the sentence must name exact endpoints and the original underlying source. For an accessible email subject, avoid the number entirely until that proof is adjudicated.

NASA's retrospective safety account establishes that this was a **ground test** on January 27, 1967, involving Virgil "Gus" Grissom, Edward White and Roger Chaffee. Investigators found an oxygen-rich cabin and abundant flammable materials; the inward-opening hatch could not be opened against internal pressure. A final exact ignition source was not determined. The danger arose from interacting engineering/test-system conditions, not an evidentially proven single spark. Do not imply that no wiring evidence existed; investigators examined evidence consistent with arcing in wiring near the environmental-control equipment but could not establish a unique ignition event.
