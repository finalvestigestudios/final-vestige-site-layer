**Subject:** The tree that was never magnetized
**Proposed preheader (Jetpack delivery behavior unverified):** Franklin's 1784 investigation asked a harder question than whether a witness felt something.
**Allocation:** Editorial 02 of six; preserves founder's 4 editorial / 2 commercial distribution.

At Franklin's house in Passy in 1784, an apricot tree was prepared for an experiment. The physician Charles Deslon, who practiced animal magnetism, had performed his operation on that tree. A boy of about twelve, whom Deslon considered particularly responsive, waited elsewhere. Then the investigators covered the boy's eyes.

He was not taken to the prepared apricot tree. Instead, the commissioners led him to four other trees, one after another. According to their report, he described increasing discomfort and, at the fourth tree, collapsed. That fourth tree had not been magnetized.

This wasn't a demonstration that the boy was pretending. The report describes what the observers said happened; the commissioners interpreted their experiment as evidence against the proposed magnetic fluid, while attributing the reactions to expectation and imagination. Their interpretation is a historical conclusion, not a modern clinical diagnosis of the child.

The detail worth noticing is the comparison they built: a particular claimed cause was supposed to act on a particular prepared tree. The reaction came at an unprepared one. A sensation could be reported without proving the mechanism used to explain it.

The document also records other blindfolded trials and the commissioners' attempts to separate awareness of a treatment from the supposed treatment itself. But these are eighteenth-century observations with their own limits, not a contemporary randomized trial.

Try that method on the next persuasive claim you encounter: write down (1) what a source actually observed, (2) the explanation the source proposed and (3) what comparison would distinguish that explanation from an alternative. Even a convincing first-person account cannot answer all three.
