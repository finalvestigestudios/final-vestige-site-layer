SUBJECT: Twelve minutes is a duration, not a result
PROPOSED PREHEADER (UNVERIFIED IN JETPACK): How to judge an audio meditation claim before you pay for it.

Twelve minutes can fit into a crowded day. An hour often cannot.

That is the attractive part of a short audio routine. The uncomfortable question arrives when a seller suggests those twelve minutes deliver the equivalent of an hour of advanced meditation. What, exactly, is being compared?

Zen12, an Inspire3 meditation-audio product, makes that comparison on its sales page. The seller describes twelve progressive levels and four listening styles per level. A convenient format is not proof of a particular result.

A 2023 systematic review of fourteen binaural-beat studies found five consistent with an entrainment hypothesis, eight inconsistent and one mixed. A separate meta-analysis found potentially promising effects for attention and memory but conflicting findings across tasks. Neither tested this branded product.

Try three quiet twelve-minute sessions without buying anything. Record: Did you complete each session? What interrupted you? Would you voluntarily repeat the routine next week? These notes can show whether the habit fits your life; they cannot prove cognitive or clinical benefits.

A paid recording could provide structure or an appealing listening style. The product's scientific claims are a separate question.
