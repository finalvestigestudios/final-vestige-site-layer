Subject: The drawing stayed still. The animal changed.
Proposed preheader (native WordPress mapping NOT verified): An 1892 cartoon, an 1899 psychologist, and a small test of how attention shapes what we see.

Two animals. One drawing. Neither one moves.

A duck-rabbit image may be read as two animals from the same marks. The figure appeared in print in 1892 and was discussed in Joseph Jastrow's 1899 essay “The Mind's Eye.” Jastrow used an existing image; he did not invent it.

What an observer says they saw can include an interpretation layered on a real physical trace. A photograph does not establish every story told about it; a witness report does not independently prove every explanation.

Try this: view a responsibly sourced reversible figure without its caption. Record your first interpretation, look for an alternative, then record what changed. If the second interpretation never arrives, note that too. This does not prove any blanket claim about memory or the efficacy of a commercial product.
