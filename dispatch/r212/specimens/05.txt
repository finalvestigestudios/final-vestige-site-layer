Subject: Before buying a hypnosis recording, read the terms

Preheader: Total Focus is a specific product. Its advertised benefits are not verified outcomes.

Content type: commercial-review-pending

---

The first question about a self-hypnosis recording is not whether its advertising sounds persuasive. It's what is actually delivered.

Hypnosis Live's official Total Focus page describes a 33-minute, 47-MB audio recording. Those are concrete product characteristics that a buyer can compare with alternatives. The same page promises striking changes in concentration and describes its content as reprogramming the brain. Those are sales claims, not independently demonstrated outcomes for this recording.

The historical records in our Puységur archive offer a useful warning. An account of an experience and an explanation for why it occurred are different kinds of evidence. A modern recording deserves exactly the same separation.

Before considering any guided audio, write down what you expect to receive, what the seller says it will do, and what evidence would convince you it did so. Consider whether a free, unbranded practice would meet your needs just as well. You do not have to buy anything to make a useful observation about your routine.

Read the return terms with the same attention. The Total Focus page advertises a fourteen-day guarantee, while Hypnosis Live's FAQ says refunds are limited to five sessions per year. Current checkout price, eligibility and actual refund administration still require verification.
