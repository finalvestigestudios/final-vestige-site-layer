THE DISPATCH · ISSUE 02

On a surviving chart dated 1513, the Ottoman admiral Piri Reis depicted part of the Atlantic world using information available to mapmakers of his time. It is a remarkable artifact even before the extraordinary theories begin.

The trouble comes when a fragmentary map becomes a blank canvas for every coastline its audience wants to find. One popular interpretation turns the surviving shapes into evidence of knowledge that the original chart never clearly establishes.

We approached the map like a physical document: what survives; what the mapmaker wrote; what later researchers could check; and which comparisons are interpretations rather than direct evidence.

Notice how the most persuasive stories sometimes depend on a missing section of a source. The absence of the missing piece can invite an explanation that no surviving evidence can resolve.

You don't need to accept a sensational interpretation to feel the pull of the map. The real historical record is worth understanding on its own terms.
