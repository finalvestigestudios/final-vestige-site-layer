THE DISPATCH · ISSUE 04

A complicated historical event often gets reduced to one simple cause. Apollo 1 resists that treatment.

On January 27, 1967, three astronauts died during a ground test. The Apollo 204 Review Board found an oxygen-rich cabin environment, combustible material, vulnerable electrical systems and escape constraints. An electrical arc was considered the most probable initiator, but a single exact ignition source was never conclusively identified.

The surviving account also records markers across approximately fifteen seconds after the initial verbal report. Our interactive chronology includes those markers and does not invent measurements between them.

That matters because uncertainty isn't a failure of investigation. Sometimes the best possible result is an accurate boundary around what remains unknown.
