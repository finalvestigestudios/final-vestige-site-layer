THE DISPATCH · ISSUE 06

Over the last five letters we met a crystal skull, a map, a Roman cup, a tragic spacecraft investigation and a deck of cards with a long symbolic afterlife.

Each carried a temptation: start with the compelling story, then make the evidence fit. An investigator has to reverse the order.

Here's a five-step case-file exercise you can keep: list the surviving objects or documents; date each source; separate firsthand evidence from later retellings; identify one competing explanation; and write down the conclusion you cannot yet justify.

Try it in our Apollo 1 Evidence Atlas. Change the timeline position, read a documented finding and then check what Final Vestige labels as interpretation. You may discover that the unanswered part of a file is more interesting than a tidy conclusion.

There's another way we're exploring this idea: an optional fictional detective case you can solve at home, with statements, conflicting clues and an actual answer.
