THE DISPATCH · ISSUE 01

An object can look ancient without being ancient. The British Museum's crystal skull is a case in point: its dramatic appearance helped generate extraordinary stories, while the museum identifies the object as probably European and nineteenth century.

That doesn't make the skull uninteresting. It changes the question. Instead of asking what lost civilization made it, we can ask when the object entered the record, how it was worked, and how the story of its supposed origins grew.

Try this whenever you encounter a remarkable artifact: separate three columns in your mind. What is physically present? What is documented about its history? What has someone claimed it means?

The gap between those columns is often where the most interesting mystery lives.

We examined the crystal skulls and the provenance behind their reputations in a sourced Final Vestige investigation. The evidence doesn't need an invented ancient civilization to be compelling.
