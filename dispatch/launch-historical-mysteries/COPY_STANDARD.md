# Final Vestige Studios — Conversion Copy Standard v1

**Source:** Founder-supplied screenshots and direct-response copywriting checklist (November 2026).  
**Use:** The Dispatch, affiliate presell pages, Collection product guides, relevant investigation-to-offer bridges.  
**State:** Working editorial standard — draft only. No partner approval or send authorization.

## Positioning

**Audience:** people drawn to historical mysteries, enigmatic objects, archival research, ancient symbols, tarot's material history, secret societies, documentary investigations, and tactile clue-solving.

**Desired progression:** *“I want to know more” → “I trust the method” → “I can try this” → “I know whether this is right for me” → purchase or an equally satisfying free next step.*

**Promise:** Final Vestige helps readers follow an intriguing trail with sourced context, clear limitations and meaningful ways to participate. It does not promise supernatural results, secret exclusive history, guaranteed discoveries, medical outcomes or financial gain.

## Required landing page architecture

1. **Reader + outcome in one line:** “For historical-mystery readers who want to work a case instead of just reading one.”
2. **Headline:** concrete, benefit-oriented and specific enough that an unrelated company could not use it.
3. **Subhead:** what the buyer can actually do, how the format works, and what it *does not* promise.
4. **Primary CTA:** accurately describes the immediate next page. If it leads to FV editorial review, label as review, not “Start now.”
5. **Problem before product:** one small recognisable tension from the visitor's experience—not fake scarcity or guilt.
6. **Benefit translation:** feature → what the reader gets to do/understand/keep; show a true product screenshot or source where useful.
7. **Credibility:** clearly attributed original source, real screenshots, verified third-party testimonials only with permission and proper context; never invent reviews, star ratings, personal use or test results.
8. **Fit and objection panel:** who it's for, who should skip it, platform, access format, region, delivery/shipping, actual current price, and validated returns. Unknown facts must be marked “verify before publication,” not left as sales copy.
9. **Commercial disclosure:** plain English beside a qualifying affiliate link and in the email itself as appropriate; never hide commission relationships in a distant footer.
10. **Closing CTA:** one precise next action, with free first-party alternative for people who don't want to buy.

## Email warm-up, without false “open loops”

- Email 1–3: deliver genuine findings and invite a *free* investigation. Tease a **real upcoming** next topic in one sentence.
- Email 4: transition from “read our evidence” to “try our Evidence Atlas.”
- Email 5: offer a specific hands-on evidence challenge; mention that comparable paid experiences exist but do not manufacture a mystery or scarcity.
- Email 6: teach a repeatable five-step case-file method. The reader should gain something even if no product is ever approved.
- Conditional Email 7: reveal **one** verified product *after acceptance and actual review*; explain the choice and honest limitations. Include disclosure.
- Conditional Email 8: answer real purchasing questions and alternatives; do not use fake countdowns, false testimonials or repeated hard selling.

## Conversion scoreboard

Primary business measure: **merchant-confirmed net sales and net commission per 1,000 qualified first-party visitors**; include related first-party confirmed email subscribers and verified post-email CTA visits. Also monitor drop-off, refunds, unsubscribes and complaints. Don't infer sales from clicks, views, email opens or tiny-sample percentages.

Per asset QA questions:
- Can the reader identify the offer and the next action in five seconds?
- Is the first sentence specific to that visitor and traffic source?
- Is the main benefit a believable outcome rather than a feature label?
- Is each material claim documented and current?
- Does an authentic image or demonstration carry explanatory weight?
- Is the cost/format/limit/risk explained before the outgoing purchase click?
- Are the disclosure and true destination visible together?
- Does one CTA dominate?
- Does the asset make sense even if the reader never buys?
- Would the headline feel native to Final Vestige, not a generic “make money fast” page?

**Release gates:** partner acceptance, merchant email permission, genuine product evaluation or clearly identified research-only comparison, factual/price/return verification, legal/footer controls and founder-specific release approval. No unapproved seller destination links.
