Subject: The blank days in your focus log
Preheader: An unfinished week can tell you more than a perfect one.

An empty line in an archive is frustrating. You know something happened, but the record stopped before anyone wrote it down.

A blank square in your own focus log is different. You can go back and ask what happened.

Maybe the planned twelve minutes ran into an urgent call. Maybe you sat down but kept checking the clock. Maybe you felt unusually calm, then discovered that the next afternoon was just as scattered as usual.

Those are different outcomes. Treating all three as “I failed” throws away the useful information.

For the next two days, try a small experiment. Set aside twelve quiet minutes, with no purchased audio. When you finish—or don't—note three things:
1. Did you begin? Write yes, no, or partly.
2. What interrupted you, if anything?
3. Would a little more structure have made it easier to return?

Don't turn two observations into a diagnosis, and don't expect a two-day experiment to prove a technique works. The point is to learn whether a simple routine is workable for you. Your Fieldbook already has space to track that.

Some readers ask whether guided audio would help them stick with a practice. Zen12 is one option we're examining. Its seller describes a library of twelve progressive levels and 48 Premium recordings. The seller also suggests its short sessions can deliver unusually large effects. Those are advertising claims, not effects we've independently established for Zen12. Broad research on meditation cannot prove a specific commercial recording delivers its promised result.

Before spending money, we'd rather you read a buyer's evaluation that explains what's included, what remains uncertain, and the terms we'd check.
