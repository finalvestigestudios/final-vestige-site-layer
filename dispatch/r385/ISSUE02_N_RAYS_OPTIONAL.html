A 1904 experiment asks what happens when expectation becomes evidence.

FINAL VESTIGE STUDIOS — THE DISPATCH

The prism was gone. They still saw the rays.

In a darkened French laboratory in 1904, researchers described a faint N-ray spectrum, even after the crucial prism had been removed.

Rene Blondlot announced N-rays in 1903. Robert W. Wood reported his visit in Nature on September 29, 1904. He described how experimenters continued seeing a spectrum after the supposed cause was absent. Other laboratories had difficulty reproducing the rays.

That does not prove everyone lied. Expectations can influence subjective judgments, especially when a signal is faint.

Try this: choose one surprising claim. Separate the observation, the explanation and a control that could distinguish competing causes.
