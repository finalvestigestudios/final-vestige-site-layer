In 1784, investigators in France asked a surprisingly modern question: if people reported feeling something during an unusual treatment, how could anyone tell what caused it?

The commission examining *animal magnetism* included Benjamin Franklin and Antoine Lavoisier. It compared what subjects believed was happening with whether the alleged magnetizing procedure was actually performed. Its report challenged the claimed magnetic fluid as the explanation for those experiences.

A person could sincerely report a sensation without having established the proposed mechanism. That was the insight—not that everyone's experience was false, and certainly not that every modern hypnosis recording is ineffective.

That distinction matters before you buy anything advertised as a way to sharpen attention or change habits.

**Four questions worth asking before paying:**

1. **What are you receiving?** A one-time MP3, a series of recordings, ongoing access, or a subscription? Which track and how long is it?
2. **Which outcome is being promised?** A guided listening experience is a product feature. Instant, permanent concentration is an effectiveness claim that needs independent support.
3. **What's the comparison?** Could a free quiet practice or written routine help you evaluate the format before a purchase?
4. **What if it isn't useful?** Look at the exact seller's current refund conditions, format and final checkout—not a screenshot of an old promotion.
