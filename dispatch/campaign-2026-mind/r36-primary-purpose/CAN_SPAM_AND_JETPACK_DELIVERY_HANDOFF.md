## New substantive finding — "four editorial" is NOT the CAN-SPAM classification

The founder's **4 editorial / 2 conditional commercial recommendation** split is an editorial plan, *not* a legal determination. The FTC says coverage turns on **the primary purpose a reasonable recipient would perceive** (including subject, placement, volume and prominence of promotional content), and explicitly discusses messages promoting products or services on commercial websites. Some genuinely editorial messages may not be commercial, but we must not mechanically exempt Issues **01, 02, 04 and 06** from postal-address/opt-out review because they lack a product recommendation. **Safest pilot acceptance policy: require a valid Final Vestige postal address, clear functioning unsubscribe and actual received-email evidence for all six.** This is a conservative internal requirement, not a claim that every editorial newsletter is legally commercial.

Authoritative source, reviewed October 31: [FTC CAN-SPAM Act: A Compliance Guide for Business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), especially primary-purpose mixed-content Q&A and requirements for header, advertising identification, physical postal address, opt-out, and responsibility of marketers and senders. The sender's valid address may, where legally eligible, be a street address, registered USPS PO box, or qualifying registered commercial mailbox; **never replace this with Automattic's address, a vendor's address, or an invented address**. Keep exact address private in evidence, but verify its presence in delivered output. Opt-out must actually work; the mere presence of a link or a token is insufficient. A message with a subscription relationship is not automatically transactional. External legal review remains appropriate.

2. **Merchant:** receive documentary product/SKU-specific partner approval, permissible **email** channel, creative use rights, links, terms and guide approval for #03/#05. Mind Studio owns published independent guide destinations; Dispatch owns email copy, matching disclosures and route QA.
3. **Draft-native preview, not a send:** in an authorized WordPress unpublished post editor, inspect the per-post "Send this post" option, proposed excerpt/full override, expected actual email subject/preview and all content. **Do not publish, schedule, toggle global settings, or click Send test email.** If a preview requires a per-post switch, only proceed within a separately approved supervised editor session; switch state must be rechecked before any later publication.
4. **Founder-authorized self-addressed received test, separately approved:** only after explicit founder approval for exact test recipient and content, use Jetpack's own test send once. Preserve its raw message privately (no public recipient, URL tokens, street address or authentication headers). Run the R33 MIME evidence validator, compare visual Gmail/mobile/plaintext, inspect source, ensure full content vs excerpt, inspect attribution/CTA and disclosure, and verify unsubscribe endpoint behavior on an approved non-subscriber-harming test identity.
5. **Audience audit:** reconcile total/confirmed/unconfirmed semantics using private WordPress subscriber diagnostics, without export/publication or contacting them. Verify consent and suppression flags, duplicates and who would actually receive a post; small list means no statistically meaningful A/B significance claims.
6. **Founder exact approval:** obtain final approval identifying each issue, revision hash, real recipient segment, dates/cadence, full/excerpt setting, commercial permissions and received-mail evidence. The current approval is only for drafting. Never infer live-send permission from a green test or the WordPress `site_can_send` flag.
