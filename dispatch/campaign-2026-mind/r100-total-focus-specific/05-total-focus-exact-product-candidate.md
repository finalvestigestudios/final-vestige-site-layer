A 33-minute recording is measurable. Its promise to change your concentration is another matter.

That distinction is the heart of our historical work, too. In Puységur's eighteenth-century files, the fascinating question isn't simply what a witness said happened. It's which parts of the account can be checked independently—and which interpretations traveled further than the evidence.

A modern sales page deserves the same treatment.

Hypnosis Live lists a specific recording called **Total Focus**. The seller identifies it as an **MP3**, **33 minutes** long and **47 MB**, recorded by **J.A. Amos**. Those are descriptions of what is offered, not measurements of what it accomplishes. The page also promises dramatic changes in concentration and productivity. We have **not found independently verified, product-specific evidence** sufficient to repeat those results as our conclusions.

Before spending money on this recording—or any focus audio—I'd make five checks.

**1. Name the actual product.** Total Focus is an individual audio session, not the entire Hypnosis Live collection. General catalog language about what “most sessions” contain is not a substitute for this recording's specifications. Check format, language, duration and access on the exact seller page.

**2. Separate experience from outcome.** A calm guided listening session might appeal to you. That preference isn't proof that the audio will reshape attention, accelerate work or deliver the seller's strongest promises. Ask what trial would demonstrate a *specific result for this recording*, not just whether hypnosis has been studied generally.

**3. Read the limits, not the headline.** The seller's FAQ and terms describe a **14-day refund window** with a **maximum of five refunded sessions per customer in a twelve-month period**. That is a seller-published policy, not independently verified fulfillment. Checkout terms can change. We won't put a guaranteed refund or an enduring price in an email.

**4. Test the smaller assumption first.** For three ordinary work sessions, record: the task you intended to start, whether you started, minutes before your first interruption, and one change to the setting. This modest comparison won't prove a neurological effect. It can tell you whether your current routine is workable *before* you buy an intervention.

**5. Check who receives your information.** Inspire3's current privacy policy says it **may share a referred buyer's name, email address and purchased-product details with the referring affiliate**. We will not activate an earning link unless a written arrangement permits Final Vestige to receive aggregate-only reporting without identifying purchaser records.
