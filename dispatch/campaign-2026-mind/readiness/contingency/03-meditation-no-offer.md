**Subject:** Can twelve minutes be enough to begin?

**Preheader:** A small experiment is more useful than a large promise.

The timer is often the hardest part. Not the ticking, exactly, but what it means: twelve minutes in which you cannot answer another message, rearrange another plan, or convince yourself you will start tomorrow.

That's a reason to make the first session short. It's not a reason to claim that twelve minutes has the same effect as an hour, or that one person's experience predicts another's.

Here's a simple experiment that costs nothing. For the next seven days, choose a brief period when you're unlikely to be interrupted. Sit comfortably and notice your breathing or another neutral point of attention. When you notice you've wandered, gently return. Afterwards, write down whether you started, what interrupted you, and whether you want to try again. You're not trying to manufacture a particular mental state or report a dramatic breakthrough.

The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health reviews evidence that meditation and mindfulness practices have been studied for several outcomes, but it also cautions that the quality of the research, methods and individual effects vary. A personal notebook is useful for deciding whether a practice fits your life; it isn't a substitute for controlled research or treatment.
