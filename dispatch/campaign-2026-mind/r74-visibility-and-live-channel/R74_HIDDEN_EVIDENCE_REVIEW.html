The hidden evidence trap.

An email can contain the words “Unsubscribe” or “Final Vestige may receive a commission” in its HTML source—and still hide them from readers. R74 prevents those invisible strings from being treated as proof of compliance.

HOLD_UNSENT · SYNTHETIC REVIEW ONLY · NO MESSAGE SENT

Three false-positive examples

01 / commercial disclosure

Disclosure in a hidden span

<span hidden>May receive a commission</span>

Prior logic could count this hidden content as disclosure in the first 55 visible words.

Now: not counted as a visible disclosure.

02 / postal identity

Invisible publisher address

<p style="display:none">Publisher address</p>

Source bytes can contain an address while the HTML presentation leaves it inaccessible.

Now: cannot satisfy the visible HTML postal-reference check.

03 / unsubscribe

Hidden opt-out anchor

<a hidden href="[PRIVATE TOKEN]">Unsubscribe</a>

A real-looking HTTPS link is not evidence that a reader can see or use it.

Now: invisible opt-out anchors are excluded.

What evidence still matters?

Offline source inspection

Conservative parser checks both MIME renditions, flags hidden nodes and CSS ambiguity, and never echoes email addresses, tokens, full copy or secret links.

Actual authorized delivery

Requires approved Jetpack native preview, separately authorized test delivery, private account-level recipient status, qualified postal ownership and functional unsubscribe. Not performed here.

Release boundaries

  • Issue 03 Zen12 and Issue 05 Total Focus require named-product and EMAIL-channel merchant permissions, approved live buyer guides and near-offer disclosure.
  • Commercial vendor claims about hour-equivalent meditation, brain rewriting, or guaranteed concentration are not proven by a merchant's sales copy.
  • Four editorial and two commercial slots stay intact; no manuscript has been silently replaced.
  • WordPress is currently configured for excerpt emails; no publisher address or received unsubscribe has been verified.
