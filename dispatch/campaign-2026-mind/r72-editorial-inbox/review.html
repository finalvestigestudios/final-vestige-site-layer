Edition 01 · editorial

The mind is not a camera

Proposed preheader (delivery unverified): A door crossed the path of a conversation. What changed was not always seen.

Custom excerpt candidate:

A researcher asked a stranger for directions. Workers carried a door between them, and the person asking changed. In a 1998 field study, some people did not notice. That is not a universal verdict on eyewitnesses. It is an invitation to check what was visible, what a witness actually reported, and what later readers inferred. Our Missing Caption investigation makes the same distinction with photographs: a confident label is not the same thing as a reliable record. Compare the image and its caption before you decide what the evidence proves.

Edition 02 · editorial

Before hypnosis had a name

Proposed preheader (delivery unverified): One encounter in 1784. Two accounts of its duration. A larger question about proof.

Custom excerpt candidate:

On May 4, 1784, Puységur reported visiting a sick young man; his account appeared in a letter dated May 8. Later descriptions of the encounter do not agree neatly about its duration. More importantly, the surviving text records what its author believed happened—not independent proof that animal magnetism healed anyone. Read the source as a historian would: separate the reported behavior, the explanation attached to it, and the evidence that would test that explanation. Our Puységur archive preserves the unsettling question without pretending the mechanism was established.

Edition 04 · editorial

Seventeen seconds. A much longer explanation.

Proposed preheader (delivery unverified): Apollo 1's recorded radio interval is not a stopwatch for the fire.

Custom excerpt candidate:

The Apollo 1 radio record covers about 17.1 seconds between the crew's first fire report and final transmission. Of that span, 10.3 seconds contain transmissions and 6.8 seconds are silent. Those numbers do not measure the fire's entire duration, nor do they identify the exact ignition source. NASA's investigators documented the hazardous cabin atmosphere, material choices, and hatch design while acknowledging what the evidence could not settle. The larger explanation is a chain of conditions, not a single dramatic sound bite. Read the archive with the distinction in mind.

Edition 06 · editorial

What did seven days of attention reveal?

Proposed preheader (delivery unverified): A useful notebook records gaps as well as successes.

Custom excerpt candidate:

A notebook can preserve observations—or manufacture a tidy story after the fact. In a 2002 diary-compliance study, researchers compared participants' stated recordkeeping with electronic timestamps. The results belong to that study's context, not to every person's memory. Our seven-day practice asks for something humbler: write when you began a task, what interrupted you, and what you actually completed. If you miss a day, mark the gap rather than filling it with a plausible memory. The free Focus Fieldbook supplies a way to keep that honest record without promising transformation.
