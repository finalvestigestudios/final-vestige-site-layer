Issue 1: What went missing between two pictures?

Proposed preheader: How to examine an attention failure without exaggerating the science.

UNSENT OPTIONAL COPY — Original Issue 1; does not replace PR #175
SUBJECT: What went missing between two pictures?
PREHEADER CANDIDATE (not proven Jetpack control): How to examine an attention failure without exaggerating the science.

Our minds fill in more than we realize. Change-blindness experiments illustrate one reason why: a substantial change can go unnoticed when the comparison is interrupted. That is a finding about attention under particular conditions, not a claim that every eyewitness is unreliable.

Try this without an app, product, or account. Describe a familiar room from memory, then walk into the room and list three details you omitted. Did you miss an object, its location, or its color? Those gaps say something about what you focused on. They do not diagnose a condition or measure intelligence.

Our published investigation examines two classic experimental setups: a visual interruption in a flicker task and a person swapped during a real-world interaction. The historical context matters. A memorable demonstration is not a replacement for the underlying studies and their limits.

For a fuller look, we've traced the original research and explained what it can and cannot establish. The goal is to become a more careful observer, not to distrust every sensation you have.

Issue 2: A sincere witness can still mistake a cause

Proposed preheader: The surviving Puységur records and the discipline of separating fact from explanation.

UNSENT OPTIONAL COPY — Original Issue 2; does not replace PR #175
SUBJECT: A sincere witness can still mistake a cause
PREHEADER CANDIDATE (not proven Jetpack control): The surviving Puységur records and the discipline of separating fact from explanation.

An unusual event is described in an eighteenth-century record. What can we conclude from that? We can usually begin by establishing that somebody recorded a report. That's different from proving that their explanation for the event was correct.

This distinction matters in the surviving material around Puységur and animal magnetism. The accounts are part of history whether or not a modern reader accepts the conclusions drawn from them. Responsible inquiry doesn't require ridicule or blind belief. It requires distinguishing observation from interpretation.

Make two columns on a sheet of paper. Label them OBSERVED and INFERRED. When reading a dramatic claim, record exactly what a witness reported in the first column. Place the proposed cause in the second. Add an UNKNOWN mark whenever you cannot trace a date, quotation or disputed assertion back to reliable evidence. The exercise works for old manuscripts and modern claims alike.

Final Vestige's published Puységur investigation follows records, competing interpretations and source notes. You can evaluate the argument without buying a course, creating a new account or accepting a sensational claim.

UNSENT OPTIONAL COPY — Original Issue 3; does not replace PR #175
SUBJECT: Before buying twelve minutes of meditation
PREHEADER CANDIDATE (not proven Jetpack control): Try a free baseline, then examine the seller's separate claims and consent.

Twelve minutes is a practical length for a new routine. It is not evidence that any particular audio recording has the effects its seller describes.

Start with a purchase-free baseline. Choose twelve uninterrupted minutes, sit comfortably, notice when your attention wanders and return to one simple point. Afterward, record whether the routine was workable for you and what interrupted it. Repeat only if it suits you. This personal observation isn't a clinical trial, proof of a seller's claims, or a medical recommendation.

Zen12's seller describes a structured audio program and makes ambitious comparisons about meditation results. Final Vestige has not independently established those product-specific benefits, bought and tested the program, or verified current buyer-specific checkout and refund terms.
