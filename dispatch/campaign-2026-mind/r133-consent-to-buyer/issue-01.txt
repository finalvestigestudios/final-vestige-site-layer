SUBJECT: What went missing between two pictures?
PREHEADER CANDIDATE (not proven Jetpack control): How to examine an attention failure without exaggerating the science.

Our minds fill in more than we realize. Change-blindness experiments illustrate one reason why: a substantial change can go unnoticed when the comparison is interrupted. That is a finding about attention under particular conditions, not a claim that every eyewitness is unreliable.

Try this without an app, product, or account. Describe a familiar room from memory, then walk into the room and list three details you omitted. Did you miss an object, its location, or its color? Those gaps say something about what you focused on. They do not diagnose a condition or measure intelligence.

Our published investigation examines two classic experimental setups: a visual interruption in a flicker task and a person swapped during a real-world interaction. The historical context matters. A memorable demonstration is not a replacement for the underlying studies and their limits.

For a fuller look, we've traced the original research and explained what it can and cannot establish. The goal is to become a more careful observer, not to distrust every sensation you have.
