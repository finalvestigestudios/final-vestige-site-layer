SUBJECT: A sincere witness can still mistake a cause
PREHEADER CANDIDATE (not proven Jetpack control): The surviving Puységur records and the discipline of separating fact from explanation.

An unusual event is described in an eighteenth-century record. What can we conclude from that? We can usually begin by establishing that somebody recorded a report. That's different from proving that their explanation for the event was correct.

This distinction matters in the surviving material around Puységur and animal magnetism. The accounts are part of history whether or not a modern reader accepts the conclusions drawn from them. Responsible inquiry doesn't require ridicule or blind belief. It requires distinguishing observation from interpretation.

Make two columns on a sheet of paper. Label them OBSERVED and INFERRED. When reading a dramatic claim, record exactly what a witness reported in the first column. Place the proposed cause in the second. Add an UNKNOWN mark whenever you cannot trace a date, quotation or disputed assertion back to reliable evidence. The exercise works for old manuscripts and modern claims alike.
