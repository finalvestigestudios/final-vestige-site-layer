## The editorial contract
The reader must leave knowing something useful even if they never reach a purchase page. Offer placements are capped at **two of six**, not a quota to fill. A prospect's inability to concentrate is not evidence of a disorder, their reluctance to buy is not a defect, and a seller's anecdote is not independent testing. The evidence-first story may use PAS or AIDA, but the conclusion cannot outrun the underlying records.

### Optional opening and decision section, Issue 03 — The twelve-minute question
**Proposed subject (unchanged):** The twelve-minute meditation question  
**Proposed preheader (not a verified Jetpack field):** One free practice, plus the difference between an audio format and a proven effect.

Twelve minutes is a small enough window to fit between interruptions. That is why it makes a persuasive product description. It is also why the description can trick a buyer into answering the wrong question. *Can I find twelve minutes?* is a scheduling question. *Will twelve minutes of this particular recording produce the benefits of an hour of meditation?* is an efficacy question. A stopwatch can answer the first; the seller's clock cannot answer the second.

Try the version that costs nothing. For one minute, write a task no bigger than observing your attention. For nine minutes, sit or walk somewhere safe and notice a neutral sound, your breathing, or the movement of your feet. Each time attention shifts, note the return without treating the lapse as failure. For the final two minutes, record what you noticed: how often you stopped, whether the setting was workable, whether you would repeat it tomorrow. Do not grade the session on whether you felt transformed. One ordinary session is not a controlled trial, and a notebook entry is not a medical result.

Now approach a paid recording as an object of inspection rather than an answer to a problem you haven't measured. Zen12's named sales page advertises twelve-minute sessions, twelve levels across a year and four listening styles, describing 48 MP3 recordings in the full library. Its advertising also calls a session equivalent to an hour of meditation and attributes wide-ranging effects to brainwave audio. The documented format and the hoped-for effect belong in different columns. General studies of meditation cannot demonstrate that this exact commercial package has an hour-equivalent effect; the materials inspected contain no independent product-specific experiment establishing that equivalence.

What would change a careful reader's decision? A verified file list for the actual tier purchased. A chance to hear whether the guided voice is pleasant, intrusive or unhelpful. Clear current delivery and device requirements. Honest checkout terms, including what happens to information entered in a mobile-number field. The seller advertises a one-year refund, but the real transaction and any subsequent claim have not been tested by Final Vestige. A countdown and a discounted checkout price can appear in the selling flow; neither should become our time-limited pressure tactic.

### Optional opening and decision section, Issue 05 — A voice, a timer, and one honest question
**Proposed subject (unchanged):** Before you press play on a hypnosis audio  
**Proposed preheader (not a verified Jetpack field):** A free three-minute check before considering Total Focus.

There is something compelling about a recording promising that the next half-hour will leave you sharper. It asks less of you than a complicated routine. But if you wanted to evaluate the promise, you would first need to decide what *sharper* means. More pages read? Fewer times opening another app? Less frustration? More work completed? Those outcomes are not equivalent, and a person's report about feeling absorbed is not a verified measurement of every one of them.

Choose a modest task you can complete without risk: edit five sentences, sort ten notes, or outline an email. Set a timer for three minutes. Remove one obvious distraction, then note how many deliberate returns to the task were needed. Write down the actual progress, not an assessment of your worth or intelligence. Repeat under roughly similar conditions another day if it feels useful. This is a personal observation exercise, not a diagnosis or a test of hypnosis.

Now examine the named offer. Hypnosis Live calls its recording **Total Focus**, with a seller-described 33-minute duration, 47 MB download and a credited writer/narrator. Those are checkable descriptions of the listing. Claims about brain 'reprogramming', perfect focus and better career results are different: the product page advertises them, but the materials reviewed do not establish independent, product-specific tests supporting those outcomes. Broader clinical work on hypnosis examines particular applications; it cannot be copied across to this downloadable concentration product.

A responsible buyer could legitimately prefer a guided recording. They could also choose to skip it. Questions worth answering first include: Does the seller supply an audio preview that works on the buyer's device? Is the checkout for a single download, a bundle, or something with continued payments? Does the recording require quiet conditions a buyer actually has? What is the exact price at checkout today? The same product URL has appeared with materially different public prices across reviews, including a dated November 2 capture and an earlier R60 capture. Its seller advertises a fourteen-day refund; that is **not** Zen12's longer stated term and we have not tested fulfillment.

If the recording ever becomes a permitted recommendation, the article should show the same questions to someone who decides not to buy. No unverified claim should be transformed into our own testimonial. No price, discount or legal promise belongs in the newsletter without contemporaneous, product-specific verification.

## Conversion notes
- **Interest without fear:** Identify a recognizable reader problem, but never imply readers have a cognitive illness or that a purchase is needed to be well.
- **Credible specificity:** Separate duration, file size and product tiers from an effect that has not been verified.
- **Free useful outcome:** Readers should learn a repeatable observation method before any offer.
- **Decision-first CTA:** In a future approved issue, go to the corresponding first-party guide, not straight to a tracked merchant checkout. The guide must itself explain why declining to buy can be reasonable.
- **One CTA and an honest label:** A named buyer-guide CTA is not a second sales pitch disguised as a source citation. Seller source URLs in manuscripts need editorial/legal review so they cannot silently bypass the guide. Any qualifying offer disclosure belongs near the link in the **actual delivered content**, not merely in a site footer.
- **No false measurement:** Two reported confirmed registrations, zero verified sends and no legitimate experiment are not evidence of open rate, lift, click-through or conversions.
