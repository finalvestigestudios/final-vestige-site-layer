Before you press play on a hypnosis audio

Three questions that matter more than an impressive promise.

A recording offers convenience: you can revisit it, practice privately and decide whether a guided format suits you. That says something about the format. It doesn't settle what the recording can change.

In our historical Puységur files, the hardest part is separating what observers reported from the conclusions they attached to those reports. Modern hypnosis marketing deserves the same scrutiny.

Before buying any self-hypnosis audio, I'd ask three questions.

What is actually included? A downloadable recording, a course, a subscription, or something else? Are the instructions and usage conditions clear?

What is the claim? General relaxation, a pleasant guided experience and a guaranteed change in behavior are different promises. They require different kinds of support.

What happens if it isn't for you? Check current price, format, access terms and refund conditions on the seller's actual page, not in an old promotion. For example, the merchant's FAQ presently says refund requests must be made within 14 days and limits refunds to five sessions per year. Confirm those terms yourself at checkout.

A program under review: Hypnosis Live. The official Hypnosis Live catalog currently offers more than 200 downloadable self-hypnosis MP3 titles, including tracks marketed around focus, organization and habits. Its FAQ says most individual sessions are about 40 minutes and available immediately after purchase, with no recurring charge for the individual recording. This is useful format information, not proof that a particular recording changes behavior. The NIH's hypnosis review describes promising evidence for certain uses but mixed or limited evidence elsewhere; that evidence cannot automatically be generalized to these specific commercial recordings.
