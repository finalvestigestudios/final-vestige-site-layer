### Critical interpretive distinction: five minutes versus seven and a half
The retrospective opening says “five minutes”; the embedded earlier letter says “a half-quarter of an hour” (about seven and a half minutes). Both are *approximate time reports by one author in one published work*, not independent clocks. **We cannot resolve whether rounding, memory, editing, or transcription explains the difference.** Avoid writing “exactly five minutes” or “exactly 7½ minutes” in final newsletter copy; mention the discrepancy if precision is central.

### Essential editorial boundaries
1. The document attests **that Puységur made the report**. It cannot establish that magnetism cured an infection, that the subject entered a discrete modern hypnotic state, or that any paranormal cause was involved.
2. “Victor Race” is a name supplied in later historiography. The excerpt of the May 8 letter calls him a peasant; a later May 17 letter calls him “Victor.” Do not pretend the name “Race” appears in these specific paragraphs.
3. The letter is an interested witness's narrative; apparent memory loss, thoughts and recovery are **reported** not clinically observed under controlled conditions.
4. The 1784 Paris royal commissions investigated Mesmer's magnetism more broadly, not a blind replication of this May 4 incident. Avoid a false direct scientific adjudication.
5. The book's original imprint is bibliographically complex (1784/1785 first volume). Use “1784 account,” not unsupportable universal “the exact first 1784 press run.”
6. The author's own admission of a case that did not respond changes the texture of the evidence without making his claimed successes scientifically valid.
