## New finding B — issue 04 headline lacks event-specific numerical provenance
Original issue #04 subject in the authenticated original manifest: **“Fourteen seconds. A much longer explanation.”** In the original issue body, the endpoints of the interval are undefined. NASA's [NSSDC Apollo 1 summary](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/lunar/apollo1info.html) reports **17 seconds from the first indication of the fire to last crew communication**, whereas the [Apollo 204 Review Board](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/apollo/apollo-1/report-of-the-apollo-204-review-board/) documents a **much longer hatch-opening effort**, completed approximately five minutes after the first fire report. They are different intervals. These sources do *not* prove no separately defined 14-second interval occurred. They do establish that the headline's apparent precision **needs a definition and primary-source citation** before any publication/email.

The safest candidate is a **number-free subject** rather than substituting another ambiguous number: **“Apollo 1: the fire a single spark can't explain.”** Proposed but unverified native preheader: **“The official record reveals multiple hazards and an ignition source the Board could not establish conclusively.”** Verify the exact Board conclusion before approval, then compare the selected headline to WordPress's **post title** and actual inbox presentation. The original issue is unchanged.
