## Issue 01 — accountable perception journalism
- Primary experiment: Simons & Levin (1998), "Failure to detect changes to people during a real-world interaction", https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03208840 . Abstract supports conversation-stage person replacement, ~half of pedestrians detecting in one experiment, and social-group effect. Study does not report a universal eyewitness error rate. This is the **door/person substitution** result, not the gorilla experiment.
- Distinct second experiment: Simons & Chabris (1999), "Gorillas in our midst", https://doi.org/10.1068/p281059 . The publication's abstract reports an unexpected object in a dynamic scene and variation in noticing based on monitoring-task difficulty and object similarity. The R65 letter deliberately refrains from importing an unverified single detection percentage from this different design.
- Bridge from experiment to archival source criticism is an editorial inference, clearly introduced as such. Do not imply contemporary historians re-ran this study, or that a particular historical witness was mistaken.
- SINGLE editorial CTA: https://finalvestige.com/the-missing-caption/ (published destination listed in canonical Issue #175). Source citations are references, not secondary sales CTAs.

## Issue 06 — honest logging rather than a miracle
- Primary report: Stone, Shiffman, Schwartz, Broderick & Hufford (2002), "Patient non-compliance with paper diaries", *The BMJ*, https://www.bmj.com/content/324/7347/1193 . Paper users: 90% **reported** timely entries versus 11% electronically evidenced compliance within the narrow time window; 20% under wider 90-minute window. A specific study with 80 patients comparing paper and electronic diaries, not a proven effect size for focus training.
- The proposed four-column daily exercise is an original, optional editorial idea; it is NOT validated medical treatment, independent experimental evidence, or a measurement of cognitive improvement.
- SINGLE editorial CTA: https://finalvestige.com/focus/ — an existing published free Focus Fieldbook, 52-page edition verified in WordPress newsletter settings.
- Neither newsletter asserts an independently tested physical product, an affiliate purchase, or a guaranteed outcome.
