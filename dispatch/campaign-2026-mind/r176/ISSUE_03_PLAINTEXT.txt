THE DISPATCH — UNSENT INTERNAL REVIEW (NOT A JETPACK MESSAGE)
SUBJECT: Twelve minutes, two different claims
PREHEADER PROPOSAL: A workable attention exercise isn't proof an audio equals an hour of meditation.

An hour can feel impossibly long when the day is already full. Twelve minutes is different: you can find twelve minutes between the interruptions.

That makes a short attention routine practical. It doesn't prove that a recording can compress an hour of meditation into twelve minutes.

In 1784, investigators testing animal magnetism had to distinguish what witnesses experienced from the explanation offered. An observation can be genuine without its proposed cause being established. The distinction still matters.

TRY THE PART YOU CAN CHECK
On three ordinary days, choose a consistent twelve-minute window. Put your phone away. Rest your attention on breathing or an ordinary sound. When it wanders, notice and return. Afterwards, record: Did you do it? What interrupted you? Would you repeat it?

That notebook can't diagnose a condition or prove a medical benefit. It can help you judge whether the routine fits your life.

THE PRODUCT UNDER REVIEW
Zen12's maker describes a Premium package of 12 levels and 48 MP3 recordings in four formats. Its claim that 12 minutes delivers an hour's meditation benefits is a seller claim, not an independently established equivalence. Broad mindfulness studies do not verify this exact product or promise.
