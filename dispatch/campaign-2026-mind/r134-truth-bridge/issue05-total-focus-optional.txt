Subject: What a 33-minute focus recording can—and can't—promise
Preheader: Three checkpoints for Total Focus, with seller details separated from evidence.

It would be convenient if attention worked like a switch. Press play, and every interruption disappears.

That expectation is worth examining before anyone spends money on a recording. In the historical accounts surrounding Puységur, observers described striking experiences; the records still require us to separate what happened from what the observers believed caused it. The same discipline applies to modern advertisements for hypnosis audio.

Here is a useful three-part buyer's test—one you can apply without purchasing anything.

1. CHECK THE EXACT ITEM. The official Hypnosis Live listing describes Total Focus as a 33-minute, 47 MB audio download. It is not the seller's separate 44-minute Hypnotize Yourself instructional recording. Make sure a review, link and checkout all name the same item.

2. SEPARATE FORMAT FROM EFFECT. The seller describes an audio session with hypnosis and NLP exercises and advertises dramatically improved concentration. A 33-minute file is an observable format. A promise of “limitless” concentration is not an independently established result. We have not tested this recording or established its effectiveness.

3. READ THE REFUND TERMS. The seller advertises a fourteen-day window; its FAQ also describes annual limits on refunded sessions. Prices have varied across observed listings. Confirm the terms displayed at checkout before buying; do not rely on a price preserved in an old email.

No special audio is required for a useful starting experiment. Choose a task, remove one predictable distraction for ten minutes, and note afterward whether you completed the task—not whether you felt transformed.
