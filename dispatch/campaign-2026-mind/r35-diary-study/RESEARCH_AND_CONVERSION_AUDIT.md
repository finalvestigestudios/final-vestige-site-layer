## Why this revision exists
Our previous R34 proved that all four editorial **destinations** are published, but that does not establish that the underlying manuscript treats observations correctly. R35 therefore reread the six exact canonical drafts at R34 head `0ab1acb91e000e42cb035a14adc19d223b4c9c1c` and checked their key claims against primary academic records, NASA, scholarly history, and official *product-specific* sales pages. The largest actionable new concern is not a broken hyperlink: **Issue 06's diary-sensor inference is slightly stronger than the instrument could prove.**

The canonical Issue 06 says: "the sensors showed that just 11 percent of entries met the study's narrower timing window." In Stone et al.'s 2002 BMJ report, the paper notebooks contained photo-sensors logging **when each binder was opened and closed**. A binder was counted as compliant if it was opened or closed within the relevant 30-minute window. Those sensors could not observe the pen touching paper or establish the exact time the words were recorded. Calling the 11% figure an **entry-level measurement** subtly overstates the evidence and violates the site's own editorial principle of separating a direct observation from an inference. The original is not a medical or meditation study; the paper group was **40 patients with chronic pain**, and a separate **40** used an electronic diary. Researchers reported **90% self-reported** vs **11% sensor-timed** paper compliance. Their "actual compliance" is their operational criterion, not a direct camera view of someone completing a form. The article's 2002 date should not be confused with the same researchers' longer **2003** follow-up, *Controlled Clinical Trials* 24:182–199.

**Primary evidence:** Stone AA, Shiffman S, Schwartz JE, Broderick JE, Hufford MR. *Patient non-compliance with paper diaries*. **BMJ**, 2002;324:1193–1194, [free complete article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC111114/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.324.7347.1193). See Methods paragraphs and Table 1. Distinguish [the distinct 2003 publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12689739/), which is related but is not the original 2002 citation.

**Candidate edit for explicit editorial review:** The independently composed `ISSUE_06_REVIEW_CANDIDATE.md` and matching HTML/plaintext deliver the same single CTA to the **already published** /focus/ page. They replace "entries met the window" with "paper binders were opened or closed inside the window." The candidate specifically explains that sensors do not reveal what was written.
