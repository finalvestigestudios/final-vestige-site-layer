**Subject:** What did seven days of attention reveal?

**Preheader:** A diary timestamp can look precise. Here is what the underlying evidence really measured.

**Content type:** editorial, no merchant recommendation

**An entry can have a time written beside it. That does not prove when it was written.**

In 2002, a research team studying pain diaries gave 40 participants paper notebooks fitted with unobtrusive light sensors. Another 40 participants used electronic diaries. Both groups were asked to record their symptoms at three set times each day. The paper group wrote times that suggested roughly 90% compliance. The sensors, however, showed that the binders had been opened or closed within the prescribed 30-minute windows for only about 11% of scheduled opportunities.

**What did the sensor actually measure?**

It recorded when the paper binder opened and closed. It did not independently read the words on the page, prove exactly when each sentence was written, or establish why a particular entry was late. The 90% and 11% figures describe that clinical study's paper-diary timing measures—not meditation, attention improvement, or the honesty of every person who keeps a journal.

**A better seven-day record**

If you tried a short attention routine this week, resist the urge to make the story neater afterward. A modest log can be much more useful than a dramatic verdict. At the end of each session, record four things while the details are still fresh:

- When: Roughly when you practiced, or "not done." Do not backfill a minute you cannot verify.
- What: What you actually completed, including a shorter-than-planned session.
- Conditions: One interruption, distraction, or circumstance that mattered.
- Next adjustment: One practical change you will try tomorrow.

**Keep the claim proportional to the record.**

After seven days you may know whether you showed up and whether a routine fit your schedule. That does not establish that the practice caused a measurable gain in concentration: sleep, work, expectation, illness and ordinary variation can all affect a small personal log. If a day is missing, mark it missing. A blank cell is better evidence than an invented detail.
