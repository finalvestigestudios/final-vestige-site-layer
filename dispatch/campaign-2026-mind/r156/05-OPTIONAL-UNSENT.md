**Subject:** Total Focus: the recording isn't the result

**Preheader:** The specific 33-minute MP3, ambitious seller claims, and refund terms worth checking.

**Product:** Hypnosis Live Total Focus — seller-alignment review only

A recording is easy to describe. An improvement in concentration takes evidence. Marketing copy can make those sound like the same thing.

Consider one named product rather than the entire Hypnosis Live catalog: Total Focus. Its official page identifies a thirty-three-minute, 47-MB downloadable audio session. That is a checkable description of the format. The same page promises sharpened concentration, faster work, and greater professional effectiveness. Those are seller claims, not independently demonstrated results for this recording.

The historical Puységur archive is a useful reminder: observers recorded experiences, then proposed explanations. The first part of a record can be genuine even where the explanation has not been established. We use that distinction with modern products too.

Before spending, ask what you want from a session. A guided listening experience is one kind of purchase. A reliable improvement in task performance is an outcome claim requiring much stronger support. We have not verified a product-specific trial capable of proving that outcome.

Next look at the precise cart item. A single MP3 and a bundle are different products, and the seller has displayed changing promotional amounts across page captures. Our email deliberately does not promise a price, savings percentage, or exclusive discount.

Finally, read the refund terms before paying. The official FAQ describes a 14-day request period and says refunds are limited to five sessions in a year. This is what the seller states, not independent evidence that a particular order qualifies or that a refund will be completed.

You can also try a free comparison: reserve a quiet thirty-three-minute work period, write down distractions, and record what you completed. Don't claim a single session proves anything. It may still clarify whether you prefer a structured audio experience.
