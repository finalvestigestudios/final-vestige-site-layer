The part of your attention that stayed behind

Two 2009 experiments help explain why switching tasks can leave unfinished thoughts behind.

An interrupted task rarely ends when you close its window. You move on to the next thing, but a small argument about the first one may keep running in the background.

In 2009, researcher Sophie Leroy investigated what happens when people shift between work tasks. Across two experiments, she reported that lingering thoughts about a previous, unfinished task could interfere with performance on the next. The results were about experimental task transitions. They were not a demonstration that a particular meditation recording, app or daily routine can fix attention.

The useful distinction is between the work you are doing and the work you are still thinking about. One is visible on your screen. The other is easier to overlook.

Try a modest observation before deciding you need a new product. During your next two task changes, jot down what you left unfinished, whether thoughts of it resurfaced, and how quickly you settled into the next activity. Compare the notes. You are observing your own transitions, not conducting a controlled clinical experiment.
