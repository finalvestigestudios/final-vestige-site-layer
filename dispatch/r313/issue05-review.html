Before a hypnosis recording earns your trust

Proposed preheader: The specifics of Total Focus—and the questions its sales page cannot answer. Not verified as a real Jetpack inbox snippet.

A recording has an advantage over a sweeping promise: you can usually check what you're buying. The duration and format are concrete. Whether listening will change your concentration is a different question.

That distinction is familiar from the surviving Puységur papers. Historical observers wrote down what they said they witnessed, but their reports do not validate every explanation later attached to them. Modern product advertising deserves at least the same scrutiny.

The official Hypnosis Live page lists Total Focus as a 33-minute, 47 MB self-hypnosis recording credited to J. A. Amos. Seller description, not independently established evidence. Those specifications do not establish a meaningful improvement in attention.

Three questions before buying

What arrives? Confirm the named download, playback format, accessibility, support and whether this is one recording rather than a subscription.

What was tested? A general study of hypnosis or attention cannot prove this named recording's promised outcomes.

What if it doesn't fit? Verify the checkout total, selected bundle and written refund policy at the moment of purchase.

Before spending, try a modest alternative: give one manageable task twelve minutes and record what interrupted you. That is personal reflection, not clinical evidence.
