SUBJECT: Does twelve minutes buy an hour?
PREHEADER: A short routine is easy to test. The one-hour equivalence is harder to prove.
STATUS: UNSENT / OPTIONAL / NOT THE NATIVE JETPACK EMAIL

Imagine setting a timer for twelve minutes. The clock runs down, you take off your headphones, and the afternoon has moved forward exactly twelve minutes. What else changed?

The seller of Zen12 describes twelve progressive levels and four audio styles per level—48 recordings in the Premium package. It also asserts that a short listening session delivers the benefits of much longer meditation. These are SELLER CLAIMS, not demonstrated equivalence.

The eighteenth-century animal-magnetism investigations faced a related challenge: observers described sensations, but their reports did not by themselves establish the proposed explanation. An experience deserves attention; a causal claim needs a test.

Start with what you can observe. For three days, choose a twelve-minute interval. Keep your setting similar. Record whether you finished, when you noticed distraction, and whether the routine is workable for you. This is not a clinical experiment or a measurement of brainwaves.

Before buying audio, ask what you actually receive, which claims have product-specific evidence, and what the seller currently says about access, price and refunds. No purchase is necessary to use your free 52-page Focus Fieldbook.
