Does twelve minutes buy an hour?

A short routine is easy to test. The one-hour equivalence is harder to prove.

Imagine setting a timer for twelve minutes. The clock runs down, you take off your headphones, and the afternoon has moved forward exactly twelve minutes. What else changed?

Zen12's seller describes twelve levels and four audio styles per level—48 recordings in its Premium package. The same page claims that a short listening session delivers much longer meditation benefits. Those are seller assertions, not independent proof of one-hour equivalence.

The eighteenth-century animal-magnetism investigators encountered a similar gap: reported sensations do not automatically establish their proposed cause.

Three days; one ordinary question

Choose a twelve-minute interval and similar setting on three days. Record whether you completed the interval, when your attention wandered, and whether you'd repeat it. This measures the workability of your routine, not brainwaves or treatment outcomes.

Before buying: ask what is supplied, whether the product has specific evidence for its advertised benefits, and what the live seller page says about checkout, access and refunds. You already have the free Focus Fieldbook; a purchase is optional.
