Subject: Seven days aren't a verdict
Preheader: Your notes beat a feeling. Reopen the Fieldbook without signing up again.

A recovered notebook can be more useful than its author's grandest conclusion. It tells you what was written down at the time—not merely how the story felt afterward.

The same distinction matters when we ask whether a focus routine helped us.

Perhaps you tried the twelve-minute starting routine in the Seven Day Focus Fieldbook. Perhaps you downloaded the book and life intervened. Neither outcome deserves a victory speech or a guilty one.

On one page, make three columns: ATTEMPTED, OBSERVED, UNKNOWN.

ATTEMPTED: What small action did you mean to take?
OBSERVED: Did you start? How long did you work? What interrupted you?
UNKNOWN: What can't you fairly attribute to this routine?

A note like “I started twice in four days; I don't know why Thursday was easier” is more useful than “This technique changed my brain.”

In a 2016 meta-analysis, Harkin and colleagues reviewed 138 randomized studies involving 19,951 participants. Interventions increasing monitoring were associated on average with greater goal attainment. That does not test this Fieldbook or establish that any meditation/hypnosis product works.
