Twelve minutes is an inviting proposition. It fits into a crowded morning, asks for less than the time we often lose scrolling, and feels possible even when an hour doesn't.

But there's an important difference between a routine being easier to try and an audio product delivering the results its seller describes.

If you want to explore meditation, begin by testing the part you control. Choose a short, quiet period. Sit comfortably. Notice when your attention moves. Return to one simple point of focus without treating that return as failure. You can keep a few notes: how consistently you practiced, what got in the way, and whether the routine was workable for you.

This isn't a clinical intervention or a guarantee of a particular mental state. It's a modest, low-cost way to learn something about your own habits.

A tool we're examining: Zen12. Inspire3 describes Zen12 as a meditation program designed around shorter sessions. That's the seller's positioning—not an independent finding that twelve minutes produces the same effects as an hour of meditation.
