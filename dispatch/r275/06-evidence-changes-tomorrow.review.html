What if the evidence changes tomorrow?

A three-line record for tracking altered sources—without asking you to claim the Fieldbook twice.

A report can change while its address stays the same. Return to a web page after a week and the sentence you quoted may have disappeared. What changed? Before guessing why, you need to know what version you actually saw.

A website may replace a chart, correct a transcription, move an attachment or change its page layout. None of those possibilities alone proves concealment. A web archive can preserve an earlier page, but its capture may miss images, scripts or linked files. The snapshot is evidence of a particular retrieval, not a perfect copy of the whole site.

Keep a three-line evidence log. First: the exact URL, date and passage you observed. Second: the file you saved, with a name or checksum if available. Third: a dated independent copy or a second source. If you cannot recover the older version, write ‘not recovered’ rather than ‘destroyed.’

That habit extends what we ask in investigations: where did a claim come from, and what can the surviving record actually establish? It is related to attention, but not the same exercise as the Seven Day Focus Fieldbook already included in your welcome email.
