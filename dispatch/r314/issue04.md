**Proposed subject:** Apollo 1: the cause the report could not pin down

**Proposed preheader:** Investigators identified lethal conditions without proving one exact ignition point.

**Preheader is editorial metadata, not a verified Jetpack inbox snippet.**

---

When investigators opened the burned Apollo command module, they faced a puzzle. The evidence explained how a fire could spread through the cabin. It did not conclusively identify the precise event that ignited it.

The Apollo 204 Review Board called an electrical arc the most probable initiator. In the same report, it stated that no single ignition source had been conclusively identified. Those are not competing explanations. They are two different levels of certainty.

Other findings were painfully concrete. The ground test took place in an atmosphere of pure oxygen, pressurized to 16.7 pounds per square inch absolute. Combustible materials were present close to potential ignition sources. The inward-opening hatch could not open against elevated internal pressure. An ideal hatch-opening sequence required far longer than the fire allowed.

A tidy headline would point to one wire and call the case closed. The actual report described a system: design choices, test conditions, hazardous materials and emergency constraints. The question was not only where a spark occurred, but why any spark could become unsurvivable.

Here is a useful way to read the official findings. Make three columns: observed evidence; probable explanations; recommendations. The oxygen environment and hatch constraints belong in the first. An electrical arc as the likely initiator belongs in the second. Removing combustible materials and simplifying escape belong in the third.

Keeping those columns separate does not weaken the account. It is how an investigation respects the dead without borrowing certainty from a conclusion it cannot prove.

Our published investigation, The Hatch Opened Inward, follows the physical details and the historical record. It is an archival case, not a meditation analogy. The connection is our method: claims should never run further than the evidence.
