**Proposed subject:** Your seven-day focus experiment isn't a verdict

**Proposed preheader:** A useful evidence log shows what worked without pretending to prove a cure.

**Preheader is editorial metadata, not a verified Jetpack inbox snippet.**

---

Seven days can leave you with one of two grand impressions: the routine changed everything, or it changed nothing. Neither conclusion is useful if the notes tell a smaller story.

Try counting what you actually did. Which days did you start? How many minutes did you set aside? What interrupted you? Was it easier to begin at a particular time? A written observation is modest, but much more useful than a verdict based on how the week felt.

Suppose the notes show that you began four times, always just after work. On three other days you planned a later start and never got to it. That is not proof of a universal law. It is a reasonable reason to test the earlier cue again next week.

Change only one part of your routine for the next seven days. Write down the intended starting time; mark whether you began; add one sentence about the obstacle. You do not need to buy an audio recording or report results to us. Nor does a personal focus log count as a controlled study or evidence of treatment efficacy.

The Seven Day Focus Fieldbook — Complete Edition includes a twelve-minute starting practice, seven guided days, an obstacle playbook and a thirty-day log. It is a free planning resource, not a clinically tested intervention.
