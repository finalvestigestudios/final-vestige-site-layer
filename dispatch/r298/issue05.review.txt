THE DISPATCH — ISSUE 05 / UNSENT REVIEW
Subject: What a hypnosis recording can—and cannot—promise
Preheader: A 33-minute recording, a much longer history, and three questions worth asking before any purchase.

It is easy to mistake a clear instruction for a clear result.

That difficulty is not new. In the surviving accounts associated with the Marquis de Puységur, observers described striking experiences and tried to explain them using the language available at the time. Their reports are historical evidence of what people said and believed; they do not establish every mechanism proposed to explain what happened.

A modern audio recording deserves that same separation. You can verify its duration and what format you are buying. You cannot infer from a confident product description that it will reliably change your attention, habits or health.

Take Hypnosis Live's Total Focus, one of the recordings we are examining. The seller currently describes a 33-minute, 47 MB downloadable audio session. It also advertises stronger concentration and productivity outcomes. Those are the seller's claims. A product listing is not an independent study of that specific recording.

Three checks before pressing play

Separate the format from the effect. What exactly is delivered, and are you comfortable with a guided recording?

Check what the research actually tested. Evidence about hypnosis generally is not proof of the effects advertised for this recording.

Read the terms on the day you decide. Verify the seller's current checkout, access and refund conditions yourself; old screenshots and promotions can mislead.

There is no need to buy anything to test whether a shorter, deliberate period of attention fits your day. If you use the free Focus Fieldbook, its notes can help distinguish what you practiced from what you hoped would happen.
