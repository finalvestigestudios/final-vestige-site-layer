Twelve minutes is a question, not a promise

Proposed preheader: A psychologist's 1890 observation, a free comparison, and what an audio seller can actually establish. Native preheader mapping unverified.

In 1890, William James separated the attention that arrives without effort from the attention we deliberately renew. His observation was a puzzle to investigate, not a stopwatch to sell.

You sit down to work. Three unrelated tasks insist on being done first. Could twelve minutes help you begin? Perhaps. It would not prove that a particular audio product changes your brain or replaces a longer meditation.

The NIH's NCCIH notes that meditation studies vary in methods and quality, sometimes making broad conclusions uncertain. These findings do not independently establish any particular result from Zen12.

Try a free baseline before spending

  1. Pick one modest task and silence nonessential alerts.
  2. Set a twelve-minute timer. Notice when you have to bring your attention back.
  3. Record what you finished, without turning the note into a medical or scientific score. Repeat on three comparable days.

Zen12's own page describes several levels of downloadable MP3 programs. Its time-compression, concentration and relaxation promises are seller claims, not results independently verified for this product in our review.

Ask what you would actually receive, whether a free alternative does the job, which refund conditions apply at your checkout, and how you would tell whether a paid recording helped.
