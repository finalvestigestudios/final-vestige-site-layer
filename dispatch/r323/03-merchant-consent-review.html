What “free” means on Zen12’s audio page
The seller mentions its own newsletter. That is separate from The Dispatch.

A free audio file sounds like a small decision. Download it, listen, and decide whether the format suits you.

But the first decision may not be about listening at all.

On its free-MP3 page, Zen12 says people requesting the audio will also receive the seller’s newsletter. That is a second email relationship, separate from The Dispatch you signed up for at Final Vestige. We do not treat a Zen12 signup as consent to our newsletter, or your existing Dispatch subscription as permission for Zen12 to contact you.

Why does that distinction matter? Because “try a free file,” “join a newsletter,” and “buy a complete program” are three different choices. A thoughtful offer lets you see each one.

THE FORMAT VERSUS THE CLAIM

Zen12 sells several packages, not one interchangeable library. Its site presents Beginner as six levels with 24 MP3s, Premium as twelve levels with 48, and Advanced as the later six levels with 24. Before paying, identify which package is actually selected at checkout. Do not assume an introductory download includes the paid program.

The seller also says twelve minutes of its audio can deliver benefits comparable to an hour of meditation. That is an advertising claim, not a conclusion Final Vestige has independently validated. The U.S. National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health reviews studies of meditation and mindfulness more broadly; it does not establish that result for Zen12.

A useful experiment is simpler. Pick one twelve-minute period on several days. Record whether you began, what interrupted you, and whether the routine was workable. This will not prove that an audio file changed your brain. It can tell you what you were actually able to do.

THREE QUESTIONS BEFORE ANY PAID STEP

1. Which exact edition and files are included? Ask for the current checkout terms, not an old screenshot.
2. Does a free sample also enroll me in the seller’s emails? Check the merchant’s wording before entering an address.
3. Which benefits are product-specific evidence, and which are seller promises? Do not substitute studies of meditation in general.
