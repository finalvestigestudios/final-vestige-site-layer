**Subject:** The mind is not a camera

**Excerpt / suggested email preview:** A simple experiment in what attention misses—and why feeling certain is not the same as having a complete record.

**Kind:** editorial

---

Think of the last time you saw a familiar room and felt certain you had noticed everything. You probably hadn't. That is not a personal failing; it is a feature of attention.

Researchers studying change blindness have repeatedly demonstrated that people can miss striking alterations even while looking directly at a scene. The surprising part is not simply that we miss things. It is how much confidence we can attach to what we think we saw.

Here's a small exercise: look away from a room, recall five details, then look back and check them. Which details did you notice, and which did you fill in afterward? One attempt does not diagnose your memory. It does remind you that observation and explanation are different steps.

That distinction matters in every Final Vestige investigation. A photograph is a record of some things, not a guarantee that its most popular interpretation is right. A witness can be sincere and incomplete. An unusual observation can remain interesting even when the first explanation fails.

We've assembled a sourced investigation of change blindness, including what the experiments really demonstrate and what they cannot establish.
