**Subject:** Before hypnosis had a name

**Excerpt / suggested email preview:** Puységur’s eighteenth-century records raise a surprisingly modern question: what did witnesses observe, and what did they infer?

**Kind:** editorial

---

In the 1780s, observers of the Marquis de Puységur described unusual states and behaviors in the vocabulary available to them. Animal magnetism was a proposed explanation. The surviving documents tell us what some people reported, not that an invisible magnetic fluid had been established.

The temptation today is the same as it was then: choose a dramatic conclusion before examining the record.

Our research desk starts somewhere less theatrical. Who wrote the account? When was it recorded? Was the writer observing directly? Are we reading a contemporary document, a later edition or somebody else's summary?

Those questions don't strip the mystery away. They make the investigation more interesting, because the uncertainty becomes visible instead of being hidden behind certainty.

If you encounter an extraordinary claim this week, try three questions. What is the closest source? What was directly observed? What alternative explanation would make you reconsider?

We've brought the relevant Puységur materials, citations and their limitations together in a free source archive. It is historical research, not a recommendation to follow eighteenth-century treatments.
