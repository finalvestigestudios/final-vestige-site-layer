**Kind:** editorial

---

A clean explanation is often shorter than the evidence supporting it. Sometimes it is too short.

On January 27, 1967, the Apollo 1 crew died during a ground test. The Saturn IB was unfueled, but the command module was filled with an oxygen-rich atmosphere and contained multiple hazards. The inward-opening hatch made escape especially difficult.

The Apollo 204 Review Board identified dangerous conditions and found that an electrical arc was the most probable initiator. It did not conclusively identify one single ignition source. That distinction is not a technicality. Probable and proven mean different things.

The Board's surviving account records four time markers in an approximately fifteen-second interval after the initial verbal report. Our timeline shows those markers and refuses to invent measurements between them.
