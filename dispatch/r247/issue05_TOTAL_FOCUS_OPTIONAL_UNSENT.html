The file is 33 minutes. The promise is much bigger.

Imagine buying thirty-three minutes of concentration. Headphones on. A recording plays. The distractions disappear. That's a compelling picture—and a much larger claim than the existence of an audio file.

Our historical Puységur reporting taught us to distinguish observation from interpretation. Commercial claims deserve the same treatment.

The Total Focus seller page describes a 33-minute, 47-MB downloadable MP3, recorded by J. A. Amos. The seller promises exceptionally sharp concentration and refers to hypnosis and NLP. Those promises have not been independently demonstrated for this specific recording.

The NIH's NCCIH describes research on hypnosis for particular uses, with limits. That evidence cannot be transferred wholesale to this concentration product.

Three questions before spending

  1. Format: Is a guided 33-minute recording what you want?
  2. Evidence: What supports the exact advertised outcome?
  3. Terms: What are the final price and refund limits for this checkout?

A free alternative is to log one week of task starts, focused intervals and interruptions. You'll have a baseline, not a guarantee.
