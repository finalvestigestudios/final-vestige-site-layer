Twelve minutes of audio. What are you buying?

The appeal of short listening sessions is easy to understand. The evidence behind claims of automatic brainwave benefits is a different question.

In 1998 the rubber-hand illusion showed how a compelling sensation might be assigned to an unexpected place. Later researchers studied expectation and attentional instructions. None of those studies tested a consumer MP3 library. Read the original experiment, methodology critique and attention research before turning a perception anecdote into a causal promise.

Try twelve minutes without buying

Spend two minutes choosing a comfortable place, three noting ordinary visual and tactile details, three recording attention shifts, two distinguishing what you directly observed from what you inferred, and two noting what further evidence would be needed. This is a reflective exercise, not a treatment or a promised result.
