01 · An honest thirty-second finding

The bell was not a cure.

In 1987, Wegner and colleagues asked participants to speak their thoughts during five-minute tasks; those suppressing the image of a white bear could ring a bell when it intruded. Later, they mentioned the bear more often than a comparison group allowed to think about it from the start. A specified distracter reduced later preoccupation in a second experiment.

This laboratory result reveals a weakness in a particular instruction, not a product effect. It cannot establish that prerecorded meditation or a brainwave-frequency claim solves intrusive thoughts.

Three boundaries

Observed Thought reports and bell rings under specific laboratory instructions.

Interpreted A possible rebound and cognitive-monitoring explanation.

Not demonstrated That Zen12 or any other audio provides treatment or equivalent meditation hours.

02 · Free experiment, no account required

A gentler test of instructions

1 / 60 seconds

Name the rule

Pick a harmless ordinary object, such as a yellow umbrella. For one minute, try not to picture it. Note any times you catch yourself checking for it.

2 / 60 seconds

Choose an anchor

For another minute, attend to a neutral sound or the feeling of your hands. When attention wanders, return to that anchor without trying to forbid thoughts.

3 / Reflect

Compare the instructions

Which asked you to look for failure, and which gave you something to return to? Your experience is personal observation, not a scientific replication.

Use neutral objects only. Do not deliberately summon upsetting memories or distressing intrusive thoughts. Skip if uncomfortable; this is not therapy.
