The decision and the switched outcome

In 2005, Johansson and colleagues asked participants to choose a face from a pair. Researchers covertly replaced some chosen outcomes, and some participants then explained the face they had not actually selected. The phenomenon is called choice blindness. It is not a clinical test of meditation, nor evidence that an audio recording can change anyone's mind. Original study summary.

We often shop with an outcome already pictured in our head: the files we expect, the routine we'll follow, the way we'll feel. A convincing description can make us confuse the seller's promise with the package that exists. Before choosing, write down the reason you are considering this exact product. That is a practical record, not a scientifically validated cure for bias.
