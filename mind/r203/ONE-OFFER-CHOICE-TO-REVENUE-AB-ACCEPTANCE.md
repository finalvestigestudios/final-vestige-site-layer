## One high-intent reader and one choice

*Buyer intention:* “I want a repeatable, structured meditation audio program and I'm weighing what is delivered against the promise.” This is not “anyone who watched a curious optical illusion.” The relevant problems are choosing a format, finding a routine, understanding precisely what's included, and deciding whether a paid bundle is better suited than free practice.

*Offer under review:* Zen12 Premium as a recorded-audio library, **not** an anxiety treatment or a faster equivalent to advanced meditation. An affiliate decision remains conditional on merchant approval and account terms, genuine product fit, accurate substantiation, relevant disclosure, and founder release approval.

*Editorial story:* The 2005 choice-blindness face-swap is memorable precisely because the researchers preserved a ground-truth record of a participant's initial action. A product shopper can preserve a personal record of the promised feature and actually delivered content without reproducing the deception. This is a useful analogy, **not a claim that the exercise was tested as a psychological intervention**, and not a demonstration that Zen12 changes cognition.

*Evidence gap:* the specific efficacy claims on Zen12's page, final checkout total, fulfillment, refund performance and Final Vestige account approval remain unverified. The near-offer copy prominently says so.

## The experiment that could matter

**Hypothesis, not outcome:** with one authorized offer and the same independently checked price/limitations/disclosure, readers might understand the decision better when shown a short, optional original film. That additional clarity might affect verified paid purchases. It might also distract them, slow the page or reduce purchases.

**Variant A:** text-only independent buying guide with seller inventory, research caveats, free alternative, questions to investigate, and native disabled CTA pending approval.

**Variant B:** exactly the same decision content and CTA, with one additional 42-second film concept, lightweight static poster and full written transcript. No autoplay, streaming dependency, JavaScript or forced playback. The current B is only a CSS poster and storyboard/transcript; **not a rendered video**. A later film requires rights-clearance, a real MP4, checked WebVTT, verified timing, a poster with modest dimensions and independently measured mobile CWV.

**Before running any traffic test:** verify merchant authorization, link/terms/channel rights, founder-approved public release, accessible layouts, purchase route, refunds and attribution agreements. If a partner provides no independently verifiable sale report, we cannot declare winner on clicks alone. The relevant outcome is 7- and 30-day cohort **net revenue per 1,000 qualified-human landing sessions**. Also inspect confirmed orders, customers, actual paid conversion, refunds, fees, and cost of producing the variant. No traffic sample size is sufficient yet; no statistical significance or uplift should be asserted. The film is not allowed to dominate LCP or appear above editorial substance merely for decoration.

**Assignment unit** proposed for a future experiment: one consent-compatible first-party session, assigned persistently within a 7/30-day reporting window where allowed by the site's privacy rules, not a person-level fingerprint. Where persistence is not approved, randomization and repeated-session contamination must be documented as a limitation rather than secretly adding cookies or local storage. De-duplicate repeat pageviews and bots by an approved privacy-safe rule; do not invent identity.

### Key arithmetic and missing data

For a 7- or 30-day cohort: **verified paid conversion rate** = verified attributable paid orders / qualified-human landing sessions. **Net revenue** = actually recognized seller receipts or received/receivable approved commissions less refunds, fees and direct attributable fulfillment costs (consistent accounting basis, labeled). **Net revenue / 1,000 qualified sessions** = 1000 × net revenue / verified qualified sessions.

Missing denominator or merchant-confirmed payments ⇒ **NOT COMPUTABLE**, never automatically $0. A handful of purchases warrants a prominent tiny-sample warning and no causal film-winner claim. Leads and outbound clicks require their own counts and status.
