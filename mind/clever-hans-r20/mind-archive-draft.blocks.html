The Mind & Perception Archive

Some of the most consequential errors in history began when a person said: I saw it with my own eyes. This reading room follows the evidence when memory, attention, expectation and suggestion become part of the story.

Final Vestige investigates sources before conclusions. Our Mind Archive extends that method from ships, cities and lost machines into the observer. It will publish independent investigations and demonstrate everyday perceptual limits without treating them as diagnoses or proof of any seller's product.

Featured method · a horse that answered questions

What changed when nobody knew the answer?

In a historical test reported by Oskar Pfungst, a horse that apparently counted gave 29 correct responses in 31 trials when a questioner knew the result and three in 31 when none did. The experiment did not expose a cheat. It exposed a route by which the questioner's own body could convey an answer.

The full original investigation and its source trail are complete in this branch, but its public URL does not exist yet. We will not advertise a broken link.

From the same principle come two recurring questions: what did an observer actually see? and what did the observer already expect?

Make the evidence useful

1. Identify the observation

State what someone reported without inferring the mechanism.

2. Find the comparison

Ask what happens when knowledge, visibility or expectation is changed.

3. Keep the failures

Count the wrong responses, unresolved records and missing controls. A claim earns trust when contrary evidence stays visible.
