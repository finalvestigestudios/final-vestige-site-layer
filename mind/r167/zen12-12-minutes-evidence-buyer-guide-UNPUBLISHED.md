## The buyer's real problem

You are not buying a published scientific result. You may be buying convenience: an organized set of audio prompts and a ready-made routine. If you have trouble beginning a practice because you cannot decide what to listen to, a structured library may be appealing. If you already use a timer, quiet room, or free guided audio consistently, the incremental value may be modest. We cannot infer either outcome for you.

The seller says its twelve-minute recordings deliver effects comparable to an hour of deep meditation and suggests broad improvements in stress, creativity and focus. **There is no independently checked product-specific study in our evidence register demonstrating those results for Zen12 Premium.** The statement must not be republished as fact. Research on meditation as a broad activity, auditory entrainment as a category, or inattentional blindness in laboratory scenes does not automatically transfer to the commercial MP3s sold here. ## Who may find the product fit worth evaluating?

Potential fit: a buyer who explicitly wants downloadable audio, values a progression through levels, prefers to choose among four sound styles, and is comfortable evaluating a paid digital download with seller-provided instructions. This is an audience hypothesis, not a clinical indication or evidence of likely satisfaction.

Poor fit: anyone expecting a medical treatment, a guaranteed reduction in anxiety, more intelligence, effortless productivity or a proven substitute for sustained meditation practice. Also a poor fit for someone who can meet their practical need with a free timer, a public-library resource or an existing download.

**A simple demonstration you can do now:** Try one twelve-minute period without buying anything. Sit somewhere safe and comfortable; choose a neutral sound or silence, set a timer and note the instant when you realize your attention has drifted. Return your attention gently to the current task. Afterwards, record only whether the routine felt manageable. This is not a product-equivalence test, therapy or a scientifically validated exercise. It helps define whether your real obstacle is scheduling, format, or motivation.

## Questions before checking out

1. Does the seller's *current* checkout actually identify the Premium package you intended, with the same files and language? Are the formats compatible with your devices?
2. Is the total charge the same after currency, tax, optional upgrades and billing conditions?
3. How does a customer exercise the advertised guarantee? Are exclusions or time limits stated in binding terms?
4. Is the product a one-time download or tied to an account? What happens if you lose access? Are the files and transcripts usable with assistive technology?
5. Would a free seven-day practice answer the question without spending?
6. Is there a reputable, product-specific independent trial supporting any result the seller suggests? If no, separate the creative sales language from scientific certainty.

## What the Invisible Gorilla can and cannot tell you

Simons and Chabris (1999) showed how task-directed attention can leave an unexpected event unreported. Simons (2010) showed that expecting one surprise need not help detect the next. Drew and colleagues (2013) showed a related effect with 24 radiologists performing a specialized search. None of those studies tested Zen12 Premium, any of its four soundtrack styles, buyers, purchasers, or meditation efficacy. [M03][M04][M05]
