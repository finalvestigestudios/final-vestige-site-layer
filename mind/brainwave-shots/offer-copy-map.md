# Focus Shot — actual seller offer / ethical direct-response copy map
**Research date 30 October 2026 | Unpublished | No affiliate authorization established**

## Offer identification, not a generic meditation pitch
The [Focus Shot page](https://brainwaveshots.com/focus-shot) sells downloadable audio sessions rather than a course. The merchant emphasizes fifteen minutes, “laser-sharp focus”, a task-starting ritual, 30-minute alternate version, brainwave-only files and the surrounding mountain/brown-noise soundscape. The merchant's [FAQ](https://brainwaveshots.com/faq) further identifies its method as isochronic tones and says headphones aren't essential. These are its assertions, not independent measurements.

**Reader friction:** an open document and difficulty beginning one clear task. **Reader hope:** a bounded, repeatable practice that suits working life. **Actual merchant format:** MP3 files and a quick start introduction. **Specific hesitation:** will this really affect concentration, what are precautions, and does the advertised refund have conditions?

## The factual product promise we can make
The merchant *offers* 15/30-minute files in different mixes. We cannot promise they improve attention or encode a specific frequency without a file audit, and we cannot state that broad auditory research has proven this product. We have not used or purchased it.

## A real discovery worth foregrounding
The seller's page advertises total safety and fast concentration. Its own FAQ warns against use for people with epilepsy **and while you need to remain awake**. That is a significant unanswered usage conflict for a product marketed for focus during tasks. Ask the seller before promoting such a use case. The FAQ describes one refund per six-month period and limits refunds for some orders above ten purchased products. The one-year promise is therefore qualified. Price and discount banners were dynamic and conflicting between indexed results and current source; omit a dollar amount until checkout refresh on release day.

## Why the research comparison needs precision
A 2024 systematic review (Chee et al., PMID 38458383) analyzed 31 studies covering multiple audio types and found mixed/insufficient conclusions about arousal and cognitive performance. A 2023 binaural-beat review (Ingendoh et al., PMID 37205669) found inconsistent EEG entrainment effects; Basu/Banerjee (PMID 35842538) reported a positive pooled result for selected *binaural* attention/memory studies but stressed conflicting findings. The seller describes *isochronic* tones. The reviewed binaural methods are NOT equivalent to a specific seller's isochronic MP3. Do not imply the positive or negative binaural results directly prove its efficacy or inefficacy.

## Copywriting architecture
1. **An accurate, specific opening:** a task sits open while attention wanders. No made-up founder anecdote or health diagnosis.
2. **A question worth reading:** can a short listening ritual help make starting easier? The headline asks; it does not assert.
3. **Immediate zero-cost value:** original ten-minute task audit with a repeatability note and limits.
4. **Tangible format rather than magical mechanism:** file lengths, mixes, and what the merchant says is included.
5. **Difficult objections answered plainly:** no product-specific evidence, safety and refund caveats, uncertain pricing, alternative strategies.
6. **Autonomy:** a free Fieldbook CTA and no forced subscription, with independent critique accessible without purchase.
7. **Commercial CTA is deliberately disabled:** when authorized, a plainly labeled direct merchant link at end, with adjacent commission disclosure; no fabricated scarcity or visual urgency.
8. **Confirmation after the click:** final approved merchant page must use the exact same product name, specific format and current terms, reducing message mismatch.

## Social/organic and Dispatch boundaries
- TikTok hook: “A stranger was replaced behind a moving door. Why didn't everyone notice?” → original change-blindness article, not checkout.
- Free Fieldbook: “What can ten minutes of attention reveal?” → Focus Fieldbook or the specific Focus Shot evidence guide.
- Buyer-ready reader: “Can a sound make you focus?” → exact product guide, then optional merchant link **only if approved**.

## Value proposition hypothesis vs proven conversion
Concision, a useful free exercise, honest objection handling and an exact merchant match may make the experience more useful. We have **no measured evidence yet** that this copy converts better. First experiment: text-only source guide vs same guide + original opt-in 35-second clip. Compare qualified outbound rates only after account permission, analytics privacy review, real audience and enough traffic. Merchant-reported sales alone establish conversions, not site clicks.
