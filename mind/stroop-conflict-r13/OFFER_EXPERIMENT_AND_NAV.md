## Truthful message match: product-specific funnel, not generic hypnosis

**Exact merchant pages reviewed 30 October 2026:** https://inspire3.com/ ; https://zen12.com/about ; https://zen12.com/getstarted ; https://zen12.com/welcome . Public merchant descriptions are not efficacy evidence. The checkout currently enumerates 12 levels, 48 MP3s, guide and support benefits. The About page describes twelve-minute sessions and four styles (relaxation music, nature sounds, white noise, guided meditation). The seller warns against listening while driving or operating dangerous machinery. The free audio path involves enrollment in the seller newsletter, distinct from Final Vestige's single Jetpack Dispatch. Live price, discounts, refund timelines and special offers are omitted because they are mutable, may be geo/session-specific, and do not form part of verified publisher terms.

**Target reader situation:** Someone who found a true attention demonstration through TikTok or organic search; they want a manageable, unpretentious way to explore meditation, not another overstated scientific promise. Some will want a free method. Others may value a structured collection of short audio sessions. We do not know whether they will prefer guided or unguided sessions; offer features as an option rather than claiming we have researched purchasers.

**Relevant problem:** An everyday focus goal can be clear while distractions compete for attention. Stroop 1935 was a laboratory comparison of conflicting color names, not proof that daily distraction is a medical condition. A short practice routine may be practical for someone interested in noticing attention, without guaranteed improvements. [Original: https://doi.org/10.1037/h0054651]

**Honest scientific context:** A 2021 adult mindfulness synthesis found inconsistent and nonrobust executive-function gains (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34425456/). A broader 2024 meta-analysis of 111 RCTs found small-to-moderate gains in some accuracy outcomes but no generalized latency/processing-speed effect (https://doi.org/10.1080/17437199.2023.2248222). Neither synthesis establishes that Zen12 delivers its advertised accelerated-meditation effect, or changes Stroop performance. A method category is not proof for a specific SKU.

## Original offer-warm-up copy (preview only; no merchant link)

**Above the fold:** 
**The word said RED. The ink said blue. Which did you hear first?**
In a 1935 experiment, people were much slower naming colors when the printed words fought with them. The point wasn't that the mind was broken. It was that attention has competing demands. Before buying anything intended to help you focus, it's worth knowing what a scientific experiment does—and does not—show.

**Bridge:** 
Spend five minutes trying to notice a wandering thought and gently return to what you chose. If you find short guided routines easier to start than silent practice, a pre-recorded audio format may interest you. You don't have to buy audio to practice noticing.

**Offer match:** 
Zen12 is marketed as a twelve-level audio meditation program with twelve-minute sessions and four listening styles for each level. Its seller describes a full package of forty-eight MP3 files, plus supporting materials. Those are format/inclusion statements, not evidence that it causes a deeper neurological state or multiplies meditation time. We have not used or independently tested Zen12.

**Vivid sourced micro-story:** 
Stroop did not ask people to report how powerful they felt. He asked them to name the ink, used comparison conditions, and measured their time. The iconic finding was a 47-second increase for 100 conflict items in the original ink-naming comparison. His careful measurement is the standard a commercial claim needs—not an endorsement of any particular audio product.

**Mini-demonstration:** 
The original no-JS paired color-word exhibit in `wordpress-draft.html` is a low-weight optional reading experience. It has no score, does not persist input, and offers a conceptual equivalent for readers who cannot distinguish the hues.

**Limits and objections:**
- *Does Zen12 improve concentration or performance?* Not established by product-specific independent research reviewed here. The general literature on meditation cannot be directly transferred.
- *Is 12 minutes the same as an hour of meditation?* That is the merchant's assertion, not a verified equivalence.
- *What am I getting?* According to current official product materials: a structured twelve-level MP3 collection with four format choices. Check the exact official offer before purchasing.
- *Why buy if I can meditate free?* You may not need to. A downloadable collection may appeal on convenience and preference, but we have no evidence that it is superior to free practice.

- *Can I try a free audio first?* The seller's 'free' offer includes its own newsletter enrollment; our existing free Focus Fieldbook uses Final Vestige's Jetpack route, and the lists are separate.
- *What about price, guarantees, coupons, refund details or availability?* Reconfirm at checkout on release day; do not quote volatile terms from cached pages or imply publisher approval.

**No-JS / accessibility:** Both variants use server-generated/ordinary HTML with meaningful heading structure; readable at 360/390/768/1440/1920 widths and 200–400% zoom. Native keyboard playback, focus indicator, WCAG contrast, reduced motion and descriptive captions. A no-JS fallback leaves the text guide intact. No video in this R13 editorial fragment is an implicit obligation; PR #229 is the film pilot's source.

**Performance ceiling candidate:** Only ship media if independently measured small enough for mobile. Use `preload=none`; avoid third-party players, remote fonts, analytics scripts and automatic loading of the optional resource. **Avoid a false experiment:** Founder approval, QA, a written measurement policy and sufficient actual traffic are prerequisites. The existing site has no verified high-volume conversion data in Issue #171. Do not publish click-through comparisons as statistical winners with a small sample. Outcomes are qualified guide CTA clicks (still not sales), time-to-useful-content and optionally user feedback; film play should not become an engagement vanity KPI.
