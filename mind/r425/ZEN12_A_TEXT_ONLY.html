What are you actually paying for?

The most persuasive pitch for a meditation recording is also the easiest to overstate: that listening to it is equivalent to hours of practice. The better question is smaller and more useful. Would a structured twelve-minute audio fit how you want to practice?

Five questions before any purchase

1 · Does it fit your routine? Twelve-minute sessions may be convenient; ask whether you prefer spoken guidance, music, silence or self-directed practice. Format fit is personal, not demonstrated efficacy.
2 · Which package? The official page advertises multiple tiers with different level counts. Check the complete list of included recordings and guides against the exact chosen checkout.
3 · What happens if it does not help? A refund statement is a seller policy, not an independently verified refund. Check written conditions, clock start, support contact and payment method at the exact merchant order flow.
4 · What does the evidence measure? Look for product-specific trials rather than references to the general neuroscience of meditation or unrelated attention tasks. Do not confuse EEG changes with improved everyday functioning.
5 · Can you choose a free alternative? Yes. Use a quiet timer, public-library guided resources or Final Vestige's free Fieldbook. Do not buy under pressure or because a striking experiment made you feel something must be wrong with your mind.
