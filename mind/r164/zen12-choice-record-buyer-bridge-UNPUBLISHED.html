Evidence desk / choice blindness / independent buyer decisions

Before you believe the story, look at the choice.

Scientists quietly switched the result of a decision. Some participants explained the answer they had never given. What can that teach us about evaluating a promise made by a commercial meditation recording?

Research-based guide, not an endorsement or a report of first-hand product use. This preview is unpublished.

01 / The evidence

Explanation is not a time machine.

Johansson and colleagues' 2005 study asked people to choose between photographs, substituted an unchosen photograph on some trials, and observed that participants sometimes failed to detect the mismatch while supplying reasons. Later studies adapted the paradigm to jam and tea tastings and questionnaire answers.

These experiments tell us something about error detection and introspective reporting in controlled conditions. They do not establish that a merchant's audio recording will increase focus, alter attitudes, relieve anxiety or produce a clinical benefit.

02 / Try it free

Write the reason before the result.

Choose two harmless options—a book chapter or a background sound—and record which one you chose and why. Come back tomorrow. Before looking at your original note, write what you remember choosing and why. Compare the two notes.

No covert swap occurs here, so this is a record-keeping reflection, not a recreation of choice blindness, a diagnostic test or a treatment.

Who might consider it?

Someone who wants a ready-made, varied set of short listening sessions and values convenience enough to consider paying for access. A reasonable buyer should inspect the current checkout, delivery format, full refund terms, preferred audio style, accessibility of the recordings and whether a free routine meets their needs first.

What we cannot promise

The seller advertises a claim that twelve minutes of Zen12 can supply an hour's meditation benefits and suggests automatic brainwave effects. That is not established for this particular program by the choice-blindness studies, general mindfulness studies, or binaural-beat research. We do not adopt claims of guaranteed concentration, health improvements, wealth, automatic transformation or outcomes from listening.
