The Mind & Perception Archive

History records what people believed they witnessed. Experimental psychology asks how witnessing works. Between them lies a fascinating territory: a mind that can notice, miss, remember, infer and sometimes insist.

No magic tricks passed off as experiments. No commercial promise smuggled into a research conclusion. Follow the original sources, test simple observations for yourself and ask what remains unknown.

Four routes into the archive

01 Perception & attention

The things we never noticed

Inattentional blindness, change blindness, reversible figures and the McGurk illusion all ask a surprising question: what can perception miss or add while we feel certain?

Begin with the published Change Blindness investigation, then explore source-led experiments as they complete independent review.

02 The history of suggestion

The witness and the explanation

Explore mesmerism, Puységur's eighteenth-century accounts, hypnotism and the changing vocabulary of suggestion. A record of an experience is not automatically proof of its proposed cause.

Read Puységur's Animal Magnetism Files.

03 Practice & reflection

Observe without buying

Use short, low-pressure exercises to notice the difference between expectation, recollection and observation. Our original false-memory exercise is an unpublished draft; the practice has not been scientifically validated as a diagnostic test.

The existing Seven Day Focus Fieldbook remains our single free entry point. There is no second email list or duplicate signup.

04 Tools under review

What the product is. What proof exists.

Audio meditation and hypnosis products deserve buying guides that distinguish the seller's description from independent evidence.
