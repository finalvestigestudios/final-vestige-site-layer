Unusual claims. Ordinary methods.

Final Vestige began with documentary questions: who said it, what was recorded, what contradicts it, and which parts remain uncertain? The same method works for perception science. The goal is not to sell a theory of consciousness. It is to make a remarkable experiment understandable without inflating its conclusion.

Our first public bridge is the existing historical Puységur Animal Magnetism Files, with its documentary distinctions and consent warnings. Read the source, then the interpretation.

A good mind-and-perception story has three independent layers. The first is the event or recorded experiment. The second is what a participant or observer thought happened. The third is a proposed explanation. Mixing these layers creates excellent viral video and poor history.

That is why the 2005 choice-blindness face study can make for a compelling thirty-second question without proving that hypnosis, subliminal messaging or brainwave entrainment automatically reprograms choices. The original manuscript explains the card swap, 2010 supermarket replication across sensory modalities, and the ethical questions around misleading a participant.

Every topic should offer a genuine test or primary source. If an outcome depends on special samples, statistical procedures or restricted access, the limitation belongs beside the conclusion.
