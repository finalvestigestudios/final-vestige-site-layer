## Original choice-blindness story to commerce: an ethical fit, not a causal product claim

The 2005 Science face-choice experiment (PMID 16210542) and 2010 Cognition jam-and-tea extension (PMID 20637455) show that people in specific experimental settings sometimes failed to detect switched choice outcomes and offered explanations. These papers did **not** evaluate Zen12 or prove that a retail recording improves memory, focus, decision making or resistance to persuasion. Our editorial takeaway is to preserve a record of a choice and independently inspect product claims. The commercial offer is optional and separated from the factual conclusions.

The presell headline is **“Can twelve minutes change your routine without promising to change your brain?”** The benefit is a clearer product decision, not an invented transformation. The page offers one practical demonstration using a timer and notebook; buyer questions cover product tier, current checkout, formats, refund policies and product-specific science. The seller is named only as the source of its own features. The page says explicitly no recordings were personally tested. An adjacent *prospective* commission disclosure and genuinely disabled button replace a live offer.

## Two-variant experiment, with a valid null result

**Primary hypothesis:** A user-initiated, original, captioned 42-second introduction *may* increase legitimate paid orders per qualified human session compared with the equally useful text-only guide by clarifying what an audio format does and does not promise. This is a test hypothesis, not a guaranteed converter.

- **A control:** independent source-led HTML guide, same first-party links, same claim limits, same future approved offer disclosure and one CTA.
- **B treatment:** exact A buyer copy and CTA, plus optional poster / user-triggered original film of approximately 42 seconds with readable transcript, text captions and a no-JS alternative. **Current B is storyboard/poster/VTT only, not a playable film.**
- **Do not contaminate:** same approved paid SKU, same actual checkout/refund terms, same disclosure, same page depth where practical, same UTM semantics, avoid forcing sound/video, preserve functioning navigation when JavaScript is disabled. Never suppress legitimate warnings in the treatment.
- **Assignment:** After approval, use the *existing* first-party analytics and deployment infrastructure if it can assign consistently without tracking cookies/PII or duplicate scripts. Otherwise do a controlled, declared sequential test and label confounds explicitly, or defer experimentation. Don't pretend HTML prototypes constitute an A/B test.
- **Decision variable:** independently verified **realized net revenue per 1,000 qualified human sessions**, not clicks or session time. Secondary: seller-confirmed paid conversion, confirmed customers, confirmed opt-ins and CTA engagement. 7- and 30-day cohorts; display raw numerator/denominator, test assignment, adverse effects and refunds; warn whenever a cohort has few orders and cannot support a stable rate.
- **Stop rules:** Any misleading claim or missing disclosure; broken keyboard path or responsive layout; unverified merchant eligibility; high refund or complaint signal; unapproved video license; privacy/consent noncompliance.

## First-party, minimal attribution specification (Claude-owned implementation)

Use platform-permitted **UTM source** (e.g. TikTok or YouTube), **medium=organic_social**, **campaign** as stable named hypothesis, and **content** as the **actual verified** Grok asset ID; maintain a static mapping record with no user's name, email address or query string stored unnecessarily. Accept a referral only from an authenticated existing media mapping, not a guessed post title.

**Events are distinct and never interchangeable:**
1. `qualified_human_landing`: one human-eligible first-party landing session, campaign/asset_id/landing-route only after bot/internal filtering and privacy review.
2. `relevant_article_cta`: click on a source-matched next step or guide, not a sale.
3. `focus_optin_confirmed`: count only provider-confirmed subscription if permitted and observable, deduplicate through existing Jetpack (no email address exported to an analytics event).
4. `affiliate_outbound`: actual approved earning-link activation; not a payment. Never place it in HTML until acceptance.
5. `merchant_paid_order`: independently verified completed transaction tied to approved merchant report; if partner reports cannot connect campaign ID, **source attribution remains unknown**.
6. `merchant_commission_settled`, `refund`, and `fees`: reconciled period and currency, not guessed from order or clicks.

**Formula (7/30-day cohort):** `net_realized_revenue_per_1000 = 1000 * (settled_eligible_commission + eligible_owned_product_receipts - confirmed_refunds - attributable_platform_and_processing_fees) / qualified_human_sessions`. Do not divide by total Jetpack pageviews; report **NOT COMPUTABLE** when numerator or denominator is missing. Track whether refunds are already netted from commission to avoid subtracting them twice. Never equate affiliate checkout starts, purchases inferred from redirect, and actual credited commissions.

Data minimization: no individual fingerprints, raw IPs, cross-site identifiers, hashed emails, subscriber identities or unnecessary cookies; no third-party pixels. Prefer existing audited WordPress/Jetpack first-party data, supplemented only with Claude-reviewed minimal event definitions when actually needed and approved. Respect existing consent requirements and retention policies. Always separate human-traffic quality estimates from hard verified transactions.
