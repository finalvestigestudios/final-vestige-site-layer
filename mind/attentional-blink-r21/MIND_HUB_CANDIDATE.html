A bridge to the historical archive

The witness is not the whole record.

When Apollo 1's inward-opening hatch mattered, a material design detail constrained what could happen. When antiquity's overwritten texts resurfaced, an artifact could be examined against an interpretation. In experiments on attention, a control condition can do similar work: it reveals whether the explanation accounts for what was actually measured.

These are different kinds of evidence. The archive's goal is to teach readers to recognize their differences—not to collapse every historical event into a psychological metaphor.

How we review claims

An observation, an interpretation and a sales promise are not the same thing.

A demonstration can show that people sometimes fail to report a visual target. A paper may support a particular explanation of why. A vendor may then say its audio transforms attention. Only the first two statements are linked by the experiment itself. The third needs product-specific evidence before we can responsibly endorse it.

Our editorial and commercial standards remain separate. Investigations carry source trails and corrections. Buyer guides declare what a seller says, what independent research says, and what still cannot be verified. Any future earning link would be disclosed at the point of recommendation.
