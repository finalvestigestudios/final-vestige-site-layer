Field note / The boundary of evidence
One voice can guide your attention. It cannot prove its own promises.

Research about hypnosis in specific clinical settings is not itself a clinical trial of a retail recording.

01 / Start without buying

Before any audio, choose one thing you can observe.

It's easy to want an enormous result: more confidence, better concentration, a different life. Those ambitions are hard to test. A smaller question is often more useful: would a guided practice fit your schedule, and would you return to it often enough to know?

Begin by making one small promise to yourself, not a purchase. Select a task you can start within the next day. Write how long you plan to work, what usually interrupts you, and a concrete sign that the task is finished.

The two-minute preparation

  1. Write a sentence: “For the next twenty minutes, I intend to do ______.”
  2. Put your phone somewhere it cannot interrupt you. Sit comfortably, with the materials you need nearby.
  3. After the session, note whether you began, when attention drifted, and one obstacle to remove next time.

This is a simple planning exercise, not a hypnotic induction, treatment or controlled experiment. A personal log may be useful without proving causation.

Give the routine several tries before deciding that a commercial recording is necessary. You can record your observations in the Seven Day Focus Fieldbook, which is free.

04 / What science does and does not show

Research on hypnosis is not research on this recording.

The U.S. National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health summarizes studies of hypnosis for specific conditions, including some forms of pain, gastrointestinal symptoms and procedure-related anxiety. Results and certainty differ by problem and research design. That overview does not validate an MP3 catalog's particular marketing promises.

A 2023 systematic review classified forty-four studies of hypnosis for sleep disturbance: fewer than half reported unambiguously positive results, with mixed and no-impact categories also represented. Its percentages concern the included studies, not an individual listener's probability of success. The study did not test Total Focus.

A 2026 meta-analysis of mindful hypnotherapy found favorable results for some outcomes across five randomized-trial publications while explicitly calling for more research. Again, that is not evidence of Total Focus's advertised concentration results. Even a good study of a related method does not establish product-specific effectiveness.

Claims about “reprogramming” the mind with NLP, guaranteed behavior change, or extraordinary cognitive performance require stronger, directly relevant evidence than seller copy provides.

06 / The historical connection

There is an older question hiding here.

In the eighteenth century, the Marquis de Puységur recorded encounters that he believed demonstrated artificial somnambulism. The source record is compelling because it contains testimony, failed predictions, and uncomfortable tensions over the operator's influence and the subject's consent—not because it proves a modern product works.
