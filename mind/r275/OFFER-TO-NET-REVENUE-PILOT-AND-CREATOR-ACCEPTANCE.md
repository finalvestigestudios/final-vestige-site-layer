### Highest-intent reader, useful problem and truthful buying question

Audience hypothesis: a reader interested in *establishing a brief daily meditation practice* who wants a structured MP3 library rather than improvising each day. This is a buying-intent hypothesis, not a demographic profile or measured audience. The commercial question is **'Is a twelve-minute guided audio library worth paying for compared with a free timer and written reflection?'** Answer with exact contents, listening requirements, portability, and limitations. Do not turn a rubber-hand illusion, suggestion research, or a general meditation trial into proof that Zen12 works.

Presell value proposition *conditionally* supported by the seller catalog: convenience of a multi-level library containing 48 advertised recordings plus accompanying guides and support. No additional assertions of a tested outcome. Evidence-based discussion may summarize independently reviewed research with accurate limitations; the scientific evidence is not equivalent to product-specific proof.

The user-visible draft should provide a useful free twelve-minute alternative: sit comfortably, choose an ordinary audio-free or timer-based observation practice, record what distracted attention, note one question, and decide whether a packaged recording would make repeating the practice more convenient. The exercise does not measure improvement and is not clinical treatment.
