01 / FROM LAB TO CLAIM

When the room changes, the task can change.

2006 · Controlled evidence

Radvansky and Copeland used virtual rooms and object-memory probes. Crossing spatial regions reduced accessibility of some recently relevant information. This is a specific memory task, not proof that thresholds literally delete intentions.

Read the scholarly record

2021 · An important challenge

McFadyen and colleagues found no general effect in four varied experiments. A narrower interference pattern emerged under working-memory load. The headline needs conditions, not certainty.

Read the open-access replication

Neither study tested a commercial meditation or hypnosis recording. Our complete, source-linked research notes include both supportive and contradictory findings.

02 / DO SOMETHING FREE

The ten-second intention experiment.

Before a harmless errand, name the object and destination: “notebook—desk drawer.” After arriving, note whether you remembered immediately, needed a cue or lost the intention. Repeat only when convenient, and notice competing distractions.

What this can—and cannot—show: You may find a personal reminder habit useful. This is informal self-observation, not a validated treatment, a controlled trial or a diagnostic memory test. Never perform it while driving or doing safety-sensitive tasks.
