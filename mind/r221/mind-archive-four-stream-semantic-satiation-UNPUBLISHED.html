Featured research · manuscript under review

When a Word Stops Being a Word

Repetition can change a person's experience of a familiar word. Research measured relatedness decisions, acoustic repetition and an electrical response associated with semantic relationships. The studies do not show words disappearing from memory or commercial audio creating improved focus.

Cited research: Balota & Black (1997), Pilotti et al. (1997), and the 2017 EEG study. Draft not a published route.
