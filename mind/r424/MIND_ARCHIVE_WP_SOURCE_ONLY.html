The historical source trail

The difference between what happened and why someone thought it happened.

In 1784, a commission including Benjamin Franklin and Antoine Lavoisier examined practices associated with animal magnetism, including controls that probed how expectation changed the reported response. The surviving account concerns experimental conditions; it is not a blanket reconstruction of every later episode of hypnotism.

Puységur's reports are valuable historical documents. They preserve a way people described somnambulism, rapport and treatment before modern experimental psychology. What they do not provide is a controlled demonstration that an invisible magnetic fluid exists. Read them as testimony and archival evidence without transforming their interpretations into a diagnosis or a modern product endorsement.

A useful next step / no purchase

Try the evidence habit before you try an audio product.

Choose one observation. Write down in advance what you intend to find and later write what you did and did not check. In a journal, mark observations, recollections and hypotheses as separate lines. Your record may be incomplete; that is the point of recording your method, not judging yourself.
