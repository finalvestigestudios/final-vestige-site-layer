I / The experimental record

What did you notice?

Change blindness, inattentional blindness, the attentional blink and face inversion show that seeing and reporting are different accomplishments. Experiments reveal defined effects under controlled conditions; they do not tell a visitor their diagnosis, intelligence or hidden power.

III / Fieldwork, not promises

Observe before you interpret.

Start with something the reader can actually do. Record the first observation. Note later suggestions or prompts. Mark uncertain information as uncertain. Then check a source that existed before the discussion.

Two objects. One observation.

Place two familiar objects on a table. Write down their positions from memory before comparing a photograph. Keep the first record. This is not a clinical test; it is a method for understanding the difference between a report and independent evidence.

IV / Editorially separate

Tools under review

A buyer's guide is a different kind of document from an investigation. We verify what the official offer page says; we do not treat that page as evidence that the product works. The relevant question is whether a format suits a reader's preferences, obligations and alternatives.

  • Zen12 — source-checked audio-course guide. Twelve-minute sessions and four seller-described format choices. Stronger time-equivalence and brainwave assertions are unsubstantiated. Unpublished source-only A/B draft; no active purchase route.
  • Hypnosis Live Total Focus — source-checked buyer guide. Seller describes a 33-minute download. Extraordinary concentration and “brain reprogramming” claims are not accepted. Unpublished editorial draft; no active purchase route.
  • Brainwave Shots — research desk. Auditory stimulation categories must not be conflated. Every exact SKU requires its own verified offer and evidence before any recommendation.

V / Read the method

Evidence before persuasion.

What we require

An original research question, primary records where available, a reproducible source trail, explicit limitations and a useful takeaway that does not require spending money.

What we refuse

Fabricated firsthand use, invented customer outcomes, unreviewed health promises, unverifiable urgency, paid product endorsements presented as scientific conclusions, undisclosed earning links, or the implication that an attractive experiment proves a specific MP3 works.
