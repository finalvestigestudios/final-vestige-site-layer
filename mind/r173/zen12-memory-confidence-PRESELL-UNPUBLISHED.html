02 / Evidence, not association

A sharper feeling is not a sharper memory.

In 2024, researchers tested a post-hypnotic memory cue in 24 highly suggestible participants. Recognition judgments became faster and more confident. Accuracy did not significantly improve on that task.

The study did not test Zen12, a meditation library or broad health outcomes. It illustrates why sellers' subjective-benefit language cannot stand in for product-specific evidence.

Claim-to-proof separation

Three questions, three answers.

03 / Useful before you spend

Make a decision that doesn't depend on a miracle.

Three-day free trial—of your own routine

  1. Write a concrete goal: “sit quietly for twelve minutes after work.”
  2. Use a silent timer or free guided session on three ordinary days.
  3. Record whether the routine fit your life and any discomfort; do not score this as a medical or cognitive experiment.
  4. Only then ask whether an organized paid library offers convenience you value.

Questions to verify at checkout

  • Is the final charged amount still the amount advertised, with taxes or other charges?
  • Is the purchase a one-time payment, and what account/download access is included?
  • How do returns work in practice? What exclusions, deadlines or steps apply?
  • Are the recordings, instructions and support accessible on your device?

A private free preference exercise cannot validate brainwave entrainment, treat illness or predict clinical benefit. If you need help with a health condition, do not substitute an audio purchase for professional care.
