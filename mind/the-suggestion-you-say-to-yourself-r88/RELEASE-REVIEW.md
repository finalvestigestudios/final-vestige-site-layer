## Editorial result / why this isn't another generic Zen12 landing page
The 1,899-word investigation **The Suggestion You Say to Yourself** is a new historical examination of Émile Coué's 1922 primary book, early claims of autosuggestion, modern competing theories, Eason and Parris's systematic review of 22 RCTs of *self-hypnosis* (not a named consumer recording), and the fallacy of transferring therapeutic evidence to passive audio offers. Coué's original book is treated as evidence of what was claimed in 1922, **not proof of cures**. It connects transparently to the existing Puységur archive, published Mesmerism, and free Focus Fieldbook. The investigation has no merchant CTAs.

The ~1,090-word exact-offer **Zen12 independent buyer guide** appears in a byte-identical core across Variant A (text-only) and Variant B (same guide plus optional educational film *storyboard/illustration* and visible transcript). This is **not an experiment showing video conversion superiority**; the hypothesis is untested. A distinct problem-to-insight hook contrasts historical self-directed suggestion with today's passive audio without implying a purchased recording reproduces RCT interventions. The buyer gets an original three-occasion no-purchase listening-format comparison, explicit product description, seven objections and a prominent nonendorsement of seller efficacy assertions.
