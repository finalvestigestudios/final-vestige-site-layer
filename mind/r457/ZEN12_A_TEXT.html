What the marketing does not establish

Zen12 marketing includes equivalence between twelve minutes and a much longer meditation session, automatic brain-state changes and broad improvements in focus and wellbeing. We have not located product-specific, suitably controlled evidence that establishes these promises. Research about attention, suggestion or binaural beats cannot be substituted for a trial of this particular product. The FTC calls for reliable substantiation of objective health claims; the fact that a technique has been studied does not prove a specific advertised effect.

A free twelve-minute baseline first

  1. Choose one modest task you can start today.
  2. Record the task and what a completed first step would look like.
  3. Set an ordinary twelve-minute timer. You need no purchased audio.
  4. At the end, note whether you began, what was finished and anything that distracted you.
  5. Repeat across a few days before deciding whether audio would add value for your own preference.

This is a personal planning baseline, not a trial of Zen12 or a medical outcome measure. Six questions to ask before purchasing

  1. Which exact package? Does the checkout name the first six levels, last six levels or the full twelve?
  2. What actually plays? Can you preview the sound and select guided, musical or other available styles comfortably?
  3. What will you measure? A personally recorded preference or habit is not the same as improved clinical cognition.
  4. What is the actual final total? Verify price, currency, taxes, upsells and the seller's current terms at checkout, not in an old post.
  5. How do you request a refund? Read the actual checkout's refund clock, exclusions and support method. A public guarantee is not independently verified fulfillment.
  6. Can you try a free alternative? Silence, a timer, or the Fieldbook's structured exercise may already meet the practical need.
