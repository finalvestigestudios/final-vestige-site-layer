01 / Observe

The missing moment is measurable.

In a classic rapid-stream task, observers first identify a target and then try to report a second item. Raymond and colleagues found a temporary reduction in reporting the second target over a narrow interval after the first. The exact timings and task mattered. The finding is an attentional blink, not a literal closure of the eyes.

Later work revealed an intriguing exception: two targets shown immediately one after the other may both be reported, but their order can become harder to remember. A demonstration that hides this exception would make the science seem simpler than it is.

02 / Examine

What meditation research actually tested.

Slagter and colleagues studied a small group before and after three months of intensive Vipassana meditation, comparing them with a novice group. Retreat participants showed a smaller attentional blink and changes in a task-related EEG signal. This was a laboratory outcome after extensive practice, not a clinical trial of commercial audio.

Where the evidence stops

The study did not test Zen12 Premium. It did not show that 12 minutes of listening equals one hour of meditation, that a product treats a medical condition, or that every buyer will improve attention.
