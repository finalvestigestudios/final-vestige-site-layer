Observation / Orientation / Evidence

The face that looked normal upside down

In 1980 Peter Thompson rotated the eye and mouth regions inside a portrait. The altered face looked strange upright. Upside down, the same alteration often appeared less grotesque.

Inversion changes perceived relations, not the evidence on the page. Read the original 1980 journal record and a 1993 follow-up testing different distortions.

Field exercise / Free and optional

Make one observation falsifiable.

Sketch two simple faces, one with eyes and mouth locally rotated. Record how unusual each looks upright; turn both papers upside down; record again. A simple drawing may show little effect. That is useful evidence about the drawing, not a diagnosis of the observer.

Note the stimulus, viewing time, instructions and exact judgment. Do not equate failing to identify an edit with feeling that a face is “grotesque.” Those are different questions.

The seller describes twelve levels and 48 MP3 recordings in its complete course. The site's assertion that twelve minutes provides the benefits of an hour's advanced meditation is the seller's claim; the studies cited by this archive do not establish that product-specific outcome.
