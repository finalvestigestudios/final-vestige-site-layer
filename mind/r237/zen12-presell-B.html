Is twelve minutes of guided audio worth paying for?

Your mind wanders. The clock is running. A structured recording might help you begin a routine—but the product's format is not proof of what its advertised brainwave effects will do for you.

In 1784 Paris, an investigator noticed that an allegedly magnetized woman reported warmth even when the supposed operator was absent. The sensation was not itself proof of the invisible agent. It is a useful rule for modern product claims too: an enjoyable listening experience, even a sincere feeling of calm, does not prove that a marketed brainwave mechanism produced a particular outcome.

Read the existing published Mesmerism investigation and examine the original commission report. This historical evidence does not endorse any commercial recording.

A free, genuinely useful twelve-minute alternative

  1. Spend two minutes selecting one ordinary task or a comfortable quiet observation—not a medical goal.
  2. Spend eight minutes doing the task or simply noticing interruptions. Do not force yourself to stop all thoughts.
  3. Spend two minutes writing down what you completed, how often you noticed distraction and whether the short routine was workable.
