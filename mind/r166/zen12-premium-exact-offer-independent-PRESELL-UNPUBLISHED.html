Try before you decide

One free minute, with no promised transformation

Read the following sentence, then close your eyes if comfortable: “For the next minute, I will notice when my attention moves.” Sit, listen to surrounding sounds, and when you notice a distraction, name it silently and return to listening. Afterward, write what happened. There is no score, no ideal state, and no reason to judge yourself for noticing many distractions.

This is a simple self-observation exercise, not Zen12, not a clinical intervention, and not proof of an audio product's benefit. Readers wanting a structured free companion can use Final Vestige's existing Fieldbook.

Questions a careful buyer should ask

What remains uncertain?

“Is audio a substitute for practice?”

No evidence reviewed demonstrates that merely listening to this commercial recording is equivalent to a specified duration of active meditation. A meditation meta-analysis cannot be transferred automatically to Zen12's particular files. Mindfulness evidence.

“What about brainwave entrainment?”

Published EEG research has mixed findings across methods; it is not a direct validation of Zen12's audio protocol. Auditory-stimulation systematic review.

“What if I dislike the narration?”

Premium is described as including multiple listening styles. Buyers should independently confirm file accessibility, playback compatibility, sample availability, and whether their preferred style is included.

“What if I change my mind?”

The one-year guarantee is advertised by the seller; redemption details, exclusions, refunds, and merchant responsiveness have not been independently tested. Save the merchant's written terms at the time of any eventual purchase.
