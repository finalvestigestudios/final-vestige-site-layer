Before you buy twelve minutes of meditation

An unexpected event can disappear when you are watching for something else. That does not mean a recording can fix your attention. Here is how to separate the two claims.

Draft only • November 15, 2026 • Research and commercial permissions under review

The second surprise

In the original 1999 inattentional-blindness study, viewers absorbed in a monitoring task sometimes missed an unexpected event. A 2010 follow-up found that people familiar with the gorilla demonstration often spotted the gorilla again while missing other changes in the scene. Knowing the trick did not give them universal awareness.

That is an honest reason to become curious about attention. It is not clinical evidence that any commercial audio improves focus.

What Zen12 actually offers

Zen12's official product page presents a structured meditation-audio program with twelve levels, multiple MP3 recordings and supporting instructions. The seller promotes dramatic benefits, including comparisons between shorter listening and longer meditation. We do not repeat those comparisons as independently verified outcomes.

Its possible value to a buyer is simpler: a ready-made, guided audio structure for someone who prefers to practice with a consistent format rather than build a routine alone. That is a format preference, not a guaranteed psychological result.

Free first

Try twelve minutes without buying anything

Choose one small task. Note your expected distractions. Work for twelve minutes; make a tally when you *notice* a distraction. At the end, record task completion and how often your attention wandered. On another day, repeat under similar conditions. The tallies are personal observations, not a cognitive test: fewer tallies may mean you noticed fewer interruptions.

Five things to check before purchasing

  1. Do you want a multi-level audio library, or would simple quiet practice meet the need?
  2. Are the exact files, instructions and listening requirements clear?
  3. Do you understand the seller's claims as advertising rather than verified product-specific scientific results?
  4. Does the final checkout display acceptable pricing, refund conditions and customer support?
  5. Would you still value the structure if it did not produce a dramatic improvement in focus?

For context, Hypnosis Live Total Focus is a seller-described 33-minute self-hypnosis recording, while Brainwave Shots Focus Shot offers 15- and 30-minute audio variants. These are different offers and cannot inherit results from studies of Zen12 or from generic meditation literature.
