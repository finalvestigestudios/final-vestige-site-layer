## Controlled A/B hypothesis: useful text vs useful text + optional film
**Question:** Among accurately attributed qualified human sessions already showing legitimate audio-buying intent, does an optional, genuinely playable 42s original story add enough independently **verified paid orders or cleared commissions** to outperform a text-only guide *without* decreasing trust, accessibility or performance?

- Eligible random unit: privacy-safe pseudonymous session assignment if and only if consent/local rules permit, else deterministic anonymous campaign split with documented confounding. Preserve equal offer, disclosures, headline, body copy, pricing caveats and CTA position outside the film. Publish neither variant until authorization.
- A: complete source guide, immediately accessible no-JS.
- B: same guide plus user-initiated, light original short film. Current treatment is a poster/transcript **only**. To activate B, produce rights-cleared audio/video, a poster, a plain text transcript, synchronized checked WebVTT and keyboard-operable play control; set `preload="none"` and no autoplay, test captions, 360/390/768/1440/1920 and reduced motion; no hidden external autoplay third party.
- Primary outcome: merchant-verified settled net revenue / 1,000 qualified humans (stratify by device/platform, 7/30-day cohorts). Secondary: confirmed paid order rate; confirmed purchase count; net refunds/fees; confirmed opt-in rate only where Jetpack evidence validates it; source-to-article and buyer guide CTR as *diagnostic*.
- A/B cannot be judged by CTA clicks, affiliate_outbound, download display, or mere view count. Stop for high checkout abandonment, unexpected refund complaints, misleading claims, keyboard or caption failures, disproportionate mobile performance impact. Small samples: report absolute n and uncertainty, do not claim 'winner' or significance from tiny cohorts.
- Expected test status: **not live, 0 legitimately enrolled experiment sessions; paid outcomes NOT COMPUTABLE, not assumed to be zero**. Both CTAs are disabled, matching the available authorization evidence.
