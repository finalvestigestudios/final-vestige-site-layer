Archive investigation / auditory reconstruction

The syllable that was not there.

In 1970, a psychologist deleted a speech sound and replaced it with a cough-like intrusion. Listeners often reported hearing the missing phoneme anyway. A missing waveform is one fact. Hearing a whole word is another. Why did the two disagree?

What the experiment supports: under specific conditions, listeners perceive continuity in edited speech. What it does not support: that every memory can be rewritten, that hypnosis creates false memories in every listener, or that a commercial MP3 changes brain function as advertised.

Warren (1970), DOI 10.1126/science.167.3917.392; Samuel (1981), DOI 10.1037//0096-1523.7.5.1124. Article manuscript in this source-controlled R288 bundle; not yet published.

Try the evidence rule. Choose a sentence whose last word is missing. Write down what you predict it should be, then separate that prediction from what was actually audible. This is an exercise in source annotation, not a reproduction of Warren's laboratory task or a measure of suggestibility.

Questions to ask before choosing any recording

What format and listening time do you actually receive? Does the offer enroll you in the merchant's newsletter? Are returns limited by date, previous claims or downloads? Do the studies test this very product, at its stated dose, in a relevant population? If the offer doesn't answer those questions, do not treat the promotional headline as proof.
