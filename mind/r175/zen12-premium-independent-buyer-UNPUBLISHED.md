## What the research does *not* establish
Zen12 marketing says twelve minutes can deliver the benefits of a much longer meditation session, attributes this to brainwave entrainment, and uses language about automatic relaxation, focus and health. A study of ordinary meditation, perceived auditory phenomena or hypnotic suggestion does not establish an equivalent outcome for these exact MP3s. The primary [2017 ideomotor experiment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5858027/) does not test commercial meditation audio at all. ## A structured-listening use case, not a promised transformation
A buyer may prefer a defined twelve-level library to searching for a fresh recording every week. Four styles offer choice when music is distracting or a voice is preferred. A short fixed session may also be easier to schedule. These are **possible preference and convenience advantages**, not proof of superior attention, stress treatment, brainwave changes or memory improvement.

You might reasonably decline the product if you dislike guided audio, already have recordings you use, want independent medical treatment, expect a scientifically demonstrated one-hour equivalence, or are unsure about the total checkout or refund process. No scientific obligation to purchase follows from an interesting perception experiment.
