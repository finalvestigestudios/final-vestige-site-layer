02 / The evidence boundary

What the research actually measured

Hypnotic-suggestibility studies show that some people respond differently to defined suggestions. Raz et al. compared 16 highly and 16 less suggestible people in a laboratory attention task. The findings concern that task, not Zen12. The 2024 theory review emphasizes unresolved mechanisms. Neither paper tested this MP3 package.

Core distinction. A measurable response to suggestion is not proof that a seller's brainwave recording treats a condition, reproduces hours of meditation, or yields guaranteed focus. A structured library can be a consumer convenience without those claims.

Research: Raz et al. 2002 · Zahedi et al. 2024 · Raturi et al. 2024. Read the full unpublished investigation.

03 / Try before you decide

A free three-session fit check

Set a timer for twelve minutes. Try a quiet pause or free guided recording on three ordinary days. Record only whether you finished, what interrupted you, whether you liked the experience and whether you would choose it again. This measures personal preference and friction, not cognitive improvement or clinical treatment.

04 / Buyer decision

Who might want this—and who should pass?

Potential fit

You already want a regular short listening routine, prefer a graduated set rather than choosing daily, and value several audio styles. These are fit considerations, not proven outcomes.

Reasons to pause

You need a clinical treatment, expect guaranteed brain changes, want verified health improvements, have unresolved accessibility/device questions, or haven't checked current charges and returns.

Is Zen12 the same as hypnosis?

No. Inspire3 markets Zen12 as meditation audio. Hypnosis Live describes downloadable self-hypnosis audios. The science and product features of one do not automatically substantiate the other.
