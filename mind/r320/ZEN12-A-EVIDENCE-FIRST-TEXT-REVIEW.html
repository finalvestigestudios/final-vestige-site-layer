The white-bear problem—and the more useful question

In Daniel Wegner's 1987 laboratory, volunteers tried not to think about a white bear, ringing a bell when the forbidden thought returned. The better-known result was a later rebound; the experiment did not measure any commercial meditation audio. Later meta-analyses distinguished during-suppression effects from after-suppression effects. Rather than asking which download will make your mind silent, ask which routine you're likely to use—and what the product contains.

Read our sourced white-bear investigation (unpublished manuscript) or read the 1987 research abstract.

Try twelve minutes for free first

Set a timer for twelve minutes. Choose a comfortable seat and a neutral sound in your environment. Notice sounds or shifting attention without trying to empty your mind. If you prefer, simply read or take a quiet break instead. Afterward, note whether a timed session felt convenient, irritating, calming or neutral. That observation belongs to you; it is not an efficacy assessment and cannot predict a product's performance.

This optional nonclinical exercise is not intended to manage distressing intrusive thoughts. If it feels uncomfortable, skip it.
