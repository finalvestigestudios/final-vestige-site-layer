# The Gorilla-to-Guide Bridge: Editorial Conversion Blueprint
*For draft use only — no live affiliate link or publishing authorization*

## Decision

**Keep The Thing in the Middle of the Room noncommercial.** A phenomenon as memorable as inattentional blindness invites a hard-sell headline: “This tool will open your eyes.” We cannot justify that promise. The Simons/Chabris (1999), Most et al. (2005), Simons (2010) and Drew et al. (2013) studies never measured any commercial meditation, hypnosis or brainwave-audio product. A CTA asserting that audio prevents inattentional blindness would convert science into a false implication.

The strongest offer bridge is the reader's practical question: *What do I do when my attention wanders?* The reader has learned that selective attention is a feature of goal-directed perception, not proof of personal failure. We can offer a free task-starting routine. If a reader wants to evaluate a short-form meditation recording, we can separately explain the actual offering, the limitations of evidence, and purchase considerations. Our earnings relationship, if any, is disclosed at the decision point.

### Message architecture

**TikTok hook** — “The gorilla wasn't invisible. The task was selective.” Do not claim all trained experts are blind; describe the 2013 20-of-24 finding with its artificial target caveat. A curiosity gap should invite reading, not withhold a public-safety warning or imply a medical diagnosis.

**Editorial landing** — Original 2,511-word feature, primary-paper links and a no-JS text plate. At each source claim, separate what was actually measured from the analogy. The current user-visible CTA is the live 52-page Focus Fieldbook (free), not a checkout.

**Reader's voluntary next step** — A 12-minute beginner's routine may be attractive because it is bounded and repeatable; neither this article nor the fieldbook establishes that it changes gorilla detection. No email gate in front of the article. Existing Fieldbook signup and Jetpack Dispatch remain the only first-party opt-in journey.

**Independent Zen12 evaluation** — Draft PR #180 is not published. After it undergoes scientific, accessibility and partner claims review, a *separate* buyer's guide may describe 12 levels, 4 listening styles and short sessions. The product's format is a seller statement. The seller's “one hour in twelve minutes” and automatic brainwave/meditation effects are not established by the gorilla studies. We should omit those claims or unambiguously mark them as unsupported vendor assertions, never use them to pre-sell an outcome.

**Approved earning link** — Only after specific partner-account acceptance and link format/channel permission are verified. Place in immediate visual proximity to the actual CTA: “If you buy through this link, Final Vestige may earn a commission at no additional cost to you.” Do not hide the disclosure in a footer. A general Affiliate Disclosure page complements, but does not replace, near-link disclosure.

Don't present this as an unconditional free trial that creates no additional marketing subscriptions. It also advertises an equivalence between 12 minutes of audio and one hour of meditation. That is a **seller claim**, not an established research finding. The offer's “free” framing must be evaluated alongside explicit opt-in and current terms.

**Current terms unknown:** Pricing, order bumps, refunds, commercial rights to seller's images, checkout flows and all email-promotion terms are not verified for our account. Merchant promotional emails or public affiliate invitation text are not account-specific evidence of acceptance. The claim ledger marks both earnings link and approval null/false.

### Who the future buyer guide could serve

A person who finds a short, consistent routine more approachable than an hour-long meditation session may find the format worth investigating. The product guide can ask how often they expect to listen; whether nature sound, music, guided or ambient material is preferable; whether the audio format works on their device; and what the seller currently promises if they dislike it. The article cannot supply truthful answers about actual effectiveness. Product information should be checked directly on the live merchant offer and updated when it changes.

A reader with medical concerns should not be pushed into a retail audio purchase based on this cognitive-perception story. The guide is informational and does not replace professional assessment or care.

### Objections that deserve honest answers

**“Does it improve my attention?”** The gorilla studies do not test it. Separate meditation research may speak to some attentional outcomes under different conditions, but that is not proof of this product's specific effect.

**“Are those sessions really an hour of meditation in twelve minutes?”** The seller says so; we have not substantiated a comparable benefit or even established a single meaningful equivalent measure across practices. Do not inherit the headline.

**“Have you tried Zen12?”** No; no full-product hands-on review has been completed by Final Vestige. Don't imply user experience or independently measured outcomes.

**“Can I just use your free resource?”** Yes. The Focus Fieldbook is a real existing, independently accessible choice. Requiring the paid product to get value from this article would undermine the editorial promise.

**“What if I don't want another newsletter?”** The seller's free-audio opt-in includes their newsletter. We should disclose that if we ever mention the sample, and distinguish it from Final Vestige's existing Dispatch opt-in.

**“Why is Final Vestige recommending it?”** We can eventually explain documented format/fit, limitations and any affiliate arrangement. We cannot claim the seller paid for or controlled the science; any commission relationship must be disclosed.

## Measurement and ethical testing

**Control A — article only:** qualifying first-party visitors to the new investigation, engaged page visits where permitted, free Focus CTA clicks, book downloads and newsletter confirmation only if those aggregate counts are available and permitted. No raw full referrer URLs or personally identifiable data, and never infer new subscriber consent.

**Control B — article + optional original film:** compare the *same* article with optional 42-second click-to-play editorial video and full transcript. It must not autoplay, carry paid product claims, or lock content behind playback. Because traffic is initially small, use descriptive directional comparisons only rather than claiming statistically robust lift. Stage the film with captions, accessible controls, verified rights and proper file caching.

**Later separate offer test:** only after the Zen12 guide is actually published, the partner is approved and the proper tracking link is confirmed. Readers may choose the independent guide; the guide may then provide the disclosed earning link. Measure unique qualified guide clicks, aggregate merchant outbound events and actual merchant-verified conversions. Click ≠ sale. Do not import purchase or subscriber identities into WordPress.

**Avoid:** UTM strings containing person identifiers; third-party pixels; localStorage IDs; session fingerprinting; raw referrer URLs; fabricated ROI; link cloaking violating merchant terms; cross-domain matching of identities without transparent lawful basis.

The actual experimental comparison must preserve article copy and campaign channel as much as possible, avoid simultaneous new paid promotions and require enough observations for meaningful conclusions. If the sample is only a handful of readers, report uncertainty rather than declaring a winner.
