Zen12: What are you buying—and what has actually been tested?

You do not need a promise about brainwaves to decide whether a twelve-minute recording belongs in your routine. Start with format, evidence and the exact terms at checkout.

This is an independent evidence and offer-terms guide, not a personal product test or endorsement.

Start with a problem you recognize

You set aside a quiet moment, but your attention drifts almost immediately. Perhaps music would help you enjoy the practice; perhaps a narrator would be easier to follow; perhaps you dislike both. A buyer benefits from knowing that preference before committing money.

Final Vestige's change-blindness investigation illustrates how limited attention can be in a specific visual task. Its findings do not establish that any purchased recording corrects attention or memory. The boundary-extension research in our unpublished Mind investigation adds a similar warning about interpreting subjective experience as product efficacy.

Try a no-cost twelve-minute comparison first

In a safe, stationary setting, choose silence or a freely available neutral sound. Set a twelve-minute timer. Notice whether the format is comfortable; if your attention wanders, return to the sound or breathing without scoring yourself. At the end, record preference and usability, not a medical or cognitive outcome.

Six questions worth answering before checkout

  1. What is the precise package? Full 12-level course, limited starter audio, or a separate bundle?
  2. Which formats are included? Guided instructions, music, natural sounds, white noise—verify against the exact SKU.
  3. What will you actually pay? Confirm total price, currency, tax, renewal terms and any checkout upsells.
  4. How does delivery work? Are the files downloadable, playable offline, and accessible on your device?
  5. What are the precise refund and cancellation terms? Do not substitute a general marketing statement for transaction-specific terms.
  6. What independent evidence supports the claimed benefit? Ask whether a study evaluated the sold product and the exact claimed outcome, not merely meditation in general.
