## A vs B experimental logic
Control A: exactly the same independent buyer's guide and disabled callout, without film. B: identical substantive guide core, with contextual original 40-second optional film poster and transcript. Only once the original film is hosted and verified can a consented experiment compare variants. Hypothesis: some visitors may comprehend a key distinction better after watching the film; no benefit is established.
- Primary metric *once legally and technically approved*: percentage of eligible on-page sessions choosing to open authorized offer (qualified intent), NOT sales or treatment outcomes. Consent and platform terms control what is collectible.
- Secondary: educational source link clicks, transcript expansions and optional verified merchant reports; avoid session fingerprints, third-party pixels, user IDs, persistent identifiers and query-string PII.
- Pre-register minimum credible sample size and stopping rule with independent QA before claiming any lift. Do not report significance from a small noisy sample. Attribution may be aggregated by campaign/topic and coarse source, never by person.
- Performance budget: zero third-party video JS; lightweight first-party SVG poster, lazy visual loading, CSS-only layout, native video controls if/when hosted, muted/no autoplay, VTT and transcript, respect prefers-reduced-motion, no JS dependency for reading.
- Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA review; keyboard/source links visible; semantic article and heading hierarchy; alt text; transcript visible via native details/summary; 360/390/768/1440/1920 target widths; inspect real browsers before publication.

## Privacy-hard-off state
No outgoing telemetry of any kind exists in this branch. The optional local attribution module defaults disabled and rejects any identifiers. Its test helper accepts only categories [variant, source, topic, action] and returns a local grouped count only when at least ten synthetic events exist. No cookies, localStorage, fetch, beacon, analytics tags, IP capture, timestamps or unique event IDs. Any future deployment requires explicit legal/consent and founder review; this prototype is not a recommendation to bypass privacy law.
