## Actual product, offer and buyer

**Candidate:** Zen12 structured meditation-audio program. **Buyer:** an adult who is *already* comparing short guided or sound-based meditation courses, wants an MP3 library with choices, and is deciding whether a paid structured program is more suitable than a free practice. **Not the buyer:** somebody watching the McGurk effect, Thatcher illusion or invisible-gorilla experiment because they enjoy science. Fascination with perception is not proof of purchase intent.

The https://zen12.com/hnp seller page describes:
- Twelve levels, generally presented as a progression across a year;
- Twelve-minute listening sessions, four presentation styles per level (relaxation music, guided meditation, nature sounds and white noise);
- Premium with 48 MP3s, guides and seller-described support and bonuses;
- Beginner and Advanced configurations different from Premium, with 24 recordings each in the examined presentation;
- A displayed Premium promotional price of **$87.99** as observed Nov 4, 2026, not a guaranteed checkout price;
- A seller-described one-year money-back guarantee, with precise application requiring review of official terms at checkout.

A second official page, https://zen12.com/secret/, displays a conditional **$37** offer with an on-page coupon code. It uses high-pressure benefit headlines and a countdown-like presentation. We must not reproduce the timer, claim that a random FV visitor is eligible, paste the coupon or imply this is the same bundle, price or route as `/hnp`. The buyer guide should direct an *authorized* purchaser to one merchant-approved URL and ask them to verify its live checkout price, included package, tax, refund handling and order confirmation themselves. As a control, keep numeric promotional prices in the dated evidence ledger, **not prominent presell CTA copy**.

**Benefits and limits:** the seller says proprietary sounds can induce meditation and bring benefits equivalent to longer training. The scientific evidence assessed does not validate those Zen12-specific equivalence, neurological, clinical, cognitive or wealth outcomes. A meta-analysis of 22 studies of binaural beats (Garcia-Argibay et al., published online 2018/in a 2019 issue; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/) found a pooled effect across distinct tasks and outcomes. A 2023 systematic EEG review of 14 studies (Ingendoh et al., https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37205669/) found five results in line with entrainment, eight contradictory and one mixed, with substantial method heterogeneity. Neither study evaluates the exact Zen12 package. Mindfulness interventions and proprietary binaural-audio courses are **not interchangeable treatment classes**.

FTC guidance: disclose material affiliate relationships near each earning recommendation (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking); do not repeat unsubstantiated product-specific benefits as FV's own claims (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance). No personal testimonials unless real, permissioned and accurate. No clinical diagnoses or wealth improvement promises.

## The high-intent presell, deliberately disabled until permission

**Problem-to-insight opening:** "A twelve-minute session can fit your schedule. But twelve minutes of audio isn't automatically twelve minutes of a studied meditation intervention. Here's what the Zen12 package contains, what remains unproven, and what to check before paying."

**Original sourced micro-story:** The 1976 McGurk experiment showed that visual mouth movements can change what listeners report hearing from identical audio. That finding is about audiovisual perception, not meditation, and it is not offered as proof of Zen12's mechanism. It supplies an ethical analogy: the sensation of an experience can differ from the explanation supplied by an advertisement. Cite https://doi.org/10.1038/264746a0 beside the story. Do not pretend the reader has experienced the illusion.

**Useful free demonstration:** Before purchasing, do twelve minutes of quiet, comfortable listening using ordinary non-proprietary audio, an unembellished ambient sound or silence. Write down which environment felt tolerable; do not use scores as health diagnoses, and do not promise improved concentration. Repeat on another day if desired, not as a blinded scientific trial. Ask whether choices and convenience matter enough to pay for a prepared library.

**Offer value, without hype:** The independent value proposition is convenience, session duration, structured levels and four available formats, as described by the seller. Paid audio is optional; it does not replace clinical care or prove better results than free exercises. We do not claim to have used the product.

**Objections honestly answered:**
1. Does it replace a full course of meditation? No demonstrated product-specific equivalence.
2. Will it treat anxiety, depression or ADHD? No supported product-specific treatment claim; never imply a clinical effect.
3. Is it twelve minutes for all sessions? The merchant describes the core session format; confirm contents of the selected live package.
4. Is $37 my price? Not verified; the alternative offer is conditional and may not apply.
5. Is there a refund? The seller describes one, but the purchaser must check the applicable live checkout terms and contact procedure.
6. Will FV know what I purchased? The seller's privacy policy says it **may** share personal details with affiliates. FV will not activate without approved handling.
7. Can I skip spending money? Yes. Read our free Focus Fieldbook and keep all investigations accessible.

**Disclosure for future earning CTA**, to display immediately adjacent to the link *once independently approved*: "If you buy through this link, Final Vestige may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. We haven't personally tested Zen12; product details come from the seller, and the claimed health or performance results have not been verified for this program." Until approval, show **"Merchant recommendation unavailable — authorization and buyer protections under review."** A truly disabled `<button disabled>` prevents phantom clicks; do not place a clickable product URL in href, JS, data attributes or a hidden layout.

The active alternative is the *existing* Focus Fieldbook page at https://finalvestige.com/focus/. The purchasing guide must not insert a second form, send email, reconfigure Jetpack or duplicate the list. The single signup flow is owned by the existing site team.

## Controlled conversion experiment (PRE-REGISTERED; NOT LAUNCHED)

**A:** identical source-reviewed 900–1400-word buyer guide, without video.
**B:** the same buyer guide, plus one **optional original 40-second** video with poster, captions, transcript and no autoplay. A viewer can skip it. On mobile no third-party embed, loading below first screen, explicit user gesture, reduced-motion friendly, keyboard reachable, transcript useful without JS. Videos must be independently rights-cleared and locally optimized; the current version is only storyboard/prototype, not a playable clip.

**Hypothesis:** a succinct optional audiovisual explanation may improve *paid orders per qualified visitor*, not just dwell time or affiliate clicks. This hypothesis has no established uplift.

**Experiment eligibility gate:** written affiliate acceptance + merchant-approved earning URL + verified privacy/reporting + current checkout/returns + first-party analytics approval + creative rights + non-production browser QA + founder release. Otherwise, both variants remain offline.

**Randomization:** assign a first-party, consent-compatible anonymous variant per eligible session, stable for the experiment window, with mutually exclusive A/B assignment and no user profile; no new paid SaaS. If consent/config does not permit accurate randomization, use a transparent sequential observation **without calling it an A/B causal experiment**. Avoid pre-selecting high-intent visitors for B.

**Primary metric:** finalized (settled, attributable, non-refunded) **net realized revenue per 1,000 qualified human sessions** over 7 and 30-day cohorts. Definitions: revenue in smallest monetary units; net = gross commission or direct receipt minus refunds, seller fees, chargebacks and applicable payment costs. Paid conversion rate = verified paid purchasers / eligible qualified sessions. Lead-to-customer rate = verified paid customers / independently confirmed leads when the join is lawful and technically demonstrable.

**Event contract:** `campaign_landing` (human-qualified, consent and bot caveats), `article_to_resource_click` (click, not subscription), `confirmed_signup` (only after explicit confirmation event), `affiliate_outbound` (click, not sale), `verified_checkout_start` (only independently observable), `merchant_confirmed_paid_conversion` (authoritative partner/merchant reconciliation), `refund_or_chargeback`, `net_settlement`. Dedupe events by anonymous session and merchant transaction identifiers stored only as keyed or hashed opaque values; never ingest email, name, phone, postal address, IP or full URL query fragments into a public export.

**Stop conditions:** missing consent, tracking mismatch, unverified merchant approval, broken CTA, any personally identifiable affiliate report fields, stale price/disclaimer, unreadable disclosure at 360px or 390px, broken keyboard focus, unacceptable CWV regressions, non-working PDF/email confirmation, and any evidence of shared false claims.

**Do not report:** zero as a substitute for unknown, inflated session count from bot/pageview spikes, click as purchase, subscriber as customer, a marketing email as transaction, or a promoter's public EPC as Final Vestige realized earnings. Tiny samples require uncertainty warnings; without authoritatively joined sessions and paid orders the primary metric is **NOT COMPUTABLE**.
