Independent offer guide · research before recommendation

What are you actually buying with twelve minutes?

A day full of interruptions makes the promise of effortless focus appealing. But before buying a meditation recording, compare the contents with what research can actually show. This is an independent editorial guide, not a first-person listening review. Final Vestige has not used or clinically tested this product.

The surprise hidden in an attention experiment

In a 2011 study, people remembered one or six digits while deciding which of two lines was longer. A single unexpected stimulus appeared. In that specific setup, holding six digits improved detection. The result does not mean multitasking always helps or that branded recordings improve working memory. It means a neurological-sounding promise deserves an equally specific test. Check the published study abstract.

A free, useful first step

  1. Set a timer for twelve minutes and choose one ordinary task.
  2. Record your intended result on paper before you start.
  3. Work normally, marking each occasion when you become aware of drifting.
  4. At the end, note what you completed. Repeat on another day if useful; do not claim an effect from a single session.
