An experiment · Johansson et al., 2005

The face you did not choose

Researchers covertly swapped which face a participant was shown after selecting between two portraits. Some people failed to catch the mismatch and explained the photograph presented to them. The result concerned a specific controlled decision task: it does not show that every consumer is easily deceived.

Read the university's primary-study record. Final Vestige's new full-length investigation is in draft and intentionally has no broken public link.

A convincing reason is not the same thing as a verified record.

Try the decision receipt

Not a laboratory test: an everyday record-keeping habit.

  1. Choose between two safe, free alternatives.
  2. Before acting, write down your choice and two concrete reasons.
  3. Tomorrow, explain your choice again without looking at the note.
  4. Compare your accounts. New reasons can reflect learning—not necessarily cognitive bias.

Private, optional, offline. No health data or signup required.
