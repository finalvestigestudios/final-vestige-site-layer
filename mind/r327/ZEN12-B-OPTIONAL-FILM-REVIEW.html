A decision, not a promise

A quieter twelve minutes is worth testing. A miracle claim is not.

There is a frustrating moment in the attentional-blink experiment: you know that the second letter was shown, yet you cannot confidently report it. It tempts us to want a tool that fixes attention on demand. The original experiments and later training research offer a more interesting lesson: attention depends on the task, timing and training conditions. They cannot establish how a commercial audio course will work for you.

Zen12 is one optional way to put a regular audio-listening routine on the calendar. Before weighing a purchase, try the free exercise and examine exactly what the seller offers—and what remains unproven.

Useful before checkout

Try twelve minutes without buying anything

  1. 0–2 minutes: Choose a safe, quiet place and write the intention to notice your attention without judging it.
  2. 2–6: Sit comfortably in silence or with ordinary ambient sound. If you become distracted, simply notice and return.
  3. 6–10: Read a short paragraph, look away, and jot down what you remember. Check against the text. Do not interpret the result as a brain test.
  4. 10–12: Note what interrupted you and whether the routine felt worthwhile. Keep the observation separate from any claims of measurable cognitive change.
