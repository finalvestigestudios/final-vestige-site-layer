What if trying harder becomes the distraction?

You sit down to focus. An unwanted thought arrives. You tell yourself not to think about it; soon you are monitoring whether it returned. The monitoring is a second task.

Wegner's 1987 white-bear experiment found a later rebound in thought reports after a suppression condition. A 2020 meta-analysis of 31 studies found rebound was more consistent than an immediate increase, which emerged under additional cognitive load. Neither source tested Zen12.

Try a free, low-pressure twelve-minute observation

Take a neutral task. Do it for four minutes. Spend four more noticing ordinary distraction without trying to force it away; calmly return to the task. Spend the final four recording what you did, what you felt and what you simply assumed. Stop if uncomfortable. This is not a therapy or a replication of the laboratory protocol.
