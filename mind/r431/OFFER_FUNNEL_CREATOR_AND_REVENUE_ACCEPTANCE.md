## Buyer pilot variants: A vs B
- **A text-only:** Original seller-specific buyer guide, comparison of actual catalog tiers, five buyer objections, no deceptive urgency, free practice, claim limits, seller-source refs, clear prospective disclosure, and a **non-clickable** merchant hold status.
- **B hybrid:** Exactly the same substantive buyer guide plus a 42-second original six-scene storyboard, SVG poster, synchronized conceptual VTT cues and visible no-JS transcript. Not a working video until a rights-cleared, locally hosted media file is encoded, rendered and validated. No forced playback, no remote embed, no tracking pixel and no broken empty media player.
- Hypothesis *only*: B's short evidence-first demonstration might reduce confusion about the nature of the product and improve **verified paid orders per qualified session** over A. It might also distract, impair mobile performance or produce no difference. Do not present as a known improvement.
- Predeclare 50/50 assignment by first-party, consent-compatible method only if Claude identifies a compliant existing facility; otherwise conduct controlled non-personal QA and **do not call it a randomized sales test**. Prevent cross-variant cookie/fingerprint tracking. Keep article, CTA copy and seller terms identical across versions.
- **Primary metric:** realized net revenue per 1,000 qualified-human campaign sessions at 7/30 days. **Secondary:** independently paid orders/eligible sessions, refunded fraction, verified lead-to-customer conversion if source attribution is supported. **Diagnostics only:** viewport visits, article-to-Fieldbook clicks, affiliate_outbound events. **Never** equate these with purchase.
- If no authorized purchase link exists, **do not attempt the A/B test**. In that state, only assess editorial comprehension, layout, caption readiness and safety.

## First-party measurement contract (design specification, not instrumentation deployment)
Claude owns implementation and existing analytics integration. Do not create a second script or paid SaaS. The only lawful campaign identifiers are disclosed, source-owned, non-personal labels:
`utm_source=(youtube|tiktok|instagram|...actual verified platform)`;
`utm_medium=organic_social`;
`utm_campaign=mind_pilot_2026_11`;
`utm_content=<verified immutable asset_id>`.
All inbound URLs must be normal first-party article destinations and honor each platform's link limitations. These identifiers are not evidence that traffic was human or that Grok actually posted the asset.

State machine with separate audit facts:
`campaign_landing` (qualified-human status only if appropriately established) → `article_to_fieldbook_click` (diagnostic) → `confirmed_signup` (Jetpack/provider-confirmed, not form-submitted) → `buyer_guide_view` → `affiliate_outbound` (only after approved link) → `merchant_confirmed_paid_order` / `independently_reported_paid_affiliate_conversion` → refund/fee reconciliation → `realized_net_revenue`.

Minimum non-PII fields: campaign/source/content, first-party destination slug, variant A/B where lawful, timestamp rounded to date for cohort aggregate, event type, verified merchant non-customer-specific settlement reference maintained in restricted owner system, value/currency/net adjustment and confidence tier. **Do not store raw IP, full user agent, device fingerprint, customer email, name, payment fields, order IDs or coupon leakage in GitHub.** Need consent assessment and data-retention policy before instrumentation.

A qualified-human session must have an explicit human/bot/internal traffic classification definition; a single view is not a unique person. The reported KPI is:
`1000 * SUM(verified paid merchant revenue - verifiable refunds - attributable fees) / COUNT(qualified-human sessions)`.
If numerator or denominator cannot be independently reconciled, report **NOT COMPUTABLE**, not $0. Never convert a $0 click ledger into a sales claim. Keep uncertainty intervals and severe small-sample caveat for 7/30-day cohorts; don't publish a "winner" from tiny counts.
