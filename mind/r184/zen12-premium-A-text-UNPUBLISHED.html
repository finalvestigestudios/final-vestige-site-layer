Can twelve minutes fit your mind better than a promise?

The attentional blink reveals a limit of rapid attention. It does not establish that a commercial recording removes that limit. Here is the useful information a prospective buyer can check.

A genuine discovery. A different product.

In 1992, researchers reported difficulty identifying a second target in a rapid stream of visual events. In 2007, a group receiving three months of intensive meditation training showed changes in the attentional blink and an associated scalp-recorded signal. Neither experiment tested Zen12. 1992 study · 2007 study.

Claims that twelve minutes of a particular audio program equals an hour of deep meditation, alters your brainwaves in a beneficial way or treats stress require independent product-specific evidence. We have not established that evidence.

A free three-session decision test

  1. Try twelve minutes with silence. Record how easy it was to start and whether you would repeat the routine.
  2. Try twelve minutes with free or already-owned sound. Record the same two observations.
  3. Choose your preferred format. Ask whether what you need is more audio—or a usable time and place.

This is a personal preference exercise, not neuroscience research, treatment, a validated attention test, or evidence of benefit.
