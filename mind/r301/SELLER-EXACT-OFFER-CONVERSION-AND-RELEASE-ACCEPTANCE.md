## High-intent buyer and one conversion hypothesis

**Chosen potential buyer:** an adult already looking for a multi-level, downloadable meditation-audio library, who values a predictable schedule and is comparing an organized recording collection against free structured practice. This is an audience-intent hypothesis, not evidence that any identified visitor has bought such material.

**Copywriting promise we can defend:** *“Before you buy a meditation recording, find out exactly what you receive—and which benefits have actually been tested.”*

Problem: marketing about remarkable mental results can obscure simple product questions. Insight: inventory and effectiveness are distinct. Useful demonstration: the existing free twelve-minute activity/start-and-record exercise; explicitly **not** a treatment or an attentional-blink study. Offer value: the seller **describes** a 12-level/48-track collection that may be preferable to someone who wants many styles and an organized library. Objections: paid files are not necessary for reflection; not all listening experiences are enjoyable; guarantee, access, support and taxes must be checked with the merchant. No promise of immediate relief, guaranteed focus, disease treatment, earnings, or purchase satisfaction.

**Prototype CTA state (currently disabled):** “Merchant purchasing link unavailable.” Accompany it immediately with: “If you eventually purchase through a qualifying link on Final Vestige, Final Vestige may earn a commission at no additional cost to you. No affiliate purchasing link is available here; publisher approval and tracking permissions have not been confirmed.” This preserves transparent intent without masquerading as an approved link. An eventual active outbound link requires a new verified, account-issued merchant destination, on-page adjacent disclosure, release review and link QA. Do not leak it into article text, form actions, social descriptions or redirect scripts.

Do **not** treat storyboard markup as a film conversion test. In the eventual staged experiment, keep headline, claims, offer, disclosure, mobile CTA placement, and selection criteria the same. The sole designed difference is the optional 30–45-second original video: self-hosted, never autoplaying, user-initiated controls, source-cleared artwork, still poster, captions and transcript, no JS needed to read or activate the page. Test with video blocked, captions enabled, reduced-motion settings, keyboard-only navigation, screen-reader controls and 360/390/768/1440/1920 layouts. Measure byte weight and LCP change on mobile; do not add third-party player scripts or paid tracking.

**Hypothesis (not result):** the film may clarify the buyer's distinction between product contents and efficacy, and could improve *verified paid conversion* without misdirecting traffic. Stop if it degrades trust, mobile performance, accessibility, disclosures or net value. No winner can be declared from preferences, pageviews, watch time or affiliate clicks.
