02 / Original investigation in editorial review

The word that fought the color.

In 1935, J. Ridley Stroop found an asymmetry between naming the colors of contradictory printed words and reading those words. The result became a research field, not a self-diagnosis or a promise that audio can “rewire” attention.

BLUE

The ink is red; the letters spell BLUE. Try saying the ink color rather than reading the word. The difference between noticing competition and measuring it is why experiments need comparisons.

Illustration only · No score · No script, flashing or timed response · Accessible description included

Science is not a sales claim. Stroop interference does not demonstrate that hypnosis, brainwave audio or any commercial meditation program improves concentration. The 2,486-word research manuscript is source-controlled, unpublished and not a public social landing route.
