Before you buy twelve minutes of meditation

You want a smaller, more manageable way to begin focusing. That's a reasonable goal. But an audio track's comforting experience, its advertised neuroscience and an independently proven improvement are three different things.

In a 2002 word-memory experiment, hypnotized and nonhypnotized groups confidently recognized words that had not been presented. Hypnosis neither improved accurate memory nor increased false recognition over the comparison group in that study. This is an example of why confidence and proof must be separated—not evidence that any particular meditation audio helps or hurts attention. Original abstract.

A free twelve-minute baseline

Write one small next action and one prediction about distraction. Work for eight minutes without new audio. Make a dot when you notice your attention drift. Spend the last two minutes writing what you observed and what you merely inferred. This is reflective practice, not a medical intervention or clinical test.
