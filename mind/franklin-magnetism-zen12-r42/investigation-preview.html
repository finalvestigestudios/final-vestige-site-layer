The Tree That Had Never Been Magnetized

Franklin's orchard, a blindfold, and the problem of believing an effect explains its cause.

Editorial note: The full sourced manuscript is in investigation.md. This HTML preview carries the introductory and evidence trail sections; import the complete manuscript only after editorial copyfit and WordPress staging QA.

In the garden of a house at Passy, outside Paris, a physician prepared an apricot tree. A boy of about twelve waited indoors. He was supposed to be unusually receptive to an invisible force.

The boy was then led blindfolded to four other trees. At the fourth he fainted. None of those four was the tree the physician had prepared. The commission did not deny that something happened; it asked whether the proposed magnetic cause had been established.
