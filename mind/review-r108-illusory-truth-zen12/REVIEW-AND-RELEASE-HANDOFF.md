## Conversion hypothesis and measurement design

**Question:** For visitors arriving from an accurately labeled mind/perception article or a TikTok explainer, does a truly optional, original 40s video help them understand what Zen12 is—and what is not established—compared with an otherwise identical text-only guide?

Hypothesis, *not observed result*: a short visual clarification could improve qualified engagement or informed offer intent. It could also distract, increase page weight or reduce comprehension. Version A is the baseline and is complete without JavaScript. Version B **currently offers poster and transcript only**; test the actual film *only after* a verified same-origin MP4, reviewed VTT timing and WCAG/browser performance evidence. A live experiment is **not authorized**.

Design rules after release approval: same article copy, headings, disclosure, disabled→approved CTA language, placement and internal Focus path in A and B; only original optional video can differ. Do not identify people or set tracking cookies to assign groups. Any planned assignment design must be explicitly reviewed for comparability and consent; self-selected clicks on two links do not produce a randomized A/B experiment. Do not state conversion uplift without valid exposure denominators and statistical uncertainty. Never treat outgoing clicks as purchases.

**No real visitor measurement exists in this PR.** `synthetic-attribution.cjs` is offline only, accepts a strict categorical schema, rejects IP/email/user agents/referrers/unique identifiers, rounds to calendar day, refuses live calls, and suppresses any group below 20 synthetic event rows. It does not claim unique visitors, merchant sales or revenue. Do not wire it to a production browser. Only the existing site's approved privacy-compliant first-party analytics, if permitted, could later report coarse page and CTA counts.
