The most unreliable witness may be your mind.

You can miss a change directly in front of you. A familiar sentence can feel truer without gaining evidence. Two hundred years ago, magnetism investigators confronted another difficulty: a powerful report is not the same as a proved explanation. Here, we trace the observation back to its source.

Try this question: If you hear a claim three times but all three accounts cite the same witness, how many independent witnesses do you have? The answer is not automatically three. Follow the source trail before counting agreement.

This is an editorial reflection, not a validated psychological test or clinical evaluation.
