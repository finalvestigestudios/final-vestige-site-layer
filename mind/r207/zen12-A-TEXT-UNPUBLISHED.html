The song, the sentence, the difference

You finish reading a paragraph with music playing. You feel settled. But what did you retain? Those aren't interchangeable outcomes. A 2018 meta-analysis of 65 studies found small but reliable reading costs associated with background noise, speech and music, with intelligible speech and lyrical music particularly distracting. Those researchers did not study Zen12.

A separate background-music meta-analysis reported an overall average near no effect across mixed outcomes, but differentiated reading and memory costs from more favorable emotional and sports outcomes. No single study makes a universal soundtrack.

A free way to decide what you value

Use two comparable passages, one in silence and one with a soundtrack you choose. Give each the same reading time. Afterward, write the main idea and two details without looking, then check them. Next time, reverse the order and use fresh passages. The result may tell you something about your preferences and immediate performance; it is not a blinded trial, diagnosis or product efficacy test.

The existing Seven Day Focus Fieldbook offers a free twelve-minute starting routine, reflection pages and questions for checking audio claims. There is only one existing Jetpack/Dispatch signup pathway: no new form, customer list or extra subscription is created here.
