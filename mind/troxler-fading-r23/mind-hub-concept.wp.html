A field note on the evidence

Suppose a faint ring of color remains on a page but seems to fade when you keep your gaze nearby. The record says the pigment was still present. The witness says it vanished from awareness. Precision experiments reveal important roles for small involuntary eye movements. The striking part is not that somebody was fooled; it is that these are three different kinds of statement. A trustworthy archive does not collapse them into one.

That principle also governs commercial product coverage. A seller's claim that an audio session changes brainwaves isn't independent proof. Evidence about meditation generally isn't a study of a named product. An enjoyable recording is not a medical treatment. We show the distinction before discussing a possible purchase.
