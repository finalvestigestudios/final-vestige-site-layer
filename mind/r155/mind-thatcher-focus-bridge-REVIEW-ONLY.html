The face that looks wrong only when it's right side up.

A tiny alteration to a portrait can go unnoticed upside down. Turn the entire image, and the mistake becomes difficult to miss. The evidence is more interesting than the clickbait.

01 / The investigation

What inversion changes

Peter Thompson's 1980 experiment began the story; later researchers tested feature orientation, whole-face relationships and recognition at different angles. This is about perception, not a measure of intelligence or susceptibility to persuasion.

02 / Try a no-cost observation

Draw, rotate, compare

Sketch a simple imaginary adult face, make a second drawing with eye and mouth shapes inverted inside the upright head, and rotate both sheets. Results vary with image quality; this is an illustration, not a validated diagnostic.

How is this different from change blindness and the invisible gorilla?

Those experiments concern unnoticed changes or unexpected objects under particular attentional conditions. The Thatcher illusion concerns perceived features when an entire face is rotated. The effects should not be collapsed into one broad claim about the brain.
