The Unreliable Witness

The mind does not record the whole room.

A person can watch a familiar game and miss someone crossing the court. The strange part isn't the costume. It's how little that surprise tells us about what we usually notice—and how much advertising asks us to infer from it.

Follow the experiments back to their original papers, explore the stranger history of suggestion, and decide which practices and products are actually supported by the evidence.

A working rule for strange evidence

Before believing a sweeping conclusion, identify what a person was asked to notice, what the experiment measured, what alternatives were compared and what the source actually says. Treat the difference between reported experience and measured outcomes as a question worth investigating—not a character flaw, and never a ready-made sales pitch.
