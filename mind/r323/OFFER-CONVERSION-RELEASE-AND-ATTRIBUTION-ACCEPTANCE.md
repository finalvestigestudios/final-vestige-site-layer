## Editorial product-fit rule — one buyer, one prospective offer

**Buyer:** A viewer genuinely seeking a short audio-guided or sound-based meditation *format*, not seeking treatment, supernatural powers, wealth gains, a cure, or a proven concentration breakthrough. Wants a session that can fit a break, a choice of formats and clear pricing/returns.

**Why Zen12 is a *candidate*:** The seller's 12-minute duration fits the free Fieldbook's practical timebox, without implying equivalent results. Four offered styles address a real pre-purchase question: instruction versus ambient audio. The buyer may still prefer a free timer or the Fieldbook. **The choice to decline the offer counts as a successful informed reading outcome, not a commercial failure to manipulate away.**

**Why it remains blocked:** The 2023 FTC health-products guidance gives the crucial example of an unrelated brain-training study: population-level or third-party evidence cannot substantiate claims for a *specific* marketer's program. Primary vision experiments studied eye movements, not Zen12's particular recordings, and cannot support seller claims such as an hour of meditation in twelve minutes. Reference https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance .
