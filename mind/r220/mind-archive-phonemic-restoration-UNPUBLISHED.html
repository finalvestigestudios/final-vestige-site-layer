The Syllable That Wasn't There

Richard Warren's 1970 experiment removed a speech sound from a recording. A cough could make the absence feel as though it had never occurred. Samuel's later work shows that the acoustic replacement itself matters, not just the listener's expectations. Our unpublished original essay follows the evidence into transcripts, archives and the limits of modern audio-product claims.
