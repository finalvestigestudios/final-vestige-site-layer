A useful question before a purchase

In the original 1999 inattentional-blindness study, observers concentrating on passes could fail to report an obvious unexpected event. A 2023 study involving 4,493 participants across three experiments found that fast motion can make unexpected objects easier to notice. The details of the task matter. Neither result tested commercial audio.

If you're attracted to a short meditation program because you feel distracted, start by distinguishing three things: wanting a pleasant listening routine, wanting a structured practice you'll actually use, and expecting a measurable improvement in concentration. Those are not interchangeable promises.

Before buying, try the free twelve-minute audit

  1. Two minutes: choose one modest, safe task and write what you hope to notice.
  2. Three minutes: focus only on that task; don't stage surprise events or do this while driving.
  3. Two minutes: record what you recall, including uncertainties.
  4. Three minutes: compare your recollection with the page or notes you used.
  5. Two minutes: choose one small adjustment for next time, without treating the exercise as a diagnostic test.

You won't reproduce the original gorilla experiment: you already know you're auditing attention. The point is to see whether a simple routine is useful to you before paying for a branded one.

Studies about the general effects of meditation or brainwave entrainment do not prove this exact comparison.

Who might find the format useful?

A buyer who already enjoys listening to MP3s, wants four sound options, and prefers a packaged progression may value the convenience of a structured library. That's an inference about format preference, not a claim that Zen12 improves focus, health or mental performance.

A reader looking primarily for a quick productivity fix may prefer to begin with a free timer, a quiet workspace and a written plan. If what you want is treatment for anxiety, insomnia, trauma or another health condition, don't infer clinical effectiveness from a product sales page. Seek appropriate qualified guidance instead.
