The hatch opened inward.

It wasn't a single wire that made the fire fatal. The Apollo 204 Review Board recorded a chain of hazards in the spacecraft, including the cabin atmosphere, combustible materials and the hatch arrangement.

Evidence boundary: This crew photograph documents the astronauts—not the hatch mechanism. The Board did not establish one certain ignition source.

02 / THE ANNOTATION1847–48 · KING WILLIAM ISLAND

“All well” was written first.

The first message said the expedition was well. Written around it later, the April 1848 update told a different story: the ships had been abandoned, and the surviving party was setting out overland.

Evidence boundary: The note records what its writers reported then. It does not establish the fate of every crew member afterward.

Read the complete investigation ↗
Inspect the source & limits

Object: Victory Point note, 1847 original and April 1848 margin update; Commons scan from an 1869 printed reproduction.

Limit: A documentary record is not a complete reconstruction of subsequent events.

03 / THE MAP1903 / 1918 · BROOKLYN

The map predates the curve.

A 1903 rapid-transit map helps establish the system's earlier form. But the tightly restricted Malbone Street curve associated with the 1918 wreck was added later. The old map cannot locate the disaster's exact curve.

Evidence boundary: We are not claiming that the 1903 map depicts the 1918 derailment geometry—or that the two images literally align.

Read the complete investigation ↗
Inspect the source & limits

Image: Brooklyn Rapid Transit system map, 1903; NYPL Digital Collections item 5003296, as published in Final Vestige's Malbone investigation.

Limit: It is a system-context document, not a plan of the later temporary curve.
