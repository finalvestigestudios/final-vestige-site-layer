The claim the photograph cannot settle.

Use two original records, not a reconstruction, to test one historical claim. This short comparison is suitable for readers or classroom discussion; it is not a paid or NASA-endorsed lesson.

Record A · technical findings

The Review Board report

Event: 27 January 1967. Report completed: April 1967.

Supported: The Board investigated hazardous test conditions and determined that an electrical arc was the most probable initiator.

Not established: Which exact spark or individual electrical fault started the fire.

Verify the original NASA Review Board report.

Record B · aftermath image

NASA S67-21294

Catalog date: 28 January 1967.

Supported: The photograph documents the command module's damaged condition after the fire.

Not established: The initiating spark, the crew's experience, or a continuous sensor record.

Verify NASA photograph S67-21294.

Try the evidence test

  1. Identify the creator, date, and record type for each source.
  2. Write one supported claim from each record and label any inference.
  3. Explain why the two records cannot conclusively identify an ignition point.
Compare your reasoning with the source-based answer guide

Source distinction: A retrospective investigation is not a photograph of ignition. The dated image documents damage after the event; it is not direct evidence of the spark.

Careful conclusion: The Board identified hazardous conditions and a probable electrical arc. Neither source establishes the precise ignition location or a specific responsible component.

Quality check: A defensible answer cites both sources, distinguishes observation from inference, and names at least one limit without inventing details.
