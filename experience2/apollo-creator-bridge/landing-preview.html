Apollo 1 · Evidence and uncertainty · January 27, 1967

Fourteen seconds. More than one failure.

A cabin fire during a ground test killed Virgil “Gus” Grissom, Edward White and Roger Chaffee. The surviving investigation explains how a deadly system of hazards developed—without establishing one uniquely proven ignition source.

This independent educational overview is not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA. Technical findings are traced to the Apollo 204 Review Board; the photographic source register below is a separate record of context, not a fire reconstruction.

EvidencePlate / bounded interpretation

What the investigation shows—and what it cannot

Showing all four findings.

Board finding

Ground-test atmosphere

The test used a pure-oxygen cabin at elevated pressure; combustible materials and ignition hazards were present.

Primary source: Apollo 204 Review Board

Board finding

Escape hatch

The inward-opening pressure hatch could not be opened against the rapid pressure increase.

NASA safety summary

Evidence boundary

Ignition was not established

Investigators found possible electrical arcing but did not establish the specific ignition source conclusively.

NASA accident investigation

Interpretation boundary

A hatch was not the entire cause

The hatch worsened escape conditions; it did not alone explain oxygen, combustible materials, wiring, testing procedures or rescue readiness.

Question: Why is “the hatch caused the Apollo 1 fire” an incomplete conclusion?

Reveal a suggested evidence-based answer

The inward-opening hatch complicated escape as pressure rose. The investigation also identified the high-pressure oxygen atmosphere, flammable materials, possible ignition hazards and gaps in test and rescue planning. The exact ignition source was not established. Separate the conditions that promoted fire, the event sequence, the escape barrier and unresolved evidence.
