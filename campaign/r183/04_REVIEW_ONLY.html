The coast that outlived the certainty

A 1513 chart offers a better question than an Antarctic mystery.

The map survives. The claim built around it needs a separate examination.

In 1513, the Ottoman navigator Piri Reis assembled a chart of the Atlantic world. Some coastlines on the surviving fragment resist a neat fit against a modern globe.

A map is not an overhead photograph. It brings together observations, earlier charts and human judgments. An uncertain southern coastline cannot by itself prove that its maker saw Antarctica or a landscape beneath the ice.

Cartographic historian Max Justo Guedes compared the chart with contemporary nautical sources and sixteenth-century Portuguese seafaring knowledge. The useful question isn't which modern coast a line resembles, but what sources a 1513 mapmaker could plausibly have used.

The lasting mystery is the movement of geographical knowledge between navigators and cultures.
