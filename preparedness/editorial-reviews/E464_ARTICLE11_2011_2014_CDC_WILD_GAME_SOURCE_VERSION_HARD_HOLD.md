## Newly verified source lineage

1. **March 2011 University of Georgia Cooperative Extension** original *Preparing Safer Jerky* PDF (FDNS-E-43-11), [NCHFP-hosted original](https://nchfp.uga.edu/papers/UGA_Publications/prep_safe_jerky.pdf), page 1 **visually inspected** on Nov 18: its one parasite-freezing passage applies broadly to **pork and wild game** and reports **5°F or below / at least 20 days** for a portion 6 inches or less thick.
2. **2014 UGA/NCHFP *So Easy to Preserve* 6th ed.** [current official Jerky HTML](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/dry/meat-and-poultry/jerky/), directly read Nov 18: again groups **pork and wild game**, but its numerical passage says **0°F or below / at least 30 days**, with the same thickness condition. It explicitly identifies 2014 as its extracted origin.
3. **CDC March 12, 2024**, [How to Prevent Trichinellosis](https://www.cdc.gov/trichinellosis/prevention/index.html), directly read Nov 18: a distinct example concerns **pork**, then warns **wild-game worms may resist freezing**. The CDC's [May 23, 2024 frozen bear-meat outbreak](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7320a2.htm) identifies freeze-resistant *Trichinella nativa*. The [2026 North Carolina bear-jerky case report](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/32/7/26-0062_article) is a separate incident: implicated jerky **had not been frozen**.

**Interpretation:** Two same-institution document versions publish different freezer parameters while using a broad wild-game category. CDC's later scope distinguishes pork and wild game, and describes freeze resistance in wild game. This is a **species/applicability and source-version safety problem**, not something an editor may solve by selecting the colder or longer figure. Numeric statements above are **internal source-audit facts, NOT reader instructions**.
