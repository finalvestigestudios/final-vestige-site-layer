### Proposed original Article #15 insertion, after Step 5 school-reunification discussion (unapproved)

> A reunification plan has a middle as well as an ending. Ask your school who supervises children when pickup is delayed, how it will notify families if a site changes, and how staff can access an already approved care plan if a child depends on medication, equipment, communication support or special food. CDC's current school-preparedness guidance raises the possibility of overnight separation and asks about school policies for keeping and administering medications; it does not promise every school has the same supplies or permissions. Arrange medical details with the child's healthcare team and school. A contact card is not a substitute for that plan.

### Proposed original Tool #104 disclosure, after school-care section; NOT on print card (unapproved)

> This compact FV tool is a contact reminder only. It does not collect medical instructions, confirm that a school has supplies or alter its authorized-pickup records. If a child has essential care or accessibility needs, ask the school where the approved care plan is kept, who can access it, and what would happen if reunion took longer than the school day. Discuss medications, equipment and transport with qualified school staff and the child's clinician; do not place treatment instructions into the card's free-text fields.
