## Practical editorial gap and review-only redline

**Exact bounded gap:** Original Article #15 has a question about who can pick up, but gives no explicit planning discussion for **every school-authorized adult being physically unable to reach reunification**, nor an accessible notification pathway question. Literal original-body search confirms no case-insensitive `transport`, `interpreter`, `translation`, `mobility` or `accessible` in Article #15; general language and disability mentions exist and must NOT be called absent.

**Proposed addition after Article #15 Step 5 (not cleared):**

> A trusted adult named on a household card is not automatically approved to collect a child. Before an emergency, ask the school how it records and updates authorized adults, how the reunification site verifies identity, and what the school will do if none of those adults can arrive. Ask how location changes reach caregivers who cannot receive a voice call, need information in another language, or cannot travel to a moved site without accessibility support. CDC addresses these as school emergency-planning concerns; its guidance does not provide a national release rule, promise transport, or override school procedures. During an incident, follow the school's and local officials' instructions rather than traveling to an assumed pickup location.

**Proposed Tool #104 disclosure (not on minimal print card):**

> This card is a contact reminder, not proof of identity, custody, school authorization, or transport assistance. A person's appearance on FV paper does not update the school's approved pickup record. Confirm the school's actual alternate adult, no-pickup and accessible-notification procedures directly. Keep private care/legal records within approved school and clinician channels.
