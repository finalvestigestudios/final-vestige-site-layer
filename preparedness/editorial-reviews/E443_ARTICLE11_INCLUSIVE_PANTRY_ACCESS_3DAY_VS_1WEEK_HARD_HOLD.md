**Important source-scope boundary:** [FoodSafety.gov's emergency food guide](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/food-safety-in-disaster-or-emergency) recommends **at least 3 days of emergency food** and explicitly says specialty dehydrated food is unnecessary. [CDC's disability-inclusive individual emergency planning](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-emergency-preparedness/people-with-disabilities/make-a-plan.html) says to list **supplies personally needed for at least a week**, consider communication and assistive devices, rehearse and share the plan. These are distinct scopes, **not contradictory fixed-duration food instructions**. Neither promises universal household nutrition or medical sufficiency. Its [kit page](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-emergency-preparedness/people-with-disabilities/build-a-kit.html) addresses daily needs, communication aids and service animals; the [CDC easy-read page](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-emergency-preparedness/communication-resources/emergency-plan-easy-read.html) supports pictures/chosen communication methods. [FDA Food Allergies](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies) documents the nine major allergen labels but jurisdictional exceptions, so a stale label or written inventory **is not an allergy-safety clearance**.
