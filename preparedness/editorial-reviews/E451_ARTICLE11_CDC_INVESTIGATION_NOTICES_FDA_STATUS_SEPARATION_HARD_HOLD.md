## What E450 missed

A two-column "product recall" versus "public-health alert" comparison is insufficient. CDC's [November 24, 2025 notice framework](https://www.cdc.gov/foodborne-outbreaks/outbreak-basics/issuing-notices.html), reread directly Nov 18, explains its **food safety alert** (specific actionable advice on implicated food) and **investigation notice** (outbreak source unknown or only a broad category). These notices can overlap a recall, and are *not* ordinal risk classifications. FDA's [live CORE outbreak-investigations page](https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/investigations-foodborne-illness-outbreaks), directly read Nov 18, separately records an **active/closed investigation**, an **ongoing/ended outbreak**, and **recall initiated**; an active investigation is not automatically a product-specific consumer warning. FDA's [outbreak explanation](https://www.fda.gov/food/recalls-outbreaks-emergencies/outbreaks-foodborne-illness) describes when public advisories are issued. A category under investigation must not be falsely presented as proven contaminated.
