## Material consumer-safety omission

The existing FDA-focused recall explanation could lead a reader to search the FDA warnings page for an ordinary beef/pork/poultry jerky product and falsely assume they have checked the responsible federal recall agency. FDA’s own directly verified jurisdiction guidance assigns traditional livestock and domestic poultry meat products primarily to **USDA FSIS**; many commercially sold game meats (including deer and elk) are **FDA-regulated**, with possible voluntary FSIS inspection of some nontraditional species. Jurisdiction can be product-dependent. The word “jerky” is **not** a sufficient agency selector.

**Proposed bounded reader copy, review only:**

> Two packages can both say jerky and still lead to different federal recall records. Begin with the product’s species, label, ingredients and inspection information—not the product name alone. For many conventional livestock and domestic-poultry meat products, consult USDA FSIS Recalls and Public Health Alerts; for many commercially sold game meats and other FDA-regulated foods, consult FDA's recall resources. When jurisdiction or identity is uncertain, check FoodSafety.gov, follow the original FDA and FSIS notices, consult applicable state/tribal/local alerts and seek agency clarification. A missing result in one database is not a finding that the food is safe.
