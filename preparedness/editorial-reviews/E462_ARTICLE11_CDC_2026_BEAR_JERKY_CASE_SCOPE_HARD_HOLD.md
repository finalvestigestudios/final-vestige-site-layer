## Newly checked primary evidence

- **CDC Emerging Infectious Diseases, July 2026 issue (online June 24, 2026)**, [2024 North Carolina bear-jerky outbreak](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/32/7/26-0062_article): three cases among six consumers (one confirmed, two probable). The **jerky was prepared without prior freezing**. Investigators found *T. spiralis* in **different frozen cuts from the same bear**. Their discussion explicitly says weak larval motility in frozen cuts is **not proof of maintained infectivity**. Do not characterize the ingested jerky as previously frozen, or suggest that frozen-cuts observations establish safety/effectiveness of a home freezing method.
- **CDC MMWR, May 23, 2024**, [2022 bear-meal investigation](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7320a2.htm): bear meat **frozen 45 days** then grilled/served rare; six cases among eight interviewed; *T. nativa* identified in remaining frozen meat; two people with cases ate **only vegetables** cooked with the meat. This was **not a jerky outbreak**. It documents freeze-resistant species and cross-contamination, without providing population incidence.
- **CDC prevention guidance, March 12, 2024:** https://www.cdc.gov/trichinellosis/prevention/index.html distinguishes pork from wild game and says some wild-game parasites resist freezing.
- **NCHFP Jerky, UGA 2014 edition:** https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/dry/meat-and-poultry/jerky/ uses broadly worded wild-game freezing instructions. These **cannot be combined** into a universal treatment instruction without species/process adjudication.
