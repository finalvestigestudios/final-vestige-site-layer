## Exact new bounded reader passage, pending review
> **A recall list is not a certificate of safety.** The [federal FoodSafety.gov recall guide](https://www.foodsafety.gov/recalls-and-outbreaks) says that a package whose identifying details do not match a particular recall is not included in that recall. It goes further, describing such a product as “safe to use.” Those are not equivalent findings. A recall identifies the product covered by *that notice*; it cannot prove that every other product is free of hazards. [CDC explains](https://www.cdc.gov/foodborne-outbreaks/outbreak-basics/issuing-notices.html) that investigations can prompt alerts before an implicated product is identified, and [FDA cautions](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/are-you-storing-food-safely) that dangerous food may look and smell normal. For this archive, “no matching recall found” records a search result, *not* permission to consume or store a product. The original agency wording remains linked so readers can examine the difference.

Original free one-page **Notice Result Log** records brand, lot, printed date/storage words, issuing-agency URL, check date, *three search outcomes*, exact notice instructions and recheck date. It deliberately has **no checkbox to certify a food safe**. The worksheet complements the unchanged E403 package-label printable and collects/stores/transmits no personal information.
