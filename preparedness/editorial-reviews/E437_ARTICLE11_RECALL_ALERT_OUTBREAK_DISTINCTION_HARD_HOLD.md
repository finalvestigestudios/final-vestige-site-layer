## Source-led finding
E436's reader-first candidate correctly rejected treating a blank recall search as a safety certificate, but still suggested that recall lists might exhaust an agency's public warnings. Three sources checked directly November 17, 2026 clarify the boundaries:

1. **FoodSafety.gov** https://www.foodsafety.gov/recalls-and-outbreaks — public health/safety alerts may be appropriate when a recall is not recommended. Product-specific recall matching does not prove other hazards absent.
2. **FDA investigations register** https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/investigations-foodborne-illness-outbreaks — outbreak investigations are at different stages; FDA issues public-health advisories when there are specific actionable consumer steps. Do not treat every investigation as an active product warning.
3. **CDC retrospective 2023 outbreak review**, published September 17, 2025, https://www.cdc.gov/foodborne-outbreaks/php/data-research/summary-2023.html — a confirmed source does not always lead to recall or public notice, sometimes because the food is no longer in commerce. Historical example, NOT a current prevalence estimate.
4. **USDA FSIS Q&A**, updated September 3, 2025, https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/Is-my-product-part-of-a-recall — only indexed agency search text was accessible; complete direct page timed out. Do not label this direct verification.
