## Why this matters
E419's free notice worksheet inadvertently combined **"Lot / UPC / plant or EST code"** into one field. These are distinct identifying facts. CDC expressly lists the USDA plant number as useful on meat/poultry labels, in addition to product name, date, lot codes and purchase details. E421 makes separate worksheet fields and clarifies the plant number identifies an inspected establishment, not a specific batch, recall verdict or food safety clearance. "Not found" replaces a guessed number.

## Literal reviewer-candidate addition
> **Keep the plant number separate from the lot code.** For USDA-inspected meat or poultry, CDC specifically recommends recording the USDA plant number on the original label. It identifies an establishment, not the product's particular batch; the UPC, lot code and date answer different identification questions. USDA FSIS explains that an establishment number can appear inside the inspection mark or elsewhere on a package, sometimes with a statement telling readers where to look. That FSIS explanation was available in the agency's indexed text, but the complete original page returned a retrieval error on this review. Copy the number exactly if visible; otherwise record "not found," rather than guessing or confusing it with the barcode. For products without a USDA inspection mark, do not manufacture an establishment number. None of these identifiers is a test of whether the food is safe.
