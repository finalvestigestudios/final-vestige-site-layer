3. [CDC MMWR outbreak investigation](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7320a2.htm), published **2024-05-23** about a **2022** black bear harvested in Saskatchewan, Canada and served in South Dakota. Meat spent **45 days** in a household freezer before the meal; six of eight interviewed people met case criteria (two ate only cooked-with-meat vegetables). After **110 days** in the household freezer, investigators detected **motile T. nativa** larvae in remaining meat. Freezer temperature **unknown** per footnote. This is an observed epidemiological event, NOT a controlled NCHFP-process trial or jerky investigation.
4. [CDC Emerging Infectious Diseases / NC DHHS](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/32/7/26-0062_article), released **2026-06-24**, investigating a **2024 North Carolina** bear-jerky outbreak. Three cases among six consumers; implicated jerky **was not frozen beforehand**. Investigators tested different frozen cuts and identified T. spiralis. The two incidents cannot be fused, and observed larval movement does not necessarily prove infectivity in the North Carolina case.
