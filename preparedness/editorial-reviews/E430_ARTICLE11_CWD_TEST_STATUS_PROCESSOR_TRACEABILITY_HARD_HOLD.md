## Consequential new evidence (direct source read)
- [CDC, About CWD (Jan. 21, 2026)](https://www.cdc.gov/chronic-wasting/about/index.html): no demonstrated human CWD cases; strongly consider testing before eating deer/elk; when commercially processed, ask that one animal be processed individually, and do not eat CWD-positive meat. Do not interpret these points as a food-safety certificate.
- [Texas Parks and Wildlife official CWD result-status definitions](https://tpwd.texas.gov/huntwild/wild/diseases/cwd/test-results/): **“Not detected” means CWD prions were not detected in the specific tissue tested**. “Pending” is not completed testing and “inconclusive” is not a negative. Texas portal terms are Texas-specific; use the issuing agency's own interpretation elsewhere. Texas [hunter FAQ](https://tpwd.texas.gov/huntwild/wild/diseases/cwd/hunters/faqs.phtml) says about two weeks for results, while its result portal says five weeks. **Do not state a fixed turnaround**, national or otherwise; ask local agency.
- [Milstein et al., *Emerging Infectious Diseases*, April 2025](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/31/4/24-1176_article): experimental tests found CWD-prion **seeding activity** on meat-processing surfaces and transfer to previously negative samples after contact with contaminated grinding equipment. This is **not** proof of human CWD infection or a population estimate of contamination rates. Chemical decontamination experiments **must not** become household treatment instructions.
- [USDA APHIS (Oct. 15, 2026)](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/livestock-poultry-disease/cervid/chronic-wasting) confirms that apparently healthy animals can be infected during a lengthy presymptomatic interval.
