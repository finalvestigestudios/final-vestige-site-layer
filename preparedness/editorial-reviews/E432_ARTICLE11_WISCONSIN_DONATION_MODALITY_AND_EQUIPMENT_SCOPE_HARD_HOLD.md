## 1. New direct-source discrepancy (retain both)
- [Wisconsin DNR press announcement, 2025-10-29](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/newsroom/release/115401), lines under donor instructions: for donated deer being tested for CWD, the processor **"will not distribute"** the deer until results are known.
- [Wisconsin DNR current Deer Donation Program](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/hunt/donation), **CWD Testing Requirements**: processor **"may hold"** the donated deer or processed venison until results are known. Requirements depend on county and age. Both official documents directly accessible 2026-11-17. A categorical description of current processor practices cannot be inferred by selectively using either statement.
- [Separate DNR-posted Wisconsin DHS health advisory](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/Hunt/regulations/OnlineHuntingRegulations/DeerHealthAdvisories_LeadPFASCWD): recommendation against consumption **or distribution** of venison harvested in CWD-affected Wisconsin areas until the tested animal result indicates not detected. This is a Wisconsin-specific public-health recommendation, not a nationwide legal rule. No negative test makes a product or preservation method inherently safe.

**Editorial delta:** E431 relied on the 2025 press release alone. E432 reader draft attributes both the 2025 release and live program page, explicitly preserves different words and directs households to Wisconsin DNR/participating processor for current practice. Do NOT silently replace the press statement with the live-page wording or assert that all processors must hold.

## 2. Material processing-science limitation
- [CDC CWD guidance, 2026-01-21](https://www.cdc.gov/chronic-wasting/about/index.html) recommends considering testing and asking commercial processors to process the animal individually, **to ensure returned meat belongs to that animal**. That is a provenance measure; it is not certification of previously shared equipment.
- [CDC Emerging Infectious Diseases April 2025 study](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/31/4/24-1176_article) found CWD-prion RT-QuIC **seeding activity** on equipment and in previously negative muscle subsequently passed through contaminated equipment. The authors warn that **seeding detection does not directly imply infectivity**. Their experimental chemical conditions are not a household cleaning or decontamination protocol; do not republish treatment recipes, dilution or contact times.

**Editorial delta:** E432 reader copy separates identity, shared-surface history and assay-infectivity limits, and corrects the existing traceability diagram without adding another. The companion offline private paper worksheet now captures what a processor actually confirmed about shared tools, without granting product/food-safety clearance or exposing contact records to a server.
