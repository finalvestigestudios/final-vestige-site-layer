## New independently checked source-specific finding

The directly inspected [FoodSafety.gov Food Safety During Power Outage](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/food-safety-during-power-outage) lists *dried beef* within the **refrigerated-food outage chart**, alongside lunchmeat and other specific refrigerated meat items. This establishes one important category limitation: the word **dried** in a food's name alone cannot certify that a particular package is shelf-stable. **It does NOT establish that every jerky, inspected shelf-stable dried meat or commercially packaged product has the same storage requirements.** For any product, actual original label, unopened/opened status, official notices and agency scope remain decisive. The E460 draft reproduces no temperature threshold, storage duration, drying process or edible/not-edible ruling.

Other directly inspected primary sources:
- [FoodSafety.gov Food Safety in a Disaster or Emergency](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/food-safety-in-disaster-or-emergency): emergency foods need to account for cooking, water, refrigeration, infants, special diets, pets; specialty dehydrated foods not required.
- [CDC Personal Needs](https://www.cdc.gov/prepare-your-health/take-action/personal-needs.html) (2024-04-18): specialty foods for dietary restrictions, allergies, infant needs, diabetes.
- [CDC Easy-Read Emergency Plan](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-emergency-preparedness/communication-resources/emergency-plan-easy-read.html) (2024-09-18): pictures, support contacts, power/phone fallback.
- [CDC Building an Emergency Kit](https://www.cdc.gov/disability-emergency-preparedness/people-with-disabilities/build-a-kit.html): individualized supplies and access planning.

All new source claims are U.S.-scoped; state, tribal, local, product-specific and individualized health instructions take precedence. E460's eight-record source ledger separates directly retrieved 2026-11-18 sources from inherited *INDEXED ONLY* FSIS and conflict-held NCHFP/CDC sources.
