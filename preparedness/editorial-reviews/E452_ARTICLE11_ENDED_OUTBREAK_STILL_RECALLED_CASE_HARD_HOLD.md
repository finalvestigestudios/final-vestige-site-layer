## Directly checked primary-source case
The E451 unpublished `What Dried Meat Cannot Promise` review edition already distinguishes recalls, food safety alerts and CDC outbreak-investigation notices. E452 now adds a real, carefully bounded **outbreak-closed versus product-still-recalled** case rather than implying investigation status determines whether an item can be eaten.

1. [FDA-posted Graziers raw milk cheese company recall](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/sierra-nevada-cheese-company-recalls-graziers-raw-milk-cheese-because-possible-health-risk): company announcement **2026-09-28**, FDA publication **2026-09-29**; recall covers specified Graziers raw milk cheeses without narrowing to one lot/date code. This is the company's announcement republished by FDA, not a regulator-written risk assessment.
2. [CDC E. coli O26 final investigation update](https://www.cdc.gov/ecoli/outbreaks/raw-milk-cheese-09-26/investigation.html): **2026-11-13**, outbreak **over**, but CDC explicitly cautions long-shelf-life recalled product may remain in refrigerators/freezers and instructs consumers to discard or return it.
3. [FDA E. coli O26:H11 outbreak investigation](https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/outbreak-investigation-e-coli-o26h11-raw-milk-cheese-september-2026): **2026-11-13**, investigation **closed** and outbreak **ended**, yet FDA retains explicit recalled-product discard/return guidance.

**Narrow conclusion:** In this actual documented case, closing the outbreak investigation did *not* remove the original recalled-product guidance. An ended investigation is not a food-safety certificate and one example does not mean every ended investigation has a continuing recall. Readers must follow the *specific current issuing-agency product notice*, not a shorthand status field.
