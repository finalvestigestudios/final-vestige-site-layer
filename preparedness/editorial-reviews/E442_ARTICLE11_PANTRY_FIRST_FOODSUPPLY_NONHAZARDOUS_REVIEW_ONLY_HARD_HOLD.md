**Date:** November 17, 2026 (America/Los_Angeles) · **Scope:** Preparedness only · **State:** REVIEW ONLY / DRAFT HARD HOLD

## The editorial advancement

Previous E439–E441 Article #11 research, *What Dried Meat Cannot Promise*, documented important FDA/CDC/NCHFP and 2011/2014 NCHFP edition-scope problems, but left the article increasingly focused on material that cannot safely be released. E442 preserves that full research and adds the practical counterpoint: **you do not need to dehydrate or preserve meat to begin a usable household emergency-food plan.** This is a new review-only alternate based on the actually mounted **E441 HTML**, not a byte-identical E440 merge. Existing headings, citations, earlier evidence figures, source variants and safety holds are not overwritten.

The proposed new section **“Start with food you already trust enough to recognize”** takes readers through a no-cost **paper inventory** of familiar, labeled cupboard foods: what they already have; whether each requires a can opener, water, heat, electricity or refrigeration; which household members, infant/special-diet needs and pets it is for; and what ordinary grocery rotation could fill a gap. It explicitly **does not** assess food safety, offer recipes, imply nutritional adequacy, determine recalled-food eligibility, or replace household-specific/incident-specific official instructions. A free original paper companion, **“Pantry First: Three-Day Planning Sheet,”** is separately prepared and remains unpublished. The new section includes an original 3-part HTML/CSS dependency diagram with visible figure caption and rights/provenance.

**Significant verified source detail:** FoodSafety.gov's [Food Safety in a Disaster or Emergency](https://www.foodsafety.gov/keep-food-safe/food-safety-in-disaster-or-emergency), **last reviewed September 20, 2023**, explicitly recommends *at least a 3-day food supply* before emergencies and notes that households **do not need to purchase dehydrated or specialty emergency food**. Its food-selection guidance considers low/no cooking, safe water or refrigeration, infant/special diets and pet needs. This is the source-led, cheaper household action; it does not justify emergency food salvage, disinfection, homemade preservation or any unreviewed procedure.
