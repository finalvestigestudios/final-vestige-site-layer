## Material E444 error caught and corrected before publication

E444's Article #11 reader alternate and crosswalk described a "mandatory Contains declaration." The requirement is **major-allergen ingredient disclosure**, not a universal requirement for a stand-alone `Contains` statement. The FDA explains that disclosure may be in the ingredient's common name, in parentheses next to an ingredient, or in a `Contains` statement. **If** a Contains statement is present, it must name the applicable major allergen ingredients. By contrast, a `May contain` cross-contact warning is voluntary. A missing Contains statement, a missing May contain statement, or a copied old label **cannot certify allergen suitability**.

**Direct primary authorities read 2026-11-17:**
- FDA, *Have Food Allergies? Read the Label*: https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/have-food-allergies-read-label (separate Contains statement not universal, voluntary May contain, read ingredient information every purchase).
- FDA, *Food Allergies*: https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies (nine major allergens, wider allergy landscape and label rules).
- FDA-hosted *FALCPA 2004*, Sec. 203: https://www.fda.gov/food/food-allergensgluten-free-guidance-documents-regulatory-information/food-allergen-labeling-and-consumer-protection-act-2004-falcpa (statutory alternative disclosure forms; updated framework includes later FASTER sesame law; do not treat 2004 act as comprehensive 2026 scope).
