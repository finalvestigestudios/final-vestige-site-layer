## Proposed bounded addition — copy-ready for qualified editorial comparison, NOT release

> A separate, [peer-reviewed Utah State University Extension fact sheet published January 23, 2026](https://extension.usu.edu/foodsafety/research/can-i-freeze-dry-that-a-practical-guide-to-safe-and-effective-freeze-drying) makes a narrower point about bacteria: freeze-drying can preserve foodborne microorganisms rather than kill them. That reinforces the need to distinguish a food's dryness from a verified hazard-control process. It does *not* test the particular wild-game parasite treatment described by Minnesota Extension, and it does not settle whether that treatment controls every *Trichinella* species.

**Editorial significance:** The USU fact sheet provides independent, peer-reviewed university Extension evidence about **bacterial survival** during freeze-drying. That is a distinct scientific question from whether any **wild-game parasite freezer treatment** works for particular species and tissues. Do not combine USU, UMN and CDC into an invented process, and do not reproduce disputed numeric freezer/time/temp schedules as household advice.
