The first hour, the first 72 hours, the first two weeks

Official guidance plans in three windows. They are planning windows, not predictions.

First hour

Check on everyone, find out what is happening, and decide whether to stay or go. Follow local officials. See {{link:household-emergency-plan|the plan guide}}.

First 72 hours

Water, food you will eat, light, a radio and medicines. See {{link:three-day-kit|the three-day kit}} and {{link:water-math|water math}}.

First two weeks

A larger water supply and a plan for the days after help is slow to arrive. See {{link:two-week-water-storage|two-week water storage}} and {{link:making-water-safer|making water safer}}.

How we handle evidence

  • Every instruction comes from a named official source, such as FEMA and Ready.gov, the CDC, USDA, the U.S. Fire Administration or the Red Cross, and is cited where it appears.
  • What an agency says and what we think are labeled differently on every page. Where agencies disagree, we say so rather than pick one.
  • We give no medical, legal or product advice. Where a decision belongs to a pharmacist, clinician, landlord or local official, the page says to ask them.
  • Essential guidance is free to read and print, with no email or payment.
