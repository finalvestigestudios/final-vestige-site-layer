What to do now

  1. Tell the planner about your household. Tick what applies. Nothing is required.
  2. Work down the checklist it builds, and tick items as you finish them. Every item says which agency page it comes from.
  3. Fill in the contacts, then print or save the page as a PDF, or download it as a text file.
  4. If someone relies on electric medical equipment or refrigerated medicine, make an individual plan with their provider, the device supplier and your electric utility. This page cannot do that for you.{{cite:shelter-fema-power-outage-sheet}}{{cite:shelter-ready-disabilities}}

In an emergency, call 911. Carbon monoxide symptoms (headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, confusion) mean get outside to fresh air first, then call 911.{{cite:shelter-cpsc-co}} Private by design: your answers stay on this page and are not sent or saved. Closing the tab clears them.

Contacts to fill in

If someone depends on medical equipment or refrigerated medicine

This is the part of outage planning that most needs a plan made for one household, with the people who know the equipment. FEMA's December 2023 sheet says to talk to your doctor to make a plan for how you will use medical devices that need electricity and how you will store your medications.{{cite:shelter-fema-power-outage-sheet}} Ready.gov's disability page suggests asking your power provider about priority restoration, knowing more than one treatment facility if you rely on dialysis, and planning how to keep equipment running or replace it.{{cite:shelter-ready-disabilities}} Ready.gov's outage page asks you to find out how long medication can be stored at higher temperatures.{{cite:shelter-ready-power-outages}}

For a long outage, the agencies use different words. FEMA's sheet says to replace refrigerated medications if the power is out for a day or more unless the label says otherwise, to call your doctor or pharmacist if you depend on a refrigerated medication that has been at room temperature, and to use the medicine only until you have a new supply.{{cite:shelter-fema-power-outage-sheet}} Do not stop, skip or throw out a medicine you depend on without asking your pharmacist or prescriber. The CDC also says not to stop or change your medicines until you talk to your doctor.{{cite:shelter-cdc-heat}}

What this tool will not tell you

It will not say how large a generator or battery you need, how to wire one, how much fuel to store, how long equipment will run, or how to operate or ventilate any fuel-burning device. None of the agency pages we verified gives those details, and getting them wrong can kill. The planner repeats the agencies' rules for generators and carbon monoxide only: outdoors, at least 20 feet from windows, doors and vents, exhaust away from the home, never connected to the home's wiring, and a working battery carbon monoxide alarm indoors.{{cite:shelter-cpsc-co}}{{cite:shelter-ready-evacuation}}{{cite:shelter-fema-power-outage-sheet}} For the reasoning and the places where the agencies disagree, read {{link:outage-heat-cold-generators|heat, cold and generator safety in an outage}}.

Where the checklist comes from

Each item names its source. The pages behind the list are the CDC's power-outage, winter-storm, heat and carbon monoxide pages,{{cite:shelter-cdc-power-outage}}{{cite:shelter-cdc-winter-storm}}{{cite:shelter-cdc-heat}}{{cite:shelter-cdc-co-basics}} Ready.gov's power-outage, heat, winter-weather, shelter, tech and building pages,{{cite:shelter-ready-power-outages}}{{cite:shelter-ready-heat}}{{cite:shelter-ready-winter-weather}}{{cite:shelter-ready-shelter}}{{cite:shelter-ready-tech}}{{cite:shelter-ready-plan-locations}} FEMA's December 2023 sheet,{{cite:shelter-fema-power-outage-sheet}} NOAA Weather Radio,{{cite:shelter-nws-nwr}} and the CPSC's carbon monoxide pages.{{cite:shelter-cpsc-co}} Two items are marked as the editor's suggestion because no source says them. Where the agencies' numbers differ, as with the time after which food in a warm refrigerator should be thrown out, the item says so instead of picking one.
