What this does

  1. Choose what describes your household. You see only the groups that apply.
  2. Tick what you already have. Each item says why it is on the list and which official source it comes from.
  3. Print the checklist or save it as a text file, then work through what is left, starting with water.

Private by design. Your choices and ticks stay in this page, in memory. They are not sent anywhere or saved in cookies, browser storage or the address bar, and reloading the page clears them. The planner never asks which medicine or device anyone uses.

  • Official guidance Items come from the Ready.gov, FEMA and CDC lists cited beside them.
  • Editor's note Where a reason is our own reasoning, it begins with "Editor's note."

This is a starting list, not a prescription. Ready.gov's own checklist calls its list only a starting point and asks you to consider your family's needs.{{cite:start-ready-supply-list}} The planner gives no quantities except the CDC and Ready.gov water baseline, and a completed checklist does not mean your household is ready for any event. If anyone is in danger, call 911 or your local emergency number.

JavaScript is off, so every group is shown. Skip the groups that do not apply and use your browser's Print command to print the list.

What this tool has and has not done

It lists items from official kit lists and shows or hides them according to your choices. It does not know your hazards, your health, your building or your budget, and it cannot check what you have packed. Where the official sources disagree (how many days of medicine to keep, how often to review a kit, which mask suits which hazard) the items say to ask a professional or local officials instead of choosing a number. Nothing here is medical advice, and no item should be used to change how anyone takes a medicine. There are no product recommendations and no affiliate links. This tool is a draft awaiting human safety review.
