What to do now

  1. Choose an out-of-area contact and ask them first. Put their name and number on the card.{{cite:shelter-fcc-comms}}{{cite:shelter-ready-evacuation}}
  2. Pick a meeting place and an alternate, then fill in only what you are comfortable carrying.
  3. Use the minimal version if you are unsure. It prints only the out-of-area contact, a phone and the first meeting place.
  4. Print, cut and fold the card, keep it with you, and review it every year.{{cite:shelter-cdc-reunification}}

In an emergency, call 911 for anything life-threatening, and follow local emergency instructions; text may be an option when voice calls are congested, but delivery is not guaranteed.{{cite:shelter-ready-tech}} Privacy note: The card script is designed to display your entries on this page, not upload them. The website, browser, extensions, printing and shared devices may handle or retain data differently. Use minimal details. Independent browser privacy checks remain pending.

A printed card can be lost

Anyone who finds it can read it. Do not put a child's full name, a home address, a government ID number, a medical diagnosis or a password on it. First names or nicknames and phone numbers are usually enough. If you are unsure, tick the minimal version, which leaves out the school, caregiver and communication details and refers to your written plan at home.

How to use it well

A communication card supports a household plan; it does not replace one. Ready.gov's planning page asks households to decide how they will contact each other and reconnect, to choose a meeting place that is familiar and easy to find, and to write the plan down and practice it.{{cite:shelter-ready-plan}} Its fillable communication plan collects family contacts, school and childcare pick-up plans, emergency contacts, meeting places and medical information, but this draft does not independently verify that site's data handling or what a browser or device may retain.{{cite:shelter-ready-plan-form}} This card is the small part you carry.

The out-of-area contact comes first because networks jam. Ready.gov says text messages require less bandwidth and can be transmitted more reliably when many people are using their phones, and the FCC's older guide advises designating someone out of the area as a central contact.{{cite:shelter-ready-tech}}{{cite:shelter-fcc-comms}} The CDC suggests choosing a meeting spot, giving children emergency contact cards for their school bags, and helping older children memorize important names and numbers.{{cite:shelter-cdc-reunification}} If you and your children are separated in a chemical emergency, the CDC says not to go to them unless police, fire or other local officials say it is safe, because they may be sheltered where they are.{{cite:shelter-cdc-chemical-evac}}

School and childcare reunification

Schools and childcare centers have their own plans, and the card cannot know them. The CDC says to learn school safety procedures from the principal.{{cite:shelter-cdc-reunification}} Colorado's Office of School Safety, as one state's example, discusses waiting for school instructions; confirm identification and pickup requirements with your child's own school; other states and districts differ. California's Department of Education, for example, recommends that schools establish reunification locations and procedures and make sure parents are aware of them, in languages they understand.{{cite:shelter-co-oss-reunification}}{{cite:shelter-ca-cde-safety-plans}} The "reunification process" field is a reminder to ask, not an instruction.

If a child is missing after a disaster, the CDC lists NCMEC's 24-hour hotline, 1-800-THE-LOST, and NCMEC's own site shows the same number as 1-800-843-5678.{{cite:shelter-cdc-reunification}}{{cite:shelter-ncmec-hotline}} FEMA's fact sheet describes national reunification support, which is activated only in some Presidentially declared disasters, and FEMA marks it as archived content, so do not rely on it being available.{{cite:shelter-fema-reunification}}

Editor's note Custody arrangements, informal caregivers and who may collect a child are decided by your school's own rules and by law, neither of which the sources we read describe. List every adult your school has on file, keep the school's list current, and ask the school. We make no legal claims. A human safety review is required before this tool is public, and the box at the bottom of the page shows where that stands.

Printing the card

Choose "Save as PDF" or print on letter paper at 100 percent scale, with margins set to default or none. Each fold-over card is 7 by 2.125 inches, so it folds to 3.5 by 2.125 inches, a little larger than a credit card, and text is set at 7.5 points. Check the print preview to confirm nothing is cut off.
