# Creative Direction Study — Three Contenders

These are original Final Vestige directions. They are conceptual, not approved designs. All preserve black, charcoal, antique gold and a historically responsible visual language. Compare them in `concept-board.html` (self-contained exploratory swatches/hero treatments, **not** a deployed website or final fidelity target).

## A · THE BLACK DOSSIER — recommended starting point

**Emotion:** You have been admitted to the records room after hours.

- Full-bleed near-black hero, enormous editorial headline with deliberate restraint, one compelling archival image occupying 40–55% of a desktop hero.
- Warm ivory typography, museum-label gold, rich charcoal; the image is the texture, not an arbitrary animated background.
- Investigation unfolds in chapters and evidence drawers; scroll-position can set the active chapter without trapping scrolling.
- A signature moment: one observed record is isolated and carefully annotated; selecting a citation opens its actual provenance.
- Motion language: a delicate reveal of one meaningful detail at a time; no parallax dependence.
- Hero message: **Find what others miss.** Secondary: 'Follow the evidence.'
- Risk: too cinematic can slow reading, diminish navigation, or use image sizes too large. Counter with immediate visible links, reduced-motion design, and performance budget.

**Fidelity priorities:** Typography composition, source photo crop, 2–3 deliberate contrasts, one exquisite microinteraction, readable longform.

## B · THE MUSEUM AFTER HOURS

**Emotion:** A serious museum exhibition whose curator respects the visitor's intelligence.

- Ink-dark navigation and galleries alternating with warm paper-like evidence surfaces.
- Large centered artifacts with tiny but crisp catalog labels, provenance and dates.
- Sparse gold accents function like curator's annotations; clear light-on-dark and dark-on-light reading regions.
- Investigation chapters read like exhibition rooms; timelines are paced but fully navigable without a forced scroll.
- Risk: may feel formal or institutional instead of mysterious. Counter with exceptional story headlines and sharply composed imagery.

**Fidelity priorities:** Document inspection, typography pairing, margins, captions, museum quality labels, visually quiet transitions.

## C · THE OBSERVATORY

**Emotion:** An investigative instrument for exploring patterns that have been hiding in public records.

- Dark base with fine cartographic grids, thin rules, sparse gold nodes, typography that feels analytical but editorial.
- First major interaction maps verified relationship types between a source, event, person, place and claim.
- Map and chronology are primary; scalable Evidence Atlas eventually becomes the site signature.
- Risk: scientific interface can overpower narrative, especially while map dataset is sparse. Counter with narrative first and strongly justified location points only.

**Fidelity priorities:** Data legends, evidence graph semantics, visual encoding, map control usability, inclusive nonvisual descriptions.

## Recommended blend after selection

A for the homepage emotional framework + B for artifact/catalog treatments + C for an Evidence Atlas destination. **Do not implement all three at once.** The founder should select one primary direction from desktop/mobile visual proposals before production engineering.

## Non-negotiable shared visual language

- Color codes communicate function and provenance, never truth/falsity solely by color.
- Image credit visually adjacent to the image and source link accessible.
- One dominant headline per visual act; no dense grid before value is demonstrated.
- Source annotations remain legible and available with JS off.
- Whitespace and silence: use negative space as intentionally as animation.
- Primary CTA always remains clear; conversion is never disguised as evidence.
