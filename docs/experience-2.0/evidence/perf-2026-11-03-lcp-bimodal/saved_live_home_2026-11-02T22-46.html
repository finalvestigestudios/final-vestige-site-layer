Hidden in the Margins
Nearly 4,000 years ago, Nanni filed a clay complaint that Ea-nasir sent the wrong grade of copper after a gulf voyage. A Roman birthday invitation from Vindolanda preserves one of the earliest known examples of a woman’s Latin handwriting. Vindolanda’s ink tablets preserve Roman fort life in scraps: duty notes, supply problems, gossip, and a birthday invite. At Bath, 130 Roman curse tablets were thrown into a sacred spring asking Sulis Minerva for justice or revenge. A Roman hoard at Bath preserved 17,660 coins fused inside eight separate money bags. The Antikythera Mechanism was a geared Greek device for predicting astronomical cycles, recovered from a shipwreck. Pompeii’s walls preserved campaign notices, jokes, insults, and advertisements because ash sealed ordinary writing in place. Herculaneum’s library survived Vesuvius as carbonized scrolls too fragile to simply unroll. Domesday Book asked who held land before 1066, who held it after, and what it was worth in 1086. Domesday counted mills, fisheries, meadows, livestock, and unfree people as evidence of power and taxation. The Voynich Manuscript has plants, stars, bathing figures, and an unidentified script nobody has convincingly read. Guy Fawkes first gave officials the false name John Johnson after he was found near hidden gunpowder. One Gunpowder Plot examination says the plan included blowing up Parliament when the king was there. A 1605 confession signed Guido Fawkes appears in state papers, not just in bonfire-night folklore. During the Great Plague, watchmen locked and guarded infected houses while parish officials carried food inside. A 1666 plague order told towns to restrict gatherings, watch travelers, burn fumes, and keep animals from wandering streets. A 1784 royal commission tested animal magnetism with blindfolds, staged treatments, and controlled deception. Captain Bligh’s Bounty story did not end with mutiny; his open-boat voyage left a navigation record across the Pacific. Civil War balloons sent reconnaissance reports from the sky before airplanes existed. The H. L. Hunley sank an enemy ship in 1864, then vanished with its own crew. The Franklin expedition ships were found in Arctic waters more than 160 years after they disappeared. The Sultana disaster killed more than a thousand people, yet its paper trail hid in steamboat and military records. The Mary Celeste inquiry tried to explain why a seaworthy ship was found drifting with no crew aboard. The USS Maine explosion became a national argument because the blast left wreckage, witnesses, and competing official theories. John Snow’s cholera map was not just a map; it was an argument against a poisoned water supply. Early lighthouse logs can preserve storms, wrecks, rescues, and failures in the handwriting of the person on watch. Titanic transmitted an iceberg warning for another ship, then entered the same North Atlantic danger field. Titanic hearings questioned binoculars, lifeboats, wireless messages, steerage passengers, and ignored ice warnings. The Carpathia manifest records the names of the Titanic survivors pulled from lifeboats before dawn. The Triangle Shirtwaist record turned locked doors, burned bodies, and factory exits into labor-law evidence. In 1919, a wave of molasses tore through Boston streets after a storage tank failed. The 1918 flu record includes public-health orders closing schools, churches, theaters, and saloons. Operation Mincemeat planted fake invasion papers on a corpse to mislead Nazi intelligence. The Ghost Army used inflatable tanks, fake radio traffic, and sound trucks to impersonate entire units. Japanese balloon bombs crossed the Pacific; one killed six civilians in Oregon. National Archives code-talker files include World War II records that still are not all digitized. Japanese American incarceration records preserve names, camps, transfers, and case files behind a national policy. Project Paperclip records include personnel files for German scientists brought into U.S. postwar programs. A 1962 Joint Chiefs memo proposed staged incidents around Cuba as possible pretexts for war. The fake-civilian-airliner idea in Operation Northwoods was real enough to be written down. The CIA once wired a cat with a microphone and transmitter. The gear worked better than the cat. CIA engineers built robotic catfish named Charlie and Charlene to collect water samples without being noticed. Project AZORIAN hid a Soviet-submarine recovery mission behind a deep-sea mining cover story. The phrase ‘neither confirm nor deny’ has a Cold War shipwreck underneath it. The Hughes Glomar Explorer was sold as ocean mining while built for a far stranger job. CIA training once borrowed techniques from stage magic and deception manuals. A CIA spycraft story says even a dead rat could be used as a concealment device. VENONA began in secret in 1943 and did not reach the public until 1995. Some VENONA files read like spy fiction because real people appear under cover names. MKULTRA was not one dark rumor. It left subprojects, invoices, approvals, and paper trails. A Senate intelligence report says Castro assassination ideas included poison cigars and an exploding seashell. The CIA’s Family Jewels files recorded domestic surveillance, mail opening, assassination plotting, and other abuses. Operation CHAOS collected information on Americans during the Vietnam-era antiwar period. Project SHAMROCK involved copies of international telegram traffic reaching U.S. intelligence agencies. Project MINARET placed U.S. citizens, including civil-rights and antiwar figures, on NSA watch lists. Government remote-viewing files do not prove psychic powers. They prove officials filed classified session reports about them. Project Blue Book does not prove UFO claims. It proves the Air Force cataloged thousands of reports. The Robertson Panel worried less about aliens than panic, overloaded reporting channels, and Soviet exploitation. The Tuskegee syphilis study ran for forty years without informed consent or proper treatment. A presidential advisory committee later reviewed government-sponsored human radiation experiments. CAIB found that foam from Columbia’s external tank struck the left wing 81.9 seconds after launch and breached its thermal protection; superheated air entered during re-entry and progressively destroyed the wing. NTSB found that inadequately designed U10 gusset plates failed under added bridge weight, traffic, and concentrated construction loads, causing the 2007 I-35W bridge collapse in Minneapolis. NIST concluded that fire-heated floor beams expanded, a Floor 13 girder lost its connection to Column 79, cascading floor failures left the column unsupported, and its buckling initiated WTC 7’s global collapse. CSB found that effective compression buckled Deepwater Horizon’s drill pipe off-center inside the blowout preventer, moving it beyond the blind shear ram’s cutting region and preventing the well from being sealed. The El Faro investigation recovered data from a recorder resting roughly 15,000 feet below the Atlantic. NTSB found that the Big Dig tunnel ceiling used epoxy anchor adhesive with poor creep resistance; under sustained load, the epoxy deformed and fractured until ceiling anchors pulled free. Federal Register rulemaking can hide major consequences in definitions, thresholds, exemptions, and effective dates. GAO’s High Risk List is a public map of federal problems that can stay open for decades. The 2011 official Pentagon Papers release opened thousands of pages not available in the common editions. Watergate’s Road Map contained 53 numbered factual statements and 97 supporting documents. JFK assassination records were still being released by the National Archives in 2025 and 2026. The 2025 JFK release even created a privacy problem: some unredacted files included living people’s information.

THE RECORD CHANGED · 1847—1848

One sheet of paper.
Two different worlds.

The Franklin expedition left a message on an Admiralty form. A second message, written around the first, transformed what the same document could tell its readers. The contrast is in the surviving record—not an invented animation or an imagined map.

Selected historical wording · original editorial typesetting, not a document facsimile

READ THE SURVIVING RECORD

Choose a dated entry, then return to the complete sheet.

Both entries are visible. The original and later addition share one surviving sheet.

01 / THE ORIGINAL NOTE28 MAY 1847

“All well.”

The first message, signed by Graham Gore and Charles Des Voeux, reports the expedition under Franklin’s command. The words cannot describe what happened after they were written.

THE MARGIN
CHANGES THE RECORD
02 / THE LATER ADDITIONAPRIL 1848

“Terror and Erebus were deserted”

Crozier and Fitzjames report that the ships had been abandoned on 22 April after months trapped in ice. Their marginal update records deaths and an intended journey toward Back’s Fish River.

What this evidence establishes—and what it cannot

Established by the document: the expedition’s earlier written status and the later account of abandonment, deaths and intended travel. These statements were recorded by expedition officers at different times.

Not established by the document: the final fate of every sailor after the April 1848 entry, or what every survivor experienced. The 1848 message does not erase the 1847 original—it changes our understanding of it.

Cataloguing note: the surviving text opens its marginal update with “25th April 1848,” while the Royal Museums Greenwich catalogue describes the addition as 26 April 1848. This exhibit uses the unambiguous month rather than silently resolving that difference.

not a verdict.

One record can be genuine and the story told about it still be wrong. Select a case, then inspect the distance between what survives, what we can reasonably infer, and what the source cannot tell us.

18471848TWO ENTRIES / ONE SHEET

READING 01 / CHANGING EVIDENCE

What survives

Officers wrote an “All well” note in May 1847. An April 1848 addition reports that the expedition had abandoned its ships.

What remains open

The later addition changes how the earlier message reads. It does not establish what happened to every person after that final recorded departure.

THE BOUNDARY A true statement about one moment is not a guarantee about the next.

WHAT CHANGED?Open the evidence note+

The circular FV seal at the lower right is present in Frame A and absent in Frame B. Every other decorative mark is unchanged. The labels “Specimen A” and “Specimen B” identify which illustration you’re inspecting; they are not part of the hidden difference.

This is a studio illustration of a comparison task, not a standardized experiment, diagnostic test, or a reenactment of a published study. Finding the seal quickly—or missing it—does not measure your attention or memory.
