## Newly clarified candidate provenance and rights conflict

1. **NARA 540012** — [DocsTeach primary catalog](https://docsteach.org/document/aerial-of-three-mile-island/) explicitly dates its aerial view to **April 10, 1979**, classifies rights as **No Known Copyright**, and identifies Record Group 220. A Commons digitization records **2700×2700**, a catalog measurement that is NOT a locally downloaded/decoded image. Conditional uncropped derivative: **960×960**, only after binary inspection and rights review.
2. **NARA 540027** — [NARA-origin archival Commons TIFF](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oblique_(view_of)_TMI_(Three_Mile_Island)_-_NARA_-_540027.tif) carries NAID **540027**, a catalog date of **April 11, 1979**, and **2700×2700** archived TIFF geometry. The page identifies the original photographer as **unknown**, even while displaying a US-government-work public-domain tag. Conditional uncropped **960×960**. This must not be assumed to match the published 16:9 crop or the NARA 540012 photograph.
3. **“Three Mile Island (color).jpg”** — [Commons photo record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Three_Mile_Island_(color).jpg) credits a former Department of Energy photo webpage, tags it PD-USGov-DOE, and documents **2592×2488** current JPEG dimensions (**960×921** conditional uncropped derivative). However, its **2009 file history cites an NARA high-resolution source**, its later versions are color-adjusted/cropped, and Commons links it to the NARA 540027 source photograph. Thus an apparent **DOE vs NARA lineage ambiguity** exists: do not count these as two independent government-authored alternatives or treat the Commons PD tag as an item-specific legal opinion. Identity, credited author, and rights still require review.

**Primary archival counterevidence:** [NARA's own RG 220 finding aid](https://www.archives.gov/research/cartographic/aerial-photography/still-pictures-rg220-aerial-photography) says the 220-TMI photos were **acquired by DOE under contract with EG&G**, an outside photographic services company. Government custody and even a federal commission's collection title do **not automatically prove federal employee authorship of a particular photograph**. DocsTeach's “No Known Copyright” statement differs from an affirmative ownership/license grant. Whether a particular contractor work was assigned to the government is not established in the records inspected. Keep reuse/rehosting on **HOLD** until creator/assignment and original file lineage are independently checked.
