## Two high-value visual-integrity findings

1. **Slot 221:4 is not merely a standalone 1981 photograph of reactor damage.** The primary INL source (*Proving the Principle*, p.157) explicitly identifies a **1961 photograph** reused in **1981** as a safety-message poster with text framing (“Lest We Forget”). Commons' date **1981** reflects the poster/reuse, not original incident photography. Published ALT already calls it retrospective but does not distinguish the poster; suggested review-only ALT: `1981 safety poster reusing a 1961 photograph of the damaged SL-1 reactor vessel.`
2. **Slot 221:6 has internally conflicting catalog credit/date metadata.** Commons asserts “Taken in 1981” and author **INEEL 81-3966**, but INL *Proving the Principle*, **p.155**, depicts the demolition scene with the figure credit **INEEL 67-2587** and says foundations were what remained **by 1962**. This demonstrates a **catalog accession/source conflict** and makes the 1981 date unsupported as the photograph's original shooting date. Preserve cautious existing “later cleanup” ALT pending item-level reconciliation; never assert the figure was photographed in 1981. Other figures in the same primary book are not automatic pixel-identical substitutes for this file.

Commons public-domain assertions for U.S. Army, AEC, and INEEL images are provenance leads only; national laboratories may use contractor-produced works. Before any localization confirm each image's creator/employment status, derivative/poster rights, attribution requirements, photograph-versus-diagram or photograph-versus-poster distinction, and whether the proposed rendition is visually identical to its intended scene and crop. Do not treat a publisher URL, image catalog, public-domain tag, or a PDF figure as an unconditional WordPress rehosting license.
