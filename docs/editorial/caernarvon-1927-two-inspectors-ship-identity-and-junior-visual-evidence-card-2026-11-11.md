## The material finding
Two separate vessels named *Inspector* appear in 1927 flood accounts. Leland R. Johnson's *Situation Desperate* (U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, 2011), printed pp. 147–152, refers in the same chapter both to (a) **the Corps inspection boat `Inspector`**, on which Mississippi River Commission personnel traveled and met a New Orleans delegation on **April 25**, and (b) **a tanker ship** reported to have rammed the levee at the separate **Junior Plantation** breach on **April 23**. Treating the April 25 meeting as occurring aboard the stranded tanker produces an apparent chronological paradox. It is not a paradox once the separate Corps towboat is recognized.

Independent marine-vessel historical listings confirm an actual **U.S. Engineers propeller towboat *Inspector***: built **1915** by Charles Ward Engineering Works in Charleston, West Virginia; assigned to the **Memphis District**; active in the **1927** flood relief fleet; refurbished **1929** and kept in service for decades. The specialist journal *The Waterways Journal* (Capt. David Smith, **April 17, 2023**) describes a **144 × 30 ft steel-hulled**, twin-screw U.S. Engineers towboat and cites *Way's Steam Towboat Directory*. NavSource's Army ship index independently catalogs a 1915 USED towboat *Inspector* and links photographs cataloged through Wisconsin/USACE collections. Both listings may share underlying ship records; do **not** count them as statistically independent documentation of every specification.

Crucial limit: The official history, ship directory and secondary sources identify a Corps towboat, and the 2011 Corps narrative distinguishes it from a tanker in context. They do **not** independently establish the tanker’s registry, hull number, owner, captain or vessel-log account, or prove that the tanker struck the levee. The collision remains contested by contemporaneous reporting.

## Interpretive correction: three claims, three evidence thresholds
1. **Established by multiple sources:** A U.S. Engineers towboat named *Inspector* existed in 1927 and the Corps recounts Mississippi River Commission meetings aboard it. The junior-crevasse account concerns a separate reported tanker.
2. **Documented allegation, not established causality:** The tanker supposedly crashed into the Junior levee April 23. A 1977 Corps retrospective, a 2011 Corps history, 1927 photographic captions and Hyde catalog captions all repeat impact as cause. These may be a **shared source tradition**, not independent witnesses. The April 24 AP report preserves a competing eyewitness account.
3. **Seen in an archival photo, not established by it:** A ship appears alongside a large break in the levee at Junior in a 1927 image; its position after the event cannot identify the mechanical sequence of the breach.

This is a factual identification correction, **not** evidence that the separate April 29 Caernarvon deliberate demolition was staged or fraudulent. Junior is downriver and separate; **do not label Junior imagery as Caernarvon blast photography**.
