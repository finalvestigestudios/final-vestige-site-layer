## Significant corrected finding

The prior reader v49c stopped at May 19, 2011 contemporaneous statements and correctly declined to presume that proposed bills became law, but it **did not report what eventually happened**.

The official Louisiana 2011 **HB475 history** states that on **June 29, 2011** the governor signed it as **Act 363** (effective that day). The **enrolled Act**, printed pp. 4–5, amended R.S. 38:330.12(B)(2): *if* non-flood authority assets were sold/transferred, proceeds were to be distributed within **30 days** in an **80% Authority / 20% Haspel & Davis class** ratio. The enacted text explicitly identifies Plaquemines **31-357** and the **December 11, 2000 judgment**. This was **not just a proposal** by the end of June. Neither the Act nor bill history establishes that an asset was actually sold, what price was realized, what was transferred to a class trust, or what a named heir actually received.

In contrast, the official 2011 **HB476 history** only records House Natural Resources referral on **April 25**, status **pending**. Its original-bill **May 15, 2011 fiscal note**, signed by fiscal analyst Stephanie C. Blanchard, estimated **approximately $15,339,600 principal owed** using district-provided data and **about $10.25m accrued interest** under the note's illustrative assumptions; under a **6%** modeled schedule through July 2020, **about $3.4m annually**. The board's May 19 discussion had put an **approximate $3.7m annually** figure on a proposed HB476 installment path; these are **different contemporaneous forecasts**, not amounts paid. The note reports that existing law acknowledged earlier partial payments, **not** dates, banks, recipients, or cleared transaction records. Do not incorrectly claim nobody was ever paid.
