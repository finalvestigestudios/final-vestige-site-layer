## Why this cycle changed the reader

The reader-facing version previously omitted a 1999 Louisiana Fourth Circuit decision bearing directly on *what a purchase under threat of expropriation meant* in the Bohemia case. It also did not use the **FY2008 official financial-report note** already preserved in the 57-source master dossier: that note says the Orleans Levee District was maintaining a “complete accounting” of royalty revenues, and states DNR certified interests in **164 of 220 tracts** before the later amendments. This is integration of a **previously indexed source into the public-facing reader**, not a claim the 2008 audit was discovered for the first time. Both are critical limits to a missing-accounting narrative.

The **March 31, 1999** *Haspel & Davis*, 732 So. 2d 113, No. 98-CA-1664, appellate opinion states that, on August 21, 1997, the board attempted through a reconventional demand to undo earlier property returns; the court affirmed dismissal, reading 1924 Act 99's power to expropriate within the 1984 Bohemia restitution law's purchases-under-threat provision. It discussed **1997 Act 1364** as introducing escrow and royalty payment procedures, unavailable when the landowners filed suit in 1988. The disposition **does not** adjudicate each deed, establish a completed claimant settlement or establish modern law outside the litigated context. [Court opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/la-court-of-appeal/1028222.html), independent [reporter transcription](https://openjurist.org/732/so2d/113/haspel-davis-milling-planting-co-v-board-of-levee-commissioners).

The **FY2008 SLFPA-East report, Note 7, printed pp. 32–33**, directly examined in the original Louisiana Legislative Auditor PDF and in **zero-based screenshot images 35–36**. It reproduces an Orleans Levee District management assertion that it maintained a complete royalty accounting, gives the 164/220 historical tract-certification count, says claims certified through June 30, 2005 were conveyed, cites multiple payments since 2001 and a $600,000 **court registry** payment on February 26, 2004, and mentions another approximately $10.08m negotiated settlement figure. All are historical disclosures, **not a verified 2026 debt, a beneficiary payment receipt or an independently audited tract-level schedule**. Treat the roughly $10.08m as a potentially superseded/conditional agreement, **not** additive to the 2007 federal court's $21.085m contractual terms. [Official 2008 scanned report](https://www.lla.state.la.us/PublicReports.nsf/CCB8B3E324D206DA8625755A007E35BB/%24FILE/0000985C.pdf). Note [court-filed federal opinion of July 23, 2007](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/Opinions/pub/07/07-30086-CV0.wpd.pdf) describes a June 2005 second agreement subsequently set aside, without proving it is the same $10.08m arrangement.
