## Question answered — why DNR land certification was not a royalty-distribution remedy
Re-read the March 31, 1999 appellate opinion, *Haspel & Davis Milling & Planting Co., Ltd. v. Board of Levee Commissioners*, No. **98-CA-1664**, **732 So. 2d 113**, using two independent full-text reproductions:
- [FindLaw judicial opinion](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/la-court-of-appeal/1028222.html), heading and *Exception of Prematurity*, with first certifications reported for 1989–91.
- [OpenJurist independent reproduction](https://openjurist.org/732/so2d/113/haspel-davis-milling-planting-co-v-board-of-levee-commissioners), paragraphs on exceptions, land-return certifications and the 1997 legislative procedural change.

Court-reported procedural facts: class lawsuit **February 22, 1988**, seeking mineral revenues accruing from **June 29, 1984** through land transfer; claimants requested a judicial determination and court-registry escrow. The board asserted that the claim was premature without exhaustion of DNR remedies. The appellate court rejected that assertion because DNR's authority to certify land-return eligibility did not supply a remedy to decide the ownership of mineral revenues or compel the turnover and escrow demanded in the action. The court also affirmed dismissal of the board's reconventional attack on returned titles. It expressly identified **Act 1364 of 1997** as later establishing procedures for escrow and revenue payments. New narrative section makes the legal-domain separation explicit without interpreting the ruling as a judgment that every claimant was paid.
