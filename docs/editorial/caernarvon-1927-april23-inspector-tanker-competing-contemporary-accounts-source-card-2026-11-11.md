# Caernarvon 1927 — the disputed *Inspector* tanker explanation for the April 23 Junior/Junius break
**Investigations source and correction card | November 11, 2026, approximately 20:42 Pacific | Editorial owner: Investigations #1463**

## Material finding and why it affects the active article

The current Final Vestige article previously stated without qualification that a tanker **struck** a Mississippi levee about forty miles below New Orleans on **April 23, 1927**. That is how Leland R. Johnson's 2011 U.S. Army Corps of Engineers history narrates the event. A dated **April 24, 1927** Associated Press report, preserved on the original newspaper page, demonstrates that the tanker-collision explanation was *already disputed* in reporting immediately after the breach. The historical fact of a separate lower-river break is distinct from an established causal finding about the ship.

This is an evidentiary correction, not a change to the claim that the **April 29 Caernarvon/Poydras cut was deliberate**. A claim about the separate April 23 break's effect on New Orleans river stage would require a specific gauge analysis, not an inference from the newspaper story.

## Source 1 — contemporary report (primary source for reporting and contemporaneous uncertainty)

- **Bibliography:** *The Brownsville Herald* (Brownsville, Texas), **April 24, 1927**, vol. 35, no. 292, **page 1**, Associated Press dispatch dated **New Orleans, April 23**; article headed **“Report Levee Break Below New Orleans.”**
- **Direct archival facsimile and OCR:** https://texashistory.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metapth1379708/m1/1/ (University of North Texas Portal to Texas History).
- **What the story actually supports:** The west-bank levee approximately **40 miles below New Orleans**, near a named plantation, had broken. The oil tanker *Inspector*, reportedly outbound for Cuba, was **suspected** of ramming it; the same report says **some residents believed it passed as the breach occurred, sounded its whistle/siren and pulled alongside to help**. A reported approximately 100-foot opening by 9 p.m. appears as breaking-news information and is *not* a later survey. The OCR renders the place name close to “Junius Plantation,” but the damaged transcription makes that spelling uncertain. Do not silently normalize it.
- **Limits:** This is near-contemporary *reporting of disputed accounts*, not a vessel log, sworn witness record or an engineer's adjudication. Do not claim any allegation or denial was proved. The newspaper page is a digitized scan with known OCR corruption; no extended verbatim quotation is cleared for precision from its OCR alone.
- **Editorial use:** In the article, attribute the tanker-collision account to the later Corps history and explain that the 1927 AP report included an immediate competing eyewitness narrative; identify what is unresolved.
