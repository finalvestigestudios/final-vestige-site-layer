## Finding and editorial value

A closely adjacent but legally distinct New Orleans flood-control undertaking produced a long property and mineral-royalty dispute decades after the 1927 Caernarvon blast. The **Bohemia Spillway** in Plaquemines Parish (authorized 1924, built 1924–26, roughly fifty river miles downstream) was not the April 29, 1927 Poydras/Caernarvon artificial crevasse, and not the **1991 gated Caernarvon Freshwater Diversion**. The three interventions must never share a single flow figure, source caption, ownership history, or compensation ledger. Their juxtaposition can strengthen a careful inquiry into the duration and distribution of flood-control costs, **not** establish that one set of claimants received money owed to another.

Potential separate companion investigation: **“The Spillway That Kept Paying After Its Purpose Ended”** — how emergency land acquisition, mineral royalties, constitutional return legislation, delayed restitution and the surviving records complicate an apparent public-works success story. This is a proposed editorial angle, **not** evidence that the spillway's original purpose was in fact scientifically obsolete: the legislature made that determination in 1984, and the 1986 court adjudicated statutory authority/constitutionality, not a modern hydrological counterfactual. Do not open a duplicate article without coordinating topic selection.

## Claim-by-claim source ledger

### 1. 1924 authorization, scope, and 1920s acquisitions (contemporaneous statute as reported by later court)

The **Louisiana Supreme Court ON REHEARING**, *Board of Commissioners of the Orleans Levee District v. Department of Natural Resources*, **496 So. 2d 281**, decided **October 20, 1986**, recites Act 99 of 1924, construction during **1924–26**, and approximately **33,000 acres** within the Bohemia Spillway. The court's categories are **3%** acquired by expropriation, **48%** purchase under threat of expropriation, and **49%** conveyed from state/public land; the latter should not be described as displaced private owners. The court says private-property owners were compensated **$389,500** in then-current dollars. These categories are from the court's retrospective factual summary, not independently examined deed-by-deed.

**Primary judicial opinion:** https://openjurist.org/496/so2d/281/bd-of-com-rs-of-orleans-levee-dist-v-dept-of-natural-resources (opening/background paragraphs, pp. 284–285). Original decision **483 So. 2d 958** and the **October 20 on-rehearing disposition** are separate opinions; cite the rehearing as controlling on the final constitutional holding. Some later case citations describe the private purchase fraction differently when excluding public land; do not mix denominators.

### 2. Mineral-income counterpoint, not a claimant-payout ledger

The same October 1986 Supreme Court opinion records gross mineral receipts to the levee board of **$1,734,480 (1929–1948)** and **$42,921,729 (1950–1981)**, plus an **approximately $5 million annual** mineral-revenue rate at the time of the 1986 ruling. They are **not** constant-dollar figures, net profit, or Caernarvon household damage payments. The uncovered 1949 interval is not quantified by the figures provided; do not interpolate. The numbers show a policy tension deserving examination, not an independently demonstrated enrichment claim.

**Source:** same Supreme Court rehearing, historical background.

### 3. Act 233 of 1984, constitutional ruling, eventual deeds

The Louisiana legislature's **Act 233 of 1984**, after a 1983 constitutional amendment, declared Bohemia's public purpose had ceased and ordered qualifying former owners/successors to receive property **including mineral rights**, subject to certification. The Louisiana Supreme Court **on rehearing** reversed the district-court unconstitutionality ruling and upheld the law on **October 20, 1986**. An appellate opinion in **Vogt v. Board of Commissioners**, decided **March 27, 2002**, confirms that **some certified parcels were transferred in 1991 and 1992**; it also recounts an accounting of **$2,853,358.44** in mineral revenues for **the particular plaintiffs' properties** since June 29, 1984. The accounting figure is neither an award for every Bohemia landowner nor evidence it was all paid.

**Primary case records:**
- 1986 *on rehearing*: https://openjurist.org/496/so2d/281/bd-of-com-rs-of-orleans-levee-dist-v-dept-of-natural-resources
- 2002 *Vogt* (La. Ct. App. 4th Cir.): https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/la-court-of-appeal/1052451.html (facts and procedural history)

**Limit:** deed-by-deed returns and present-day property registries not examined. “All land returned” is unsupported.

### 4. 2007 Fifth Circuit sharply narrows a seeming $17.4m victory

In *Haspel & Davis Milling & Planting Co. v. Board of Levee Commissioners*, **493 F.3d 570**, decided **July 23, 2007**, the Fifth Circuit **REVERSED** a federal district-court takings judgment of **$17,442,332.87**, rendered for the levee board, dismissed the federal takings claim with prejudice, and vacated seizure writs. It described a separate **state-court settlement** with **$2,318,263.72 immediately** and **$18,767,145.26 only “as and if funds are appropriated”**, and cited payments below a $2.6m/year threshold. Do **not** report the reversed federal $17.44m award as an operative payout or conflate the settlement's conditional unpaid balance with money already distributed. The takings reversal alone does not settle every state-law contract payment issue.

**Primary federal appeal:** https://openjurist.org/493/f3d/570/haspel-davis-milling-planting-co-v-board-of-levee-commissioners (opening and background paragraphs).

### 5. Fiscal-year-2008 public audit: actual page-image QA passed

Read and **visually inspected actual scanned PDF pages**, not just search snippets: the **Southeast Louisiana Flood Protection Authority–East**, *Basic Financial Statements and Independent Auditor's Reports*, year ended **June 30, 2008**, public report hosted by the **Louisiana Legislative Auditor**. Firm: **Rebowe & Company**; auditor's report dated **November 18, 2008**. **Note 7, “Litigation and Claims,” printed pages 32–33 (PDF zero-index pages 35–36)** lists: **164 of 220** Bohemia tracts involving DNR-certified interests before the 1997 and 1999 amendments; property claims certified as of October 1, 2002 returned; and all claims **certified** by June 30, 2005 had been conveyed. This is not a declaration that every one of 220 tracts had a qualifying claimant or had been returned. The note also states accrued post-June 29, 1984 mineral royalties were to be returned until transfer, records the Haspel & Davis class agreeing to accept **$10,076,559** to settle, cites a **$600,000** court-registry payment February 26, 2004, records the Fifth Circuit reversal, and states judgment payment depends on available appropriated funds. These are **historical management financial disclosures in a published audited annual report**, not proof that a particular beneficiary ultimately received all proceeds or proof of the board's 2026 accounts.
