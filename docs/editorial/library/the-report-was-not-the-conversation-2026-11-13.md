# The Report Was Not the Conversation
*Final Vestige Studios · Library editorial working draft · 13 November 2026 · DO NOT DEPLOY OR PUBLISH*

**Deck:** The conclusion gets the headline. The reply is often buried several links below it. To understand what an official investigation actually settled, follow both.

## Open the packet, not only its cover

An inspector-general page can look like a single report. The document listing for the Environmental Protection Agency Office of Inspector General's review of lessons from the Deepwater Horizon response makes the limitation plain. [EPA OIG Report 11-P-0534](https://www.epa.gov/office-inspector-general/report-revisions-needed-national-contingency-plan-based-deepwater-horizon), dated **25 August 2011**, offers its full report and summary, then separate responses from EPA offices and comments by the inspector general. There are several voices in the documentary record.

That does **not** establish that the agency won its argument, that the watchdog changed its conclusion, or that a promised fix was carried out. We have verified the official document-family index, **not** independently read all attachments in this edition. To say what any particular response conceded, disputed, or remedied, open that attachment, cite its page, and compare it with the reviewer's next document.

This is the distinction: the report is a document; its documentary afterlife is a chain of separately attributable claims.

## Five questions

**1. Which document am I reading?** Record issuing body, title, report identifier, date, edition, direct URL, and page. A landing page and its linked full report are not the same object.

**2. What lies beneath the finding?** Ask for the supporting exhibit, measurement, interview, appendix, docket item, or cited page. The [NTSB TWA Flight 800 investigation](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA96MA070.aspx) separates the public docket from final report **AAR-00-03**. Keep those identifiers separate.

**3. Who answered, and in what capacity?** Label the investigated office's response, the investigator's comment, a regulator's later action, and any independent verification. None are interchangeable.

**4. Is the matter still being argued?** Follow responses to counter-responses. The [U.S. Chemical Safety Board's Texas City refinery case](https://www.csb.gov/bp-america-texas-city-refinery-explosion/) presents a final investigation report, management-of-change material, and safety bulletins. Different forms of document do different jobs.

**5. Is there dated evidence of completion?** A recommendation, an action plan, a progress update, a closure memo, and a verified physical outcome are five distinct things. Where a dated closure record has not been located, say **not verified**.

## A parallel problem: Which volume?

Not every documentary fork is a dispute. The [NIST Station nightclub fire study](https://www.nist.gov/disaster-failure-studies/station-nightclub-fire-2003) identifies **NCSTAR 2, Volume 1 and Volume 2**. A historian citing only "the NIST report" may obscure the source of a table or particular technical conclusion. Use the volume, section and page. Do not imply the volumes have been read in this editorial draft.

A second distinction concerns authority. [FederalRegister.gov](https://www.federalregister.gov/) is a superb search and discovery portal, but it warns that its searchable XML rendition is not itself the legally official edition. If the claim depends on controlling published wording, follow the official [GovInfo Federal Register collection](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/collection/fr) and record its issue date.

## Four seductive but unproved sentences

- “The agency fixed the problem.” An agency response promising action is not an independently verified completion record.
- “The investigation found it.” A report may distinguish physical measurements, witness statements, simulations, and investigators' inference. Preserve the distinction.
- “It is on a government website.” Hosting does not make every quoted submission an agency finding, establish its legal authority, or grant media reuse rights.
- “The later report superseded it.” Assert supersession only when the issuing authority explicitly says so. Otherwise record a sequence.
