## Research question and conclusion

Do not confuse (1) ice sighting, (2) sending a warning, (3) receipt/acknowledgment in a wireless room, (4) delivery to the navigating officer/captain and (5) navigation response. The **British formal inquiry report** says the Mesaba warning was received in Titanic's wireless room on April 14, 1912, but did not reach Captain Smith. Stanley Adams's testimony as reproduced by the Titanic Inquiry Project records transmission and acknowledgment; it does not establish bridge delivery. The original court document was **NOT inspected as an item-level scan**, so the page explicitly labels this qualification. The scan and any original message should be inspected before a public first publication.

Preserve source-specific time labels: Adams's quoted 7:50 p.m. **New York time** and the British report's 9:40 p.m. **Titanic time**. No undocumented conversion has been made. Avoid assigning a single common clock to these records.
