# The Empty Search Was Not an Empty Archive

**Final Vestige Studios — Library field guide — Editorial draft — November 13, 2026**

**Status:** source-controlled draft, not approved for production publication.

A search box seems to make a promise it cannot keep: that the absence of an answer is the absence of a record.

Try a name in a national archival catalog and get nothing. Does that mean no one filed the report? No one kept the letter? No one photographed the bridge? It may mean something much less conclusive: no one described that particular thing in the words you tried, at the level you searched, in an index that the public can interrogate.

## The denominators are not interchangeable

The National Archives Record Group Explorer reported **503,600,456 scans online** in **October 2026**, or **4.04%** of an *estimated* **12,473,570,637 textual pages**. The denominator is a modeled count calculated from cubic footage using **2,500 pages per cubic foot**, not a physical census of individual pages. The statistic must not be extended uncritically to photographs, films, maps or other media.

**Primary source:** [NARA Record Group Explorer](https://www.archives.gov/findingaid/record-group-explorer), checked 2026-11-13; this statistic is time-specific.

NARA's catalog guidance says **over 95% of holdings are described at the series level**, not necessarily the individual document level. A searchable series description is not a full-text index of everything in its boxes.

**Primary source:** [NARA — Using the National Archives Catalog](https://www.archives.gov/research/catalog/help/using), checked 2026-11-13.

## Six ways to pursue a negative result

1. **Capture the attempt.** Record the exact query, catalog, filters and time, and whether you searched descriptions, digitized items or full text.
2. **Separate the object from the description.** Search people, creating agencies, places, and contemporaneous terminology; the finding aid may use the name of an office rather than your modern subject description.
3. **Climb the hierarchy.** Follow Record Group → series → file unit → item. A parent level may be the only description available online.
4. **Check the coverage.** Distinguish undescribed, undigitized, restricted, misplaced, unpublished, and not returned by this query. A blank result is not an extinction certificate.
5. **Follow the institution's research routes.** Use online tools, finding aids, research-room services, and reference inquiries where justified.
6. **State the limit in the citation.** Write “No matching digitized item was found in [named catalog] for [recorded query] on [date],” not “No records exist.”

The companion offline HTML receipt tool records the repository, collection/series, date range, query, filters, outcome, alternate lead, rights status, and follow-up. It downloads a JSON receipt entirely in the reader's browser, without transmitting the search terms to an external endpoint.

## The rights trap

An archive may possess a photograph without owning the copyright. Federal custody is not a blanket license for gifts, contractor work, donated collections, or rights-controlled individual images. Read item-level notices before using scans or photographs.

**Primary source:** [NARA Still Pictures: Permissions](https://www.archives.gov/research/still-pictures/permissions), checked 2026-11-13.
