## Reader problem and non-duplication
Researchers confuse (a) asking an archivist to locate a specific item, (b) invoking federal FOIA for certain restricted existing executive-branch agency records, and (c) asking a record custodian or rights holder whether a specific image may lawfully be reused. This original guide makes each inquiry precise without claiming that one answer grants the other two. The published [Box Behind the Citation](https://finalvestige.com/free-resources/the-box-behind-the-citation/) teaches archival identification and finding-aid hierarchy, while this article focuses on **what request to send, to whom, and with which evidence**. The unpublished local *Archive Did Not Say No* focuses on access errors rather than composing requests. ## Rights, accuracy, and access limits
FOIA is for qualifying executive-branch agency records, not court records merely because NARA stores them; FOIA requests cannot demand new analysis. Reference staff are limited in the substantive research they undertake. Rights inquiries ask who controls the actual work and which item-specific conditions apply, rather than representing custody or a copy as licensing. Examples are explicitly **fictional** and not claims about real patrons. The image is an **original explanatory diagram**, not documentary artwork or an AI archival reconstruction.
