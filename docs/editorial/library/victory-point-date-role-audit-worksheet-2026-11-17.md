## 4. A real example: the Victory Point note

The Canadian Museum of History's published transcription of the surviving 1847–48 Franklin expedition note records:

- **28 May 1847:** first written message date.
- **22 April 1848:** ship desertion, as reported retrospectively in the later message.
- **25 April 1848:** heading of the marginal addition.
- **26 April 1848:** intended start of the march on the following day; not independent verification that the march began precisely then.

The Royal Museums Greenwich catalogue of the original object **HSR/C/9/1** instead describes the addition as made **26 April 1848**, and notes discovery in **April 1859**. The cause of the catalogue/transcription difference has not been established. A broad catalogue “Date made” field covering **1847–1859** should not be read as a literal day the note was written.

## 5. Before reporting a conclusion

Record your specific claim and the words in the source that support it. State the gap between what was **reported**, what was **intended**, what the document's **heading** dates, and what a later **catalogue** asserts. Document why a calendar conversion is justified before attempting one. Confirm source independence: a museum transcription and another article citing it do not create two independent historical witnesses.
