The Ninety Doors

An archive is full of doors. This desk helps you choose one, remember which door you opened, and distinguish the place that hosts a record from the record itself.

This is a portable working index of 90 existing Final Vestige source cards, not a claim that ninety underlying documents have been inspected. Filter by subject, build a research shortlist, and export a citation receipt without submitting your notes to a server.

Method

A link is the beginning of the citation.

  1. Choose a route. Search a name, event, technology, agency or place; use the family filter to narrow the questions.
  2. Open the institution. Determine whether you are looking at a collection overview, finding aid, government edition, individual record or scan.
  3. Preserve the identity. Record the document's creator, date, stable identifier, edition, page or frame—and the actual URL inspected.
  4. Account for duplicates and limits. A second publication of the same memo is not a second eyewitness. No digital scan does not mean the archive holds nothing.
  5. Check reuse rules. Source access, quotation, reproduction and privacy are different questions.

For worked examples, see The Archive Trail and Editorial Standards. This index preserves the originating catalogue's wording and historical claims rather than independently revalidating each card.

The source receipt

Before you quote a record, write down what you actually saw. The catalogue door and the original witness may have different names, custodians, editions and dates.

What exactly am I testing?

Which institution preserves or publishes the material?

What is its original identifier?

Who made it, and when?

Original, transcription, translation, edited volume, or summary?

Which version did I actually read?

Is this a second underlying witness or the same one republished?

What may I quote or reuse? What have I not seen?
