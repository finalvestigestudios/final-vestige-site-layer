THE LIBRARY / RESEARCH METHODS / DECLASSIFIED RECORDS

Free field guide · 12 official collection doors · Last checked 18 Nov 2026

An official archive can lead you to the evidence without actually being the evidence. Here is how to tell when you have reached the record—and when you are still standing in the doorway.

Declassification makes material available. It does not resolve every date, complete every missing page, undo every translation, or transform an agency overview into the original document. This source desk separates institutional gateways from individual records, so a citation means what it says.

The document behind the door

The distinction is simple until it matters: a CIA overview written in 2022 is not a 1972 intelligence product; an edited FRUS volume is not the complete archive; a translation of a decrypted message is a processed historical witness. All can be useful. Each answers a different question.

Evidence threshold. An official landing page that loads is an accessible catalogue door. It does not prove every linked PDF is online, complete, unrestricted, or accurately described.

Choose the right starting point

Use the agency's description to locate the file; use the file's own identifier, date, pagination and caveats to support the claim. If you have only inspected the landing page, say so.

Carry a six-field source receipt

Print this page and fill in the receipt by hand, or copy the prompts into your research notebook. Nothing is submitted, tracked or stored here.

1. The claim

Write one testable historical assertion—not a broad theme.

2. Institutional gateway

Agency, collection name, landing URL and access date.

3. Actual source

Record creator, original date, file identifier, document number and page—or clearly mark NOT ACCESSED.

4. What kind of witness?

Original, facsimile, transcript, edited official volume, translation, finding aid, or later summary.

5. What is missing or changed?

Redactions, disputed dates, translation, missing pages, editorial selection, time convention, custody.

6. Reuse and credit

Rights status of the specific item, permission if required, credit line and any privacy limits.

Three traps before you cite

Publication date is not the event date. An archived source can have a creation date, a declassification date, a digitization date and a later editorial update. Preserve each when it matters.

Official language can still reflect an agency's historical bias. Historical FBI classifications describe the filing system's worldview, not a factual label Final Vestige adopts.

Readable is not necessarily reproducible. Public records may contain living people's identifiers or third-party copyrighted images. An accessible PDF is not blanket permission to copy its contents.

Provenance, access and reuse

This guide is original FV explanatory writing and illustration. No third-party scan, archival photo, file facsimile, seal or video has been reproduced. The diagram is an abstract method illustration, not evidence from any historical incident.

  • NARA copyright and permissions — federal works are often public domain, but holdings can have third-party rights or restrictions.
  • Office of the Historian, FRUS FAQ — documentary editions are edited and selected; the published series is generally public domain.
  • CIA site policies — check notices and attribution for specific content.
  • Reagan Library Iran–Contra guide — some referenced records remain withdrawn or restricted.
