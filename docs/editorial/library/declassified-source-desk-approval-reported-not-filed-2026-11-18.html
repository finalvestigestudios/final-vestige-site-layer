The Declassified Source Desk · Worked case, part II

The Approval Was Reported, Not Filed

A surviving telephone note says an operation had been cleared with the President. That is evidence of the statement. The approval itself is a different record.

At 10:55 a.m. on July 24, 1953, Secretary of State John Foster Dulles spoke by telephone with his brother, CIA Director Allen Dulles. A memorandum in the Secretary's telephone files records Allen's answer about an operation involving Iran: it was cleared directly with the President. The note can establish that the claim entered the contemporary written record. It cannot establish the President's exact words, the date and form of authorization, or the existence of a signed order. Open the State Department's transcription of the 1953 memo.

A source can acquire false weight simply by being printed twice. In 1989, the State Department published the memorandum as Foreign Relations of the United States (FRUS) volume X, Document 335. In 2018, the second-edition Iran volume reproduced the same telephone account in an editorial note, Document 251. Those two URLs do not describe two independent conversations. They describe one underlying historical record, encountered in two documentary editions. The original paper in the Eisenhower Library's Dulles papers was not personally inspected for this guide.

The essential distinction: a contemporaneous memo reports Allen Dulles saying approval was obtained. A later editorial note cites an operational chronology recording July 11 authorization. Neither is a written presidential approval. The absence of a located order does not demonstrate that no authorization occurred.

Four different kinds of witness

1953 document · 1989 transcription

The telephone memorandum

Government transcription of John Foster Dulles's July 24 telephone note, originally filed in the Eisenhower Library's Dulles papers, Telephone Conversations. It includes an editor's caution that a meeting mentioned in the call is not further identified. It is not a direct record of the alleged June 25 meeting.

2018 editorial cross-reference

One memorandum, twice in print

Second-edition Document 251 quotes the 1953 account and sends readers back to 1989 Document 335, pages 737–738. A second publication improves access and citation; it cannot be counted as a separate participant or independent contemporary witness.

2018 editorial finding

The purported June 25 meeting

Second-edition Document 225 reports Kermit Roosevelt's later memoir claim and says the editors found no official meeting record. Its cited Waller–Roosevelt chronology gives July 11 for formal authorization. The underlying memoir and chronology have not been examined here.

Nearby records · different events

Same dates do not mean same gathering

Document 226 discusses a June 25 conversation between Ambassador Henderson and British Ambassador Makins; Document 232 describes a July 1 communication reporting British prime-ministerial approval. Neither is a minute of Roosevelt's claimed high-level June 25 meeting.

How to check a claim like this

  1. Read the earliest accessible item. Document 335 gives the memorandum's source, date, time, and editorial footnotes. Record that the access format is HTML transcription, not an inspected physical original.
  2. Identify the later copy. Document 251 is a cross-reference and republication of the conversation. Do not double-count it as corroboration.
  3. Do not turn reported speech into a signed order. The note shows what the CIA director was reported to have said on July 24. It does not show the President's signature or verbatim authorization.
  4. Keep the other timeline separate. Document 225 is an editorial account of Roosevelt's memoir, a cited signed operational chronology and unsuccessful searches for certain records. The original memoir and chronology remain to be retrieved.
  5. Watch for coincidence masquerading as evidence. Document 226 describes a different June 25 conversation; Document 232 describes July 1 British approval information. The dates are useful; the participants and record types are decisive.
  6. Write the smallest defensible conclusion. By July 24, a contemporary memorandum reports Allen Dulles's statement that approval had been obtained; the 2018 editors cite July 11 in a separate, uninspected chronology. Neither inspected publication supplies an actual presidential authorization instrument.
