# Caernarvon 1927 — reparation financing still litigated in 1955

**Research:** November 11, 2026 (Pacific). **Owner:** Investigations #1463; **publication QA:** #1589. **Status:** verified source analysis **staged only**; not inserted into WordPress #2151. The existing article revision must remain stable for production-preview QA.

## New finding

The Louisiana Supreme Court's **March 21, 1955** opinion in *Dantoni v. Board of Levee Commissioners of Orleans Levee District*, **227 La. 575, 80 So. 2d 81 (1955)**, expressly describes the Levee Board's proposed **1955 tax of 5½ mills**, including an asserted **½ mill for reparation claims arising from the Caernarvon artificial crevasse** of April 29, 1927. The opinion says the litigants **conceded the Board had authority** to levy that separate ½-mill component. This is a *28-year administrative/legal afterlife* of the flood, well beyond the 1937 and 1941 reparation-bond opinions already cited in the article.

**Crucial boundary:** The Dantoni judgment concerned the legality of a *general levee-tax limitation and pre-existing indebtedness*, not an individual reparation claim or an accounting of payments actually made in 1955. The record **does not prove** that every damaged family had been paid, that 1955 payments were actually disbursed, or that the general levee bonds described below were Caernarvon reparation bonds.

## What the ruling actually decides

Vincent Dantoni, a New Orleans taxpayer, challenged the board's 1955 **5½-mill** tax following a **1954 constitutional amendment** that reduced the levy authorized for **future maintenance/construction**, to **3½ mills** for 1955–1959 and **2 mills** thereafter. In a majority opinion, the Supreme Court reversed the lower court's injunction and held that reducing maintenance-tax authority did **not** extinguish protection of preexisting bond contracts. The opinion was accompanied by a dissent and an unusual per curiam refusing rehearing with further discussion of the board's finances.

### Beware a tempting numeric conflation

- The opinion's **$8,280,000** outstanding-bonds allegation and its identification of **$7,214,000** in outstanding bonds issued under **Article XVI, §2** concern *general levee-board financing* and 1934/1935 obligations. **DO NOT** call these Caernarvon reparation-bond balances.
- Likewise the **$501,000** of bonds issued in 1909 cannot derive from a 1927 flood.
- The case's half-mill entry identifies a claimed tax component in **1955**, **not** the amount distributed to flood victims, and not proof of any unpaid individual claim.
- The 1937 opinion [*Tonry*](https://law.justia.com/cases/louisiana/supreme-court/1937/186-la-159.html) concerned **$3,387,000** in Caernarvon reparation-bond refinancing under the separate **Article XVI-A** tax; the 1941 opinion [*Graham v. Jones*](https://law.justia.com/cases/louisiana/supreme-court/1941/198-la-507.html) described several million dollars in such bonds outstanding. **Those amounts must not be carried forward to 1955** without a direct bond register/accounting source.
- **Unresolved distinction:** Tonry's tax ceiling was **one mill**, whereas the Dantoni board's 1955 budget expressly reserved **½ mill** for Caernarvon. This could reflect an actual annual rate below the legal cap, but *we have not located annual levy orders or rolls to establish that*. Do not assert legislative reduction of the cap.

## Editorial significance

Caernarvon appears repeatedly in the state's decisions long after the dynamite: first in disputes over what counted as a payable loss, then in protection of financing instruments, and by 1955 as a distinct item in contested Orleans Parish taxation. That is evidence of the engineered flood's unusually durable fiscal and legal footprint, **not** evidence of comprehensive reparations.
