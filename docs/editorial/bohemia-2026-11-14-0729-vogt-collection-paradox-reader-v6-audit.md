## Primary-document finding
Inspected the [Louisiana Fourth Circuit March 27, 2002 Vogt opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/louisiana/fourth-circuit-court-of-appeal/2002/2001-ca-0089.html), 814 So. 2d 648, and **visually inspected original [18-page court-opinion PDF](https://cases.justia.com/louisiana/fourth-circuit-court-of-appeal/2002-2001-ca-0089.pdf?ts=1440795651), image pages 7, 8 and 16**. The October 26, 2000 seizure application sought **$1,831,528.49 plus claimed income and judicial interest**. The broader 1998 judgment recorded **$2,853,358.44**, but the court does not reconcile these amounts. Do not infer a payment, negotiated discount or remaining debt from the difference.

The opinion summarizes **CPA David D. DeMonte's testimony for the plaintiffs** at the writ hearing. He had reviewed 1999/2000 legislative audits and district financial records. He estimated approximately **$120 million net worth** as of June 30, 2000, and described **$57 million in investment securities including $9 million in a money-market account**, of which **$2.7 million was earmarked for Bohemia litigation**. The two smaller figures are parts of the $57m securities account, **not additional assets**. This is court-reported witness testimony, not our independent validation of account restrictions, ownership, currently available cash or any beneficiary disbursement. He also described public and proprietary funds as physically pooled but internally allocated by bookkeeping, not an independently proven diversion.

The state appellate court affirmed denial of mandamus and seizure in **March 2002** because public property/funds could not be seized and the court lacked authority to force an appropriation to satisfy that judgment. The proposed levy under article VI §42(A) did not apply to the post-1984 royalty claim, in that court's reading. The [June 14, 2002 Fifth Circuit Vogt opinion](https://openjurist.org/294/f3d/684/vogt-v-board-of-commissioners-of-the-orleans-levee-district) separately reversed a *preliminary federal dismissal*, allowed the pleaded takings theory and **remanded without deciding ultimate recovery**. These opinions address different procedural questions.
