## Reporting advance, evidence and method

Added ~**537 words** to manuscript v2.2, section **“The board that could not sue the state.”** The article now distinguishes the **1988 board-versus-state** federal compensation claim from the **2002 private-landowner-versus-district** Eleventh Amendment litigation. Earlier writeups describing a generic federal court “victory” would conceal which litigant won what.

**Primary judicial source A:** United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit, *Board of Levee Commissioners of the Orleans Levee Board v. Huls*, 852 F.2d 140 (Aug. 15, 1988), [full reproduced opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/852/140/451290/) / [parallel full text](https://www.lexfed.com/cases/509013/board-of-levee-commissioners-of-the-orleans-levee-board-v-huls). **Actual opinion text checked**: board's federal Takings Clause theory failed because the state-created entity could not assert that federal property right against Louisiana. The board's attempt to sever “private” mineral revenue interests from its public flood-control interests did not alter the holding. The bondholder's separate Contract Clause challenge was **moot** after maturity and no expected impairment of redemption; lower decision vacated and bondholder claim dismissed **without prejudice**. **Never call that a merits dismissal of the bondholder theory or a ruling that all claimants were paid.**

**Primary judicial source B:** Fifth Circuit, *Vogt v. Board of Commissioners of the Orleans Levee District*, 294 F.3d 684 (June 14, 2002), [full reproduced opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/294/684/545697/) (existing source [6]). The 2002 opinion **expressly distinguished 1988 Huls**: “creature/agency of state” for its claim **against Louisiana** does not automatically mean “arm of the state” immune from private owners' claims. The court reversed the lower immunity dismissal and held pleaded takings allegations sufficient; it did not order or verify collection of judgment.

**Controlling Louisiana source:** [Oct. 20, 1986 rehearing decision, 496 So. 2d 281](https://openjurist.org/496/so2d/281/bd-of-com-rs-of-orleans-levee-dist-v-dept-of-natural-resources) (existing [2]). Retrospective land-acquisition classifications: **3% expropriated, 48% purchased in lieu, 49% public land transfers**, totaling approximately 33,000 acres; these are rounded judicial categories and not an original cadastral survey. The court reports approximately **$5M annual mineral revenues at the time**, **60% (~$3M)** attributable to the disputed privately acquired lands. **60% of revenue is NOT 60% of acres**, and figures are not current 2026 receipts or private-heir settlement obligations. Earlier original-hearing footnote [3], 483 So. 2d 958, groups categories as ~48% purchases and ~52% state transfers with <3% expropriated parenthetically. The 1986 **rehearing** is the later disposition; do not silently harmonize historical estimates.

**Unresolved evidence:** 1925 acquisition roster/committee minutes, enacted 1984/1985/1992 session-law originals, parcel-specific deeds, royalty revenue audits, claimant payment vouchers, some USACE PDFs, NASA images, and responsive WordPress proof.
