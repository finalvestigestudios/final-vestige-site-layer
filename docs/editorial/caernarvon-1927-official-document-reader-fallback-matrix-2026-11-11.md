## A useful and precise reader-facing distinction

Two claims sometimes appear to conflict because the reports answer different questions: **1,712,000 cubic feet/second** is a historically *reported measurement* near Chicot on **April 20, 1927**, while **2,472,000 cubic feet/second** is an estimated *counterfactual peak if the river had remained between levees* near Arkansas City during the same disaster. These are neither simultaneous, equivalent nor independently remeasured by Final Vestige. [Smith & Baeck 2015 publisher HTML](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/2015WR017927), referring to [Costa & Jarrett 2008](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20085164), makes the distinction explicit. The existing draft already uses carefully qualified wording; no claim revision is warranted from the current data alone.

USACE's own operational history is useful for the Bonnet Carré chronology, but claims that the **1927 Caernarvon blast saved the city** require comparison against later upstream breaches and the limits of recorded river stages. An official agency's prose does not by itself solve the counterfactual. The article already retains this disagreement rather than choosing a more dramatic neat ending.
