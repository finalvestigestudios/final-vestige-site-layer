## Primary archival object identified

**Repository:** Louisiana State Museums, Map Collection. **Object:** *Map of the Alluvial Valley of the Mississippi River*, approximately **1922**, mapmaker **J. P. Kemper**. Accession **T0055.1997.0232**. Original catalog record: https://louisianastatemuseum.org/map-collection/map-alluvial-valley-mississippi-river-0

The catalog's long subtitle describes a **proposed** scheme to lower river levels using spillways into **Lake Maurepas** and **Breton Sound**, an **outlet into Vermilion Bay**, increased passage through the **Atchafalaya River** near Morgan City, and a bypass through the Tensas Basin. The museum transcribes a map notation identifying the proposal's auspices as the **National Flood Commission** and **Atchafalaya Basin Protective Association**. The date is approximate in the catalog (**'1922 c.'**), not a precisely verified day or year of composition.

### What the record can and cannot establish

This is concrete pre-1927 documentary evidence that some Mississippi Valley advocates were proposing multiple deliberate relief outlets **before** the April 29, 1927 Caernarvon demolition. It supports a narrower, more interesting question for future investigation: what floodway alternatives were politically and technically discussable before an emergency made one floodway an involuntary sacrifice?

**It does not establish** that government authorities adopted the proposal, that the proposed Breton Sound outlet was the Caernarvon blast site, that the map caused the 1927 decision, or that a particular alternative would have protected New Orleans in 1927. It is a proposal plan in a museum catalog, not an engineering approval record or a tested hydraulic reconstruction. The image itself is not visibly digitized in the current text catalog (default-image placeholder); we have **not** inspected its cartography, inscriptions or scale from original pixels.

## Historical-source chronology check

The University of Iowa Libraries' finding aid for the **Arthur C. Trowbridge Papers (RG 99.0089)** records that Trowbridge worked in **1922** with the U.S. Engineer Corps to study sediment deposition on the Mississippi Delta. It lists 1921 Texas/New Orleans field notebooks and July/August 1922 field-notes materials but **does not reproduce the original 1922 source text**: https://www.lib.uiowa.edu/scua/archives/guides/rg99.0089.html

This helps establish Trowbridge's relevant pre-1927 research context but **does not validate or assign a post-1927 eyewitness role** to his 1922 quotation, subsequently reproduced by Donald W. Davis in the 1994 USGS report. Davis's page describing the 1927 Caernarvon breach and citing Trowbridge 1922 is in *Processes of Coastal Wetlands Loss in Louisiana*, USGS Open-File Report 94-275, paper **'Crevasses on the Lower Course of the Mississippi River'**, printed **p. 367**. Stable official catalog https://www.usgs.gov/publications/processes-coastal-wetlands-loss-louisiana-results-a-multi-year-collaborative-study-us ; indexed report PDF https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1994/0275/report.pdf . The PDF text was readable to the research browser, but a real PDF page screenshot was blocked by the upstream access restrictions; **no image-level inspection PASS** was established. Prior historical-source caution remains valid.
