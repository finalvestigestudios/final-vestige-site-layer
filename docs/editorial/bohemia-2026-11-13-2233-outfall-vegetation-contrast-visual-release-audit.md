## Editorial discovery and exact reporting scope
The 2023 State of the Coast conference abstract authored/presented by **Michael Hopkins (Pontchartrain Conservancy)** reports preliminary classifications of rooted subaerial vegetation in the **Mardi Gras Pass outfall**. Historical USDA NAIP imagery was classified at **2008, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2021**. Method named by presenter: NDVI, ENVI categorization, manual ArcGIS inspection distinguishing open water, mudflat, floating and submerged vegetation, and rooted vegetation. Abstract reports **33.1 km² (2008)** and **30.0 km² (2021)**, a **3.1 km² loss using the stated rounded endpoints**. Earlier three intervals (**2008–2013, 2013–2015, 2015–2017**) lost **about 1 km² each**; later **2017–2021** decline was **about 0.3 km²**. Authors' qualified interpretation: pass-associated emergent wetlands were starting to counterbalance other losses. The last interval is nonetheless **net negative** for the specific category; no post-2021 outcome established.

**Direct source:** official organizer PDF, State of the Coast 2023, *Sessions and Abstracts*, Session 11, original page image **zero-based page 19**: https://www.stateofthecoast.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Sessions-and-Abstracts-Individual-1.pdf . **Accuracy gate:** Never describe total 2008–2021 vegetation loss as *caused by* the pass, since 2008 predates its 2012 formation and a causal counterfactual was not performed. Do not claim net gain, marsh restoration success, or 2026 conditions. Values are preliminary conference-abstract reporting (not a peer-reviewed full dataset or reproducible imagery/error analysis). Search for original GIS classifications, study polygon, uncertainty assessment and a consistent 2022–2026 follow-up.
