# Poydras 1922: the flood before the engineered Caernarvon breach
**FV Investigations archival/visual source card — 2026-11-11, 18:34–19:00 Pacific.**
**Status:** STAGED research; **not added** to WordPress investigation #2151; **not publication or QA approval**.

## Why this belongs in the Caernarvon reporting
The April 29, 1927 deliberate levee blast happened near another deeply consequential levee failure, the **1922 Poydras crevasse**. The current FV draft *already mentions* the 1922 “Blue Hole” and links a Louisiana history trail, so this is **corroboration and a measured historical nuance**, not a new topic or reason to start another article. A U.S. Army/Mississippi River Commission retrospective and separately cataloged 1922 photographs establish that the earlier flooding was documented as an event in its own right. The two floods **must never be conflated in photographs or captions**.

## Verified historical source and limits
**Marion Bragg, *Historic Names and Places on the Lower Mississippi River* (Mississippi River Commission / U.S. Army Engineers, 1977), printed pp. 253–254.**
- Corps digital-library catalog: https://usace.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p16021coll4/id/261/
- Full Corps book archival scan deposited on Commons (also provides searchable text): https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Historic_names_and_places_on_the_lower_Mississippi_River_-_USACE-p16021coll4-261.pdf
- **Printed p. 253, “Poydras Crevasse”:** the author places Poydras at river mile **82.1 AHP**, calls the 1922 event a **levee collapse**, and reports that New Orleans river stage fell **0.2 foot over the ensuing 24 hours**. He writes that the water covered farms and citrus groves on the east bank, that a `$1` donation was collected from motorists who went to see the breach, and that steamer *Capitol* aided refugees. The **1977 text** was read through the indexed PDF extraction; an attempt to take a screenshot of the 20-MB-plus PDF failed because the rendering service rejected its size. Thus no claim is made that scanned page pixels or the underlying 1922 gauge sheets were inspected.
- **Printed pp. 253–254, “Caernarvon Crevasse”:** the same book lists the *1927* artificial breach at river mile **81.3 AHP**, nearby but distinct. **Important conflict:** Bragg's “April 25” first-charge date for 1927 is inconsistent with the governor's April 26 proclamation directing an April 29 demolition and same-day AP reportage. Do not allow the value of this source for 1922 to silently launder its disputed 1927 date.
- Editorial limitation: **0.2 foot is the historical author's stated *observed stage change*, not a verified modeled benefit solely caused by the breach**. The original 1922 daily gauge series, rain, upstream flows and other controls were not examined. It cannot be used as a numerical estimate of what the 1927 blast achieved or whether the 1927 act was necessary.
- Bragg says the earlier levee **collapsed**, not that officials blew it open. Claims that the 1922 event was deliberately engineered are **unproven** by this source. Use “failure” or “collapse” for 1922 and “deliberate opening” for 1927.
- Separate local historical cross-check: https://1927flood.lthp.org/story/caernarvon-crevasse/ places the prehistory at the 1922 Poydras failure/“Blue Hole.” A community perspective: https://64parishes.org/treading-water references the same earlier flood. Neither upgrades the 1977 reported stage change to independently rechecked 1922 hydrological data.

## Original analysis, ready for a **future** controlled revision (not yet inserted)
> Five years before officials chose where to blast the levee, the river had already broken through nearby. A 1922 failure at Poydras flooded small farms and groves below New Orleans. In a 1977 river history, the Mississippi River Commission reported that the New Orleans gauge fell two-tenths of a foot during the next day. That is a recorded decline in a retrospective account, not proof of what the river would have done had the levee held. It made the geography familiar: an opening downstream could send water out of the main channel, and the people who lived behind that opening would bear the result.

If used, link the **1977 Corps source**, preserve the existing 1922 link, retain the 1927 date conflict, and do **not** replace the article's stronger analysis of what the 1927 gauge series does *not* prove.
