## Original-document discovery

The **enrolled Louisiana Act 130 of 2005**, signed **June 22, 2005**, shows three separate rules crossed out on **printed page 4/PDF index 3**. Both the original scanned page and the state Legislature's explanatory digest, printed p. 2, were independently examined on November 16, 2026.

1. **Structured-settlement long-stop date:** prior law, for a specifically described committee-approved structured-settlement mechanism, prescribed full revenue-and-interest payment by **July 1, 2009**. Act 130 removed that provision. Do not generalize this into an extinguishment of the 2000 consent judgment or proof that payment did not occur.
2. **Annual legislative status reports:** a former March 1 report to House/Senate natural-resources committees specified information on returns of property and revenues/interest paid. Act 130 removed this recurring statutory reporting clause; the record does not prove whether such reports were historically filed, or whether other audits and duties existed.
3. **Disputed-revenue escrow:** a general requirement that income from disputed property be escrowed pending final judgment was removed. This does not establish that a particular bank account was depleted, nor does it supersede an unexamined court escrow order.
4. **1998 Exhibit A:** a separate instruction to pay specified judicially expropriated owners identified in a **December 8, 1998** filing was removed. That Exhibit A **is not to be conflated** with the different claimant-balance Exhibit A discussed by the 2007 federal court. The underlying exhibits were not located in this cycle.

**Appellate/legal limitation:** [Fifth Circuit, *Haspel & Davis* (July 23, 2007)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca5/07-30086/07-30086-cv0.wpd-2011-02-25.html), prior bibliography **#15**. The 2000 consent judgment and settlement have to be analyzed independently; a law's deleted repayment timetable cannot alone establish their discharge or satisfaction. The February 2007 federal district-court order (#64) is not a surviving final constitutional monetary award after the Fifth Circuit reversal.
