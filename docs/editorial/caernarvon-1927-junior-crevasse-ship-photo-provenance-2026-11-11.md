# Caernarvon 1927: the separate Junior crevasse ship photograph — source, rights and causation ledger

**FV Investigations Publishing Studio | 2026-11-11, evening Pacific | Source and image candidate, NOT publication clearance**

## Editorial use and distinction

This is **not** a photograph of the deliberate April 29, 1927 Caernarvon explosion. It is a different opening on the Mississippi, commonly cataloged as the **Junior, Louisiana** crevasse, associated with the disputed **April 23** tanker incident **about 40 miles below New Orleans**. The image shows a large ship adjacent to a levee breach, with a rough channel of water. It does **not**, by itself, show a collision occurring or establish that the ship caused the breach.

The existing WordPress **draft #2151** already properly warns that the contemporaneous report and later Army Corps history disagree over the supposed collision. **Do not override that careful passage with a categorical photo caption** merely because a NOAA-derived caption describes the ship as the cause.

## Important contemporaneous conflict

An Associated Press New Orleans dispatch dated **April 23**, printed the next day in [*The Brownsville Herald*, April 24, p. 1](https://texashistory.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metapth1379708/m1/1/), says the oil tanker **Inspector** was *supposed* to have rammed the west-bank levee near **Junius Plantation** roughly forty miles downriver. The same report records residents who said the ship was passing at the moment of the break, sounded whistle/siren and then came to the bank to help. Its OCR is imperfect, and the original scan governs disputed spelling and wording. The dispatch further describes the vessel as **outbound for Cuba**. It establishes that a collision allegation was disputed **at the time**, not just by later scholarship.

Leland R. Johnson, [*Situation Desperate: U.S. Army Engineer Disaster Relief Operations, Origins to 1950* (USACE, 2011), printed pp. 151–153](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D103-PURL-gpo27158/pdf/GOVPUB-D103-PURL-gpo27158.pdf), asserts that a tanker collided on April 23; its reproduced image is credited to **National Archives 27-G-1A-4**. A separate instance in the same history describes the Mississippi River Commission meeting **aboard an *Inspector*** on April 25. It is **not established** from the sources inspected here whether these two vessels shared a name or refer to the same hull. Given the AP description of an oil tanker outbound for Cuba and the Army's MRC vessel, **do not conflate them** without ownership/registration/itinerary records.

The NOAA/Frankenfeld-linked Commons caption also states that an out-of-control steamship caused the breach. A period caption carries evidentiary weight as contemporary interpretation, but it is **not a direct, independent eyewitness test of causation**, and cannot erase the disagreement printed in the AP dispatch.

The **Junior/Junius** geographical name variants are also not conclusively reconciled to a surveyed coordinate here. The [Louisiana State Museums catalog for *Where the Levees Broke and Why*](https://louisianastatemuseum.org/map-collection/where-levees-broke-and-why) describes a distinct “Junior Break” and “Poydras Cut”; its actual map pixels and reproduction rights remain unverified.
