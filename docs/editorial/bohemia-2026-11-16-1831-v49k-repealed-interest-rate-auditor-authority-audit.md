## New verified connection — the deleted rate-calculation mechanism

1. **Before the amendment:** The text printed as *deleted former law* in Louisiana's [enrolled SB210 / Act 130 of 2005](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/ViewDocument.aspx?d=317802), **PDF p.3, lines 9–19**, required the board to return qualifying mineral receipts and pay interest based on the annual average rate it earned on income-producing investments **as determined by the Legislative Auditor**. **Original page screenshot inspected 2026-11-16.** Critical: this is a *struck-through provision*, not current newly enacted text.
2. **June 22, 2005:** The [official Louisiana Legislature history](https://www.legis.la.gov/Legis/BillInfo.aspx?i=61456) confirms SB210 became **Act 130** effective on governor's signature, **June 22, 2005**. Page 3 strikes that old rate language; page 4, lines 1–3 contains new bold-underlined recognition that the *Haspel & Davis* class judgment had seen **partial payments**, with further payments intended. The page 4 image was inspected directly; do not confuse removed former subsections on the same page with enacted legal duties. Elsewhere the act sets a 2007 certification deadline and directions for negotiating after conveyance. A statement in legislation acknowledging partial payments is not a beneficiary bank-reconciliation.
3. **February 28, 2007:** [Eastern District of Louisiana, *Haspel & Davis*, civil 06-3829, Document 154](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-laed-2_06-cv-03829/pdf/USCOURTS-laed-2_06-cv-03829-1.pdf), **PDF p.3 / zero-index 2, lines describing the auditor and Bourgeois Bennett**, reports that the **Legislative Auditor declined legal authority** to calculate the 2000 consent judgment interest *based on Act 130*, and that the plaintiffs' alternative calculation came from **Bourgeois Bennett**, which the order calls a **state-court-approved disbursing agent**. **Original-page screenshot directly inspected 2026-11-16.** This is the judge reporting litigants' position and an accounting impasse, not independent verification of the auditor's original written reasoning or final judicial settlement of the issue.
4. **July 23, 2007:** [Fifth Circuit, No. 07-30086, PDF pp.9–11](https://cases.justia.com/federal/appellate-courts/ca5/07-30086/920070723/0.pdf), concludes that the federal constitutional takings claim had been compromised by the settlement; it **reversed** the federal money judgment and left the claimants to seek enforcement of their **state-law settlement and consent judgment**. **Original-page screenshot of the holding (PDF zero-index 10) directly inspected 2026-11-16.** Do NOT present the earlier federal interest-calculation exercise (including a 5.24% proposed federal rate) as an enforceable final debt. No conclusion is offered on what a state court ultimately ordered.

**Interpretive boundary:** We can say the statute *removed a named rate-setting provision*, and that the judge *reported a later refusal citing the law*. We cannot say the amendment conclusively caused nonpayment, lawfully stripped the court's jurisdiction, invalidated the 2000 consent judgment, extinguished every state-law debt, or established Bourgeois Bennett's calculations as accepted final payment amounts. The former formula's legal relationship to a separately approved consent judgment is precisely what the unpublished source trail still needs to resolve.
