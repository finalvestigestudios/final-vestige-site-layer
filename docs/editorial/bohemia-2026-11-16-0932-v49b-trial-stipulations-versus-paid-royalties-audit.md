## Newly sharpened reporting: where the exact Tract 78 award came from
Primary adjudicative account reviewed as accessible court-opinion text: [Louisiana Court of Appeal, Fourth Circuit, *Succession of Ursin (Eusan) v. Board of Levee Commissioners*, No. 2011-CA-1105, decided September 26, 2012](https://law.justia.com/cases/louisiana/fourth-circuit-court-of-appeal/2012/2011-ca-1105.html). The appellate opinion expressly says the parties presented **two joint stipulations in place of live testimony or documentary evidence at trial**. It treats the stipulated figures as binding legal admissions: **$996,596.40** mineral-related revenues for **June 29, 1984–August 8, 2005**, district average investment interest rates for **1991–2006**, and the agreement that **$366,957.12** statutory interest would follow **if legally authorized**. The court upheld that award after finding a vested interest right; it separately affirmed **legal interest from judicial demand** without assigning that component a fixed dollar amount in the recited figures, and reversed the additional **$365,777.34** civil-fruits-interest component.

The distinction changes editorial language: a judicially enforceable stipulation is **not** the same as FV possessing the underlying year-by-year production statements, the signed stipulation exhibits, postjudgment satisfaction documents, or a cleared disbursement to named heirs. Nor is a stipulation unreliable merely because it is not an independent audit. The legally adjudicated sums are defensible; the actual source-ledger composition and final beneficiary payment trail remain **unverified**. Do not describe the three trial figures as one final affirmed balance; do not describe the retained statutory-interest figure as an FV-calculated return; do not calculate a 2026 debt from this opinion.
