## Source-locked framing, not clickbait

**What happened:** On April 29, 1927, authorities deliberately detonated charges in the Caernarvon/Poydras levee downstream from New Orleans. The same-day Associated Press dispatch reports a first explosion around **2:17 p.m.**, with initially shallow trenches and slow water entry; later work widened the breach. Governor Oramel Simpson's April 26 proclamation ordered the planned breach for noon April 29 and publicly promised that resulting property damage would be paid. The subsequent legal and accounting record narrowed or complicated those promises. Government engineers disputed and retrospectively estimated the flood-stage relief, but the gauge data do **not** reveal a directly observed alternate history without the cut. This uncertainty is the actual investigative question.

**Editorially prohibited shortcuts:** Do not claim the blast conclusively “saved New Orleans,” did nothing, was ordered by Herbert Hoover, or that 1927 flood images with unspecified locations prove the site of Caernarvon. Hoover was Secretary of Commerce; Coolidge was president. The 1927 *Eugene Guard* page includes a prominent **New Madrid** levee photograph, **not** a Caernarvon photograph. Do not present the later **modern** Caernarvon Freshwater Diversion diagram as the April 1927 breach. Do not equate the NWS roughly half-foot estimate with the 1927 Army board's roughly two-foot stage inference: the baselines/methods differ. Do not portray corporate wildlife-damage receipts as household compensation; the $1,096,796.53 Acme receipt is from a **specific $1.5 million earmark**, with a $200 typographical inconsistency in one part of the source decision, not the full parish-reparations ledger. No manufactured interviews, fake “archival footage,” AI facsimiles masquerading as original photographs, or unsourced blast sounds represented as recordings of 1927.
