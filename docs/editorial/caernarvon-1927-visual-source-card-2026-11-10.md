**What it illustrates:** A sequence of reported, source-backed dates: >14 inches rain April 14–15 (city rainwater flooding); 21.0-ft Carrollton stage April 25; Governor O. H. Simpson's April 26 proclamation naming April 29 noon for the cut; the April 29 initial blast at roughly 2:17 p.m. reported by the *Eugene Guard*; 20.7-ft New Orleans stage on May 15 in the NWS retrospective. Its distinct lower panel contrasts a later NWS ~0.5-ft reduction estimate with the Army board's circa-2-ft gauge-relationship inference at a different reference time. **They are not a controlled before/after measurement of Caernarvon relief and must not be plotted as directly comparable gauge readings.**

**Ready-to-use alt text:** “Evidence timeline for the 1927 Caernarvon levee breach: April 14–15 New Orleans rainstorm; April 25 river stage 21 feet; April 26 governor's order; April 29 initial dynamite blast; May 15 NWS river stage 20.7 feet. Different retrospective estimates of breach-related stage reduction, about half a foot and two feet, do not reveal the unobserved no-breach river level.”

**Suggested caption:** “Two floods, one deliberate breach. The gauge dates and the governor's order are recorded, while the river level New Orleans would have reached without the artificial cut cannot be observed directly. Estimates differ in reference time and method. Original Final Vestige evidence graphic (2026); sources below.”

**Source ledger for this figure:**
1. [National Weather Service, New Orleans, “Top Ten Events of the 20th Century”](https://www.weather.gov/lix/20thcenturytopten): April 14–15 >14 inches in 19 hours; April 25 stage 21.0 ft; April 29 breach; May 15 20.7-ft stage; retrospective reduction ~0.5 ft. An agency retrospective, not a 1927 daily observation log.
2. [*Foret v. Board of Levee Commissioners*, 169 La. 427 (La. Dec. 2, 1929)](https://law.justia.com/cases/louisiana/supreme-court/1929/169-la-427.html): judicial reproduction of the governor's April 26 proclamation scheduling Poydras opening for noon April 29. The ordered time is not an independently observed blast time.
3. [*The Eugene Guard*, April 29, 1927, Associated Press front-page dispatch, University of Oregon newspaper archive](https://oregonnews.uoregon.edu/lccn/sn96088108/1927-04-29/ed-1/seq-1/ocr/): period report of the ~2:17 p.m. blast and shallow initial cuts. Newspaper reporting, not an inspected engineering shot log. The nearby newspaper photograph shows New Madrid, **not** this explosion.
4. [U.S. Army special engineering board, *Spillways on the Lower Mississippi River*, H. Doc. 70-95 (board report Nov. 12; printed Dec. 10, 1927), appendix B, pp. 26–28](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/SERIALSET-08897_00_00-026-0095-0000): inference of about two feet relief at Carrollton at the specified May 15 comparison, derived from relationships among gauges. The public GovInfo catalog was read; no claim of a newly verified page-image scan this cycle. Exact printed-page estimate previously examined in the main release audit.

**Graphical safeguards:** No scaled line graph connecting different gauge instruments; no inferred levee-free hydrograph; no geographical map claims; no presentation of retrospective stage-reduction estimates as direct readings. ## Newly verified 1927 government-map leads (research only)
- [Mississippi Department of Archives and History, “Mississippi River Flood of 1927: showing flooded areas and field of operations”](https://da.mdah.ms.gov/series/maps/detail/191551): catalog identifies the **U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey** as creator and publisher; dated 1927; call no. MA/2002.0092(os). Regional map covers relief camps, floods and levee breaks. The [Louisiana State Museums catalog](https://louisianastatemuseum.org/map-collection/mississippi-river-flood-1927) independently attributes the work to the same federal survey, accession 10786. [Wikimedia Commons file record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1927_LA_Flood_Map.jpg) declares U.S. federal public-domain provenance (National Archives RG 23) and hosts a 2,292×5,214 scan. **Candidate only:** this is a regional overview, not a precise photograph of the blast; high-resolution map annotations have not been independently read for any Caernarvon figure. No image copied to the site.
- [Louisiana State Museums, “Where the Levees Broke and Why”](https://louisianastatemuseum.org/map-collection/where-levees-broke-and-why), c. 1927, accession T0055.1997.0185: catalog lists **Poydras Cut and Junior Break** among named breaches, with possible mapmaker J. P. Kemper. Attribution is explicitly tentative, manuscript rights are not established, and visible full-resolution map content has not been verified. **Do not reproduce without rights clearance.**
- The public-domain [LOC April 30 Caernarvon aerial photograph](https://www.loc.gov/item/2002699300/) and [LOC Poydras crevasse photograph](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2002709263/) remain better site-specific documentary anchors than unlocated flood photos. The current four WP media attachments (#2148, #2255, #2202, #2149) already provide archival coverage.
