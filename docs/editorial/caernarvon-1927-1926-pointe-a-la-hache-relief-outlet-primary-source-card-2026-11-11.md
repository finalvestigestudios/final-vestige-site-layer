# Caernarvon 1927 — the earlier engineered outlet, Pointe à la Hache (source card)
**Research date:** 2026-11-11, Pacific. **Status:** staged research; not inserted into WordPress #2151 or released. **Owner:** Investigations #1463. **Related release gate:** Site QA #1589.

## The useful finding
The Caernarvon demolition of April 29, 1927 was **not the first deliberate flood-relief provision south of New Orleans**. The original War Department engineering-board report, delivered to Congress in December 1927, identifies a pre-existing **Pointe à la Hache relief outlet**, completed in **1926** by removal of **approximately eleven miles of east-bank levee**, about **65 river miles below the Carrollton gauge**. The board wrote that city authorities requested an additional cut near Poydras in 1927 because the older outlet was not expected to prevent dangerously high stages. This is especially important for any framing that treats 1927 as the instant invention of *all* flood diversion: the change was more complicated, from limited relief openings toward an integrated flood-control system.

## Close-to-event primary document
**U.S. War Department,** *Spillways on the Lower Mississippi River*, H.R. Doc. 70-95, 70th Congress, first session; report of special board dated **November 12, 1927**, transmitted by Secretary of War **December 5**, referred and ordered printed **December 10, 1927**. [Official GovInfo catalog](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/SERIALSET-08897_00_00-026-0095-0000); [GovInfo digitized source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/SERIALSET-08897_00_00-026-0095-0000/pdf/SERIALSET-08897_00_00-026-0095-0000.pdf).

- **Printed p. 8, paragraph 23:** the port authorities had created the prior outlet in 1926 by removing more than a ten-mile stretch of levee. The board's text says the 1927 additional opening was cut after planners judged the first outlet's relief insufficient.
- **Appendix B, printed p. 26, paragraphs 1–3:** the outlet's construction followed the 1922 high water at Carrollton. The board measures the earlier removed levee at **11 miles**, places it about **65 miles downstream of Carrollton**, and records the April 29, 1927 first Caernarvon cut. It describes deliberate widening through **May 9**, when the gap was **approximately 2,800 feet**. These are the board's near-contemporary reported measurements, not our independent survey.
- **Appendix B, printed pp. 27–28, paragraphs 6–8:** the board estimated that the earlier Pointe à la Hache outlet carried **about 200,000 cubic feet per second by April 15**, 1927, yet concluded gauge comparisons cannot isolate its exact effect by simple before/after comparison. It attributed roughly **one-half to three-quarters of a foot** of Carrollton-stage reduction at the 21-foot reading to the pre-existing outlet. For Caernarvon, it reported about **100,000 cfs on May 2** and, for May 15 conditions, inferred **about 2 ft of Carrollton stage effect** and roughly **250,000 cfs** through the cut.
- **Documentary distinction:** These are the historical board's flow calculations and *inferred stage changes*, not independently verified direct flow-meter measurements or observed counterfactual river stages. The board's curves and assumptions should be checked against the underlying sheet 6 map and gauge records before technical claims are newly published.

**Candidate restrained insertion, only after authorized re-edit:** “There was already an engineered escape route below New Orleans.
