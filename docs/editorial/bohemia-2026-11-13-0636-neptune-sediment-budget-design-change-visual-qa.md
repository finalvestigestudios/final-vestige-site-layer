## New substantive primary scholarly evidence: Neptune sediment budget

**Original source:** Kolker, Weathers, Swann and Renfro, “Distributary development in a 21st century river,” *PLOS ONE* 20(4):e0320502 (**April 16, 2025**), publisher full text https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0320502 , DOI https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0320502 . Directly read peer-reviewed **HTML**, including sections 2.7–2.8, 3.6–3.7, Table 3 and disclosed funding. The authors collected **12 sediment cores June 12–13, 2023**, estimated **6.1 × 10^6 cubic meters scoured** from Neptune Pass and **1.1 × 10^7 cubic meters deposited** in the bay, yielding a **~4.8 × 10^6 cubic meter net sediment** estimate. Their inferred deposited mass was **56%–79% greater than what channel scour could explain**, under two porosity scenarios. This is a mass-comparison range; it is **not** the proportion of all sediments chemically traced to the river. [Source ledger #37]

**Methods safeguards:** 2016 and 2022 channel survey comparison; 2011 and 2022 Quarantine Bay comparison; historic channel interpolation; **assumed 22 mm per year subsidence** for the bay baseline; differences in sediment porosity; sonar/remote sensing plus sediment cores. Directly examined publisher HTML rather than relying on press paraphrases. The finding is net deposition over the study interval, **not** measured acreage of stable vegetated marsh, 2026 discharge or new completed flood-protection capacity. Original publication discloses funders CPRA and National Wildlife Federation; those disclosures are retained. The article is CC BY 4.0, but no publisher figure was reused.
