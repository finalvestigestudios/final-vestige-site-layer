Act III — The Reconstruction

A Final Vestige Investigation

Here's what finding the trace actually looks like.

01 — THE CLAIM "An electrical short ignited Apollo 1."

Modern summaries often reduce the 1967 launch-pad fire to a single, tidy cause.

02 — THE RECORD 100% oxygen, 16.7 psia.

The Apollo 204 Review Board's final report puts the ground-test cabin atmosphere in writing: a much denser oxygen environment than Apollo would ever use in flight.

03 — THE CONTRADICTION NASA never found the spark.

Investigators found evidence of arcing near the environmental-control equipment — the "most probable" initiator. The specific cable was destroyed. No single ignition source was conclusively identified.

04 — THE PHYSICAL TRACE The hatch opened inward.

NASA now displays the original three-part hatch beside the redesigned one that replaced it — the clearest physical trace of what the Board's findings actually changed.

05 — THE RECONSTRUCTION Fourteen seconds.

First fire report to pressure-vessel rupture: roughly fourteen seconds, reconstructed from the surviving instrument record — more useful than any account that makes it cinematic.

06 — WHAT WE CAN ACTUALLY SAY The cause wasn't one wire.

The Review Board didn't need to prove which arc started the fire to prove why a minor electrical failure became fatal: wiring deficiencies, combustible material, a hazardous test atmosphere, and no way out in time.

Gus Grissom, Ed White and Roger Chaffee near Launch Complex 34, January 1967.
